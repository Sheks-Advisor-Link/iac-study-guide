Audit Strategy — Approach, Nature, Timing & Extent

How to answer an "audit approach / N-T-E" question · ISA 300 & ISA 330 · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden
The whole idea in one line: the audit is a response to risk. You assess the Risk of Material Misstatement (RoMM), then design procedures whose Nature, Timing & Extent reduce detection risk to an acceptable level. Higher risk → more & better evidence, closer to year-end, on bigger samples. Everything flows from the audit-risk model: AR = IR × CR × DR — only DR is yours to control.

1 · What the question is really asking

"Describe the audit approach / strategy" or "discuss the nature, timing & extent of procedures" wants two things:

  • The overall approach — will you rely on the client's controls (a combined approach) or go straight to substantive procedures (a substantive approach)?
  • The N-T-E of your procedures — Nature (what kind & how reliable), Timing (interim vs year-end), Extent (how much / sample size) — all justified by the risk.

2 · The audit approach — controls (combined) vs substantive

Combined approach (tests of controls + reduced substantive)Substantive approach (little/no control reliance)
Use when controls are well-designed AND operating effectively, and reliance is efficient (high-volume, automated, routine transactions where substantive testing alone is impractical — e.g. revenue in a large retailer).Use when controls are weak / absent / not worth testing (owner-managed, manual systems, deficiencies, override risk), or the population is small enough to test directly, or for significant risks (always need a substantive response).
Test controls → if effective, you may reduce (not eliminate) substantive work.Don't test controls; obtain all assurance substantively.
Two rules you must state① Substantive procedures are always required for each material class/balance — even with a combined approach you can never rely on controls alone. ② Every significant risk needs a substantive response (and you can't rely on analytical procedures alone for it).

3 · Nature · Timing · Extent — what each means & the "risk direction"

ElementWhat it meansIf RoMM is HIGH →
NATUREThe type & purpose of the procedure: tests of controls vs substantive; analytical procedures vs tests of detail; and the specific technique (inspection, external confirmation, recalculation, observation, enquiry, CAATs). Also the reliability of the evidence sought.Use more reliable, more effective procedures: tests of detail over analytics; external/auditor-generated evidence (confirmations, recalculation) over internal/enquiry; target the assertion most at risk.
TIMINGWhen the procedure is done: at an interim date (with roll-forward to year-end) or at/near year-end (final); and the period covered.Shift testing to at or near year-end (less roll-forward risk); test cut-off tightly around year-end; add an element of unpredictability.
EXTENTHow much: sample size / number of items / how much of the population.Increase the sample size; test 100% of large or unusual items; use CAATs to test the whole population.
Memory hookHigh risk → "harder, later, more": harder (more reliable procedures & tests of detail), later (year-end), more (bigger samples). Plus the overall responses: experienced staff, more supervision, heightened professional skepticism, unpredictability, and lower performance materiality.

4 · The step-by-step method (use this every time)

  1. Identify the RoMM — what could go wrong, at the assertion level, and why (point to the scenario facts). Is it a significant risk or a fraud risk?
  2. Choose the overall approach — can you rely on controls (combined) or must you go substantive? Justify it (controls effective & efficient vs weak/owner-managed/significant risk).
  3. Nature — name the specific procedures and why they suit the risk (reliable evidence; right assertion).
  4. Timing — interim vs year-end, and why (risk level, cut-off).
  5. Extent — sample size / 100% of key items / CAATs, and why (risk & materiality).
  6. Overall responses — staffing, skepticism, unpredictability, materiality. Conclude by linking back to reducing detection risk.

5 · Factors that drive your N-T-E (quote these to justify)

Drives MORE/HARDER/LATER workAllows LESS/EARLIER work
High inherent risk (estimates, complex, fraud incentive, related parties); weak controls; significant/fraud risk; first-year audit; new system; going-concern doubt; low materiality; material balance; prior-year misstatements.Strong, tested controls; low inherent risk; routine high-volume transactions; immaterial balance; good prior-year history; reliable analytical relationships.

6 · Worked example — full model solution

Required: Describe the overall audit approach and the nature, timing & extent of the substantive procedures you would adopt over revenue of FastGrow (Pty) Ltd. Facts: first-year audit; revenue grew 60%; a new accounting system went live on 1 September (mid-year, 28 Feb year-end); management's bonus is linked to revenue; the auditor noted weak controls over the sales cycle & no segregation of duties; thousands of small credit sales.

Step 1 — RoMM

  • High RoMM over the occurrence assertion of revenue (overstatement risk) — driven by the bonus incentive (fraud risk) and weak controls/no segregation; revenue recognition is a presumed fraud risk (ISA 240) = a significant risk.
  • Added risk from the new system (data-migration & processing errors — accuracy/completeness) and a first-year audit (no prior assurance, opening balances).

Step 2 — Overall approach

  • Adopt a predominantly SUBSTANTIVE approach: the controls are deficient, so it would be ineffective & inefficient to test them for reliance; and a significant/fraud risk always needs a substantive response.
  • Apply heightened professional skepticism, assign more experienced staff with closer supervision, build in unpredictability, and set a lower performance materiality for revenue.

Step 3 — Nature

  • Emphasise tests of detail (not analytics alone, because it's a significant risk): vouch a sample of recorded sales back to signed delivery notes, customer orders & proof of delivery to test occurrence (the overstatement direction).
  • Use more reliable, external evidence — perform a positive external debtors confirmation; recalculate invoices (qty × price); agree prices to the approved price list.
  • Use CAATs on the full sales population: re-perform calculations, run gap/sequence tests on invoice numbers (fictitious sales/completeness), and extract unusual entries (e.g. journals to revenue near year-end).
  • Test the new system's data integrity — reconcile data migrated at 1 Sept; test the periods before and after the change separately.

Step 4 — Timing

  • Perform the substantive procedures at/near year-end (final), not interim, because the risk is high (minimise roll-forward risk).
  • Perform focused cut-off testing on the last/first deliveries & invoices around 28 Feb (occurrence & completeness in the right period).

Step 5 — Extent

  • Increase sample sizes (high risk + lower materiality); use CAATs to test 100% where possible.
  • Test all large/unusual sales and all manual journals to revenue; cover both the old and new system periods.
ConclusionBecause the RoMM (especially occurrence of revenue) is high and a significant/fraud risk, the auditor cannot rely on controls and must drive detection risk low through a robust substantive approach — reliable tests of detail & confirmations (nature), at year-end with tight cut-off (timing), on enlarged samples & CAATs over the full population (extent), supported by experienced staff and professional skepticism.

7 · Copy-paste answer template

Structure 1. Risk: "The RoMM over [assertion] of [account] is [high/low] because [scenario fact] … this is a [significant/fraud] risk."
2. Approach: "Adopt a [substantive / combined] approach because [controls weak → don't rely / controls effective & efficient → test & reduce substantive]."
3. Nature: "Use [tests of detail / external confirmation / recalculation / CAATs] focusing on [assertion], because [more reliable evidence needed]."
4. Timing: "Perform procedures at [year-end/interim] because [risk/cut-off]."
5. Extent: "Use [larger samples / 100% of large items / CAATs] because [high risk & low materiality]."
6. Overall: "Assign experienced staff, increase supervision & skepticism, add unpredictability, lower performance materiality."
