MAF / Financial Management — Discussion-Question Factor Sheet

What to look for & how to apply it · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden · read each as: Factor → apply to the scenario
How to answer ANY MAF discussion: ① state the factor/point → ② link it to a specific fact in the scenario → ③ say the impact (on profit/cash/risk/value) → ④ give a recommendation where asked. ~1 mark per applied point. Always tie it to the company's situation — never list theory alone. Give both sides (advantages & disadvantages) and conclude.

1 · Risk management — the framework

  • Risk process → identify → assess (likelihood × impact) → respond → monitor & report.
  • The 4 T's (responses) → Terminate (avoid), Treat (reduce/control), Transfer (insure/hedge/outsource), Tolerate (accept).
  • Risk appetite vs capacity → how much risk the board is willing/able to take — drives the response chosen.
  • Inherent vs residual risk → assess risk before and after controls/hedges.
ApplicationForex risk → the company has just acquired a foreign subsidiary and may suffer unfavourable exchange-rate fluctuations that reduce profitability → transfer/treat the risk via forward contracts or a natural hedge.

2 · Types of risk to spot in a scenario

Financial — market risk

  • Foreign exchange (currency) → foreign imports/exports, foreign loans or a foreign subsidiary → rand weakening raises costs / cuts translated profits.
  • Interest rate → floating-rate / variable debt → rising rates increase finance costs & reduce profit/cash.
  • Commodity price → reliant on fuel/steel/maize → input price spikes squeeze margins.
  • Equity / investment price → holds shares/investments → market falls reduce value.

Other risks

  • Credit risk → large/concentrated debtors → customer default → bad debts & cash-flow strain.
  • Liquidity risk → short-term debt > current assets / low current ratio → can't pay debts as they fall due.
  • Operational risk → systems, people, process failures, fraud → losses & disruption.
  • Business/strategic risk → competition, regulation, demand, key-customer reliance → revenue volatility.
  • Going-concern/financial-distress risk → losses, covenant breach, negative equity → survival doubt.

3 · Foreign exchange risk & hedging

  • Exposure type → transaction (a specific FX payment/receipt), translation (foreign sub's results), economic (long-run competitiveness).
  • Forward exchange contract (FEC) → locks in a rate now → certainty, but you lose any favourable movement & it's an obligation.
  • Currency option → right (not obligation) to exchange → protects downside, keeps upside, but costs a premium.
  • Money-market hedge → borrow/deposit in the foreign currency now to match the exposure → fixes the cost without a derivative.
  • Natural hedge / matching → match foreign income with foreign costs/loans → cheapest, no instrument needed.
  • Leading & lagging / netting → speed up or delay FX settlement; net intra-group flows → reduces exposure cheaply.
ApplicationImporter pays a US supplier in 90 days → exposed if the rand weakens → take a forward to fix the rand cost, or a call option if they want to keep upside (justify by cost vs certainty).

4 · Interest-rate risk & hedging

  • Interest-rate swap → swap floating for fixed → certainty of finance cost when rates are rising.
  • Forward rate agreement (FRA) → fixes the rate on future borrowing for a period.
  • Interest-rate cap / collar → caps the maximum rate (option) → protects against rises, keeps some benefit of falls.
  • Fixed vs floating mix → match to cash-flow stability & rate outlook.

5 · Debt vs Equity financing (and conversions)

Factors favouring DEBT

  • Cheaper → interest is tax-deductible & lower required return than equity → lowers WACC.
  • No dilution → existing shareholders keep control & EPS.
  • Discipline → fixed repayments focus management.
  • Use when → stable cash flows, low existing gearing, tangible security available.

Factors favouring EQUITY

  • No fixed obligation → dividends discretionary → lower financial risk/liquidity strain.
  • Supports gearing capacity → won't breach covenants; preserves credit rating.
  • Use when → volatile cash flows, high gearing, no security, long-term/risky project.
  • Downsides → dilutes control & EPS; higher cost; issue costs; signalling.
  • Effect on gearing & covenants → will more debt breach a debt:equity / interest-cover covenant?
  • Effect on EPS, control & WACC → compare each option's impact.
  • Convertible instruments / debt-to-equity conversion → convertible debt = cheaper coupon + later equity; debt-to-equity swap in distress reduces gearing & interest but dilutes owners and gives creditors control.
  • Matching principle → finance long-term assets with long-term funds; short-term needs with short-term finance.
ApplicationHighly geared company in distress offered a debt-to-equity swap → reduces interest burden & improves solvency/liquidity, but dilutes existing shareholders and may hand creditors voting control → weigh survival vs control.

6 · Sources of finance — what to consider

  • Cost (interest/return + issue costs) · Risk/gearing impact · Control/dilution · Security required · Term (match to use) · Flexibility & availability · Cash-flow & covenant effect.
  • Short-term → overdraft, trade credit, factoring (for working capital). Long-term → bank loans, bonds/debentures, leases, equity, retained earnings.

7 · Cost of capital / WACC — factors & critiques

  • What it is → the blended required return; the discount rate for projects of average risk.
  • Cost of equity (CAPM) → risk-free + β × market premium; β reflects systematic risk.
  • Cost of debt → after-tax (×(1−t)); use current/market yield, not coupon.
  • Critiques (when WACC is wrong) → project risk ≠ company risk; capital structure changing; using book not market weights; β/data unreliable; not for foreign-risk projects.

8 · Investment appraisal (NPV/IRR) — qualitative factors

  • Strategic fit → aligns with strategy & core competencies?
  • Risk & assumptions → sensitivity of NPV to key inputs; reliability of forecasts; discount-rate appropriateness.
  • Real options & flexibility → option to expand/delay/abandon adds value beyond NPV.
  • Non-financial → environmental/social (6 capitals), staff, reputation, customer/supplier relationships, BEE.
  • Financing & affordability → can it be funded without breaching liquidity/covenants?
ApplicationNPV is positive but small → it's highly sensitive to the sales-volume assumption (per sensitivity analysis) and relies on optimistic growth → recommend cautiously, subject to firmer demand evidence.

9 · Business / share valuation

  • DCF (FCFF/FCFE) → best theoretically; sensitive to growth & discount-rate assumptions → do a range.
  • Relative (P/E, EV/EBITDA) → quick & market-based but needs comparable companies; watch differences in risk/growth/accounting.
  • Net asset value → floor value; ignores goodwill/future earnings; use book vs fair values.
  • Dividend growth model → for minority/dividend-paying stakes; sensitive to g.
  • Adjustments → control premium, minority/marketability discounts, surplus assets, synergies.

10 · Capital structure & gearing

  • Gearing (D:E, debt:assets) → high gearing → higher financial risk & returns volatility; covenant pressure.
  • Interest cover → EBIT ÷ interest → ability to service debt; low cover = distress risk.
  • Trade-off → tax shield of debt vs financial-distress & bankruptcy costs.
  • Effect on WACC & value → some debt lowers WACC; too much raises it as risk rises.

11 · Dividend policy — factors

  • Cash availability & liquidity → can the company afford it without straining operations?
  • Investment opportunities → retain for positive-NPV projects vs pay out if none.
  • Signalling & shareholder expectations → cuts signal trouble; stable/growing dividends preferred.
  • Legal (s4/s46 solvency & liquidity test) · covenants · tax (dividends tax) · control (retain vs raise new equity).

12 · Working capital management

  • Cash conversion cycle → inventory days + debtor days − creditor days; longer = more cash tied up.
  • Overtrading → rapid growth + thin working capital → liquidity crisis despite profits.
  • Levers → tighten credit/collections, optimise inventory (EOQ/JIT), negotiate supplier terms, factoring/overdraft.
  • Trade-off → liquidity & security vs cost/return of holding more current assets.

13 · Mergers & acquisitions

  • Reasons for → synergies (cost/revenue), market share, diversification, economies of scale, access to tech/skills/markets, growth.
  • Risks/against → overpaying, culture clash, integration failure, overstated synergies, debt taken on, regulatory/competition issues.
  • Valuation & price → max price = standalone value + synergies; don't pay all the synergy away.
  • Financing → cash (gearing) vs shares (dilution); effect on EPS & control.
  • Success factors → due diligence, integration plan, retention of key staff, realistic synergy targets.

14 · Performance measurement

  • Ratios → profitability (GP%, OP%, ROE, ROCE), liquidity (current, quick), gearing, efficiency (turnover), investor (EPS, P/E) — compare to prior year, budget, industry.
  • ROI vs Residual Income / EVA → ROI can reject good projects below divisional ROI; RI/EVA fix this (charge for capital used).
  • Balanced scorecard → financial + customer + internal process + learning/growth → avoids short-termism.
  • Watch → manipulation, short-termism, controllability, non-financial & sustainability measures.

15 · Relevant-costing decisions

  • Relevant = future, incremental, cash flows → ignore sunk costs, allocated/committed fixed costs & non-cash items; include opportunity cost.
  • Make vs buy / outsource → compare incremental cost to make vs buy-in price; plus quality, reliability, capacity, control, strategic importance, staff impact.
  • Special order / one-off → accept if price > incremental cost AND spare capacity AND it won't harm normal prices/customers.
  • Shutdown / discontinue → compare lost contribution to fixed costs saved; consider knock-on effects & reversibility.
  • Limiting factor → maximise contribution per unit of scarce resource; consider qualitative (key customers, long-term).

16 · CVP / pricing

  • Breakeven = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit; margin of safety = how far sales can fall.
  • Pricing → cost-plus (simple but ignores demand) vs market/value-based; consider competitors, demand elasticity, strategy (penetration vs skimming).

17 · Budgeting & variance analysis

  • Investigate variances → by size/%, controllability, trend, interdependence (e.g. cheaper material → adverse usage).
  • Causes → price/rate changes, efficiency, mix, volume, unrealistic standards.
  • Budget behaviour → participation/motivation, budgetary slack, beyond-budgeting, flexed vs fixed comparison.

18 · Transfer pricing

  • Goal congruence → the price should lead divisions to act in the group's best interest.
  • Methods → market-based (best if a market exists), cost-based (full/variable + mark-up), negotiated.
  • Rule of thumb → minimum = variable cost + opportunity cost; maximum = external price.
  • Watch → divisional autonomy & motivation, fairness of performance evaluation, tax/cross-border (s31) effects.

19 · Risk & uncertainty techniques

  • Sensitivity analysis → how much an input can change before the decision flips → finds the key variable.
  • Scenario analysis → best/base/worst case combinations.
  • Expected values → probability-weighted outcome → good for repeated decisions; ignores risk attitude & one-off impact.
  • Simulation / decision trees → many variables / sequential decisions.

20 · Integrated thinking & sustainability

  • 6 capitals → financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social & relationship, natural → assess impact on each, not just profit.
  • Stakeholders & long term → trade-offs between capitals; reputational & ESG risk; King IV "apply & explain".
  • Triple context → economy, society, environment → value created/eroded over time.
