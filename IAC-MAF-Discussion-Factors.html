Method for any discussion: ① state the factor → ② link it to a specific fact/number → ③ state the impact (profit/cash/risk/value/control) → ④ give both sides → ⑤ recommend & conclude. ~1 mark per applied point. Bring in numbers (ratios, NPV, gearing) to support words.
PART A · FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

A1 · Risk — framework & types

  • Process → identify → assess (likelihood × impact) → respond → monitor/report; risk appetite vs capacity; inherent vs residual.
  • 4 T responses → Terminate · Treat · Transfer · Tolerate.
  • Forex/currency → imports/exports/foreign loan or sub → rand moves hit costs/translated profit.
  • Interest rate → floating debt → rising rates raise finance cost.
  • Commodity/input price → fuel/steel/maize reliance → margin squeeze.
  • Equity/price → investment portfolio falls in value.
  • Credit → concentrated/weak debtors → bad debts & cash strain.
  • Liquidity → short-term debt > current assets → can't pay as due.
  • Operational → systems/people/fraud failures.
  • Strategic/business & going-concern → competition, demand, key-customer reliance, distress.
ApplicationForex risk → the company just acquired a foreign subsidiary and may face unfavourable exchange-rate fluctuations that reduce profitability → hedge/transfer the exposure.

A2 · Foreign-exchange risk & hedging

  • Exposure type → transaction (specific FX payment), translation (foreign sub's results/net assets), economic (long-run competitiveness).
  • Forward (FEC) → locks the rate → certainty, but obligation & loses upside.
  • Option → right not obligation → protects downside, keeps upside, costs a premium.
  • Money-market hedge → borrow/deposit in the foreign currency to match → fixes cost without a derivative.
  • Futures → exchange-traded, standardised, margined → liquid but basis risk & not a perfect match.
  • Natural hedge / matching & netting → match foreign income to foreign costs/loans; net intra-group → cheapest.
  • Leading & lagging → speed up/delay settlement based on expected moves.
  • Rate forecasting → interest-rate parity (forward = spot × rate differential), purchasing-power parity (inflation differential).

A3 · Interest-rate risk & hedging

  • Swap (floating↔fixed) → certainty when rates rising; FRA fixes a future borrowing rate; cap/collar caps the max rate, collar limits both ends; fixed/floating mix matched to cash-flow stability & outlook.

A4 · Hedging policy & treasury function

  • Whether to hedge at all → cost of hedging vs exposure size; materiality; shareholders may already be diversified; risk appetite; competitor practice.
  • Derivative dangers → speculation, counterparty risk, complexity, need for controls & authorisation, mark-to-market volatility.
  • Treasury: centralised vs decentralised → central = netting, expertise, bulk rates, control; decentral = local responsiveness.
  • Country/political risk → expropriation, exchange controls, instability → political-risk insurance, local funding, JV with locals.

A5 · Debt vs Equity & conversions

Favours DEBT

  • Cheaper → tax-deductible interest, lower required return → lowers WACC.
  • No dilution of control/EPS; discipline of fixed repayments.
  • Use when → stable cash flows, low gearing, security available.

Favours EQUITY

  • No fixed obligation → lower financial/liquidity risk; preserves covenants & rating.
  • Use when → volatile cash flows, high gearing, risky/long-term project, no security.
  • Downsides → dilution, higher cost, issue costs, negative signalling.
  • Check gearing & covenants, EPS, control, WACC for each option.
  • Convertibles → lower coupon now + later equity; debt-to-equity swap (distress) cuts interest & gearing but dilutes owners & hands creditors control.
  • Matching principle → long-term assets ← long-term funds; short-term needs ← short-term finance.
ApplicationDistressed, highly geared company offered a debt-to-equity swap → improves solvency & cuts interest, but dilutes shareholders and may give creditors voting control → weigh survival vs control.

A6 · Sources of finance & special transactions

  • Choosing a source — "CRCS-TFA" → Cost · Risk/gearing · Control/dilution · Security · Term (match) · Flexibility · Availability · cash-flow/covenant effect.
  • Short-term → overdraft, trade credit, factoring/invoice discounting. Long-term → bank loan, bond/debenture, lease, equity, retained earnings.
  • Rights issue → cheaper, pre-emptive (no dilution if taken up); compute TERP = (old value + rights proceeds)/new shares; value of a right.
  • Share buyback → returns surplus cash, boosts EPS, signals undervaluation; reduces equity → raises gearing.
  • Scrip/capitalisation dividend → conserves cash; tax considerations.
  • IPO/listing → access to capital, liquidity, profile vs cost, disclosure, loss of control, short-termism.
  • B-BBEE deal → funding structure (vendor finance, notional vesting), dilution, scorecard benefit, lock-in.
  • PE/VC & MBO/LBO → growth/exit capital; high gearing (LBO) raises risk; investor exit horizon & control rights.
  • SME/start-up finance → limited security/track record → owner funds, grants, angel/VC, crowdfunding.

A7 · Cost of capital / WACC

  • WACC = discount rate for average-risk projects; market weights, after-tax cost of debt.
  • CAPM → Rf + β(Rm−Rf); β = systematic risk.
  • Risk-adjusted / divisional rate → use a pure-play beta: ungear the comparable's β, then regear at the project's gearing (Hamada).
  • WACC critiques (when not to use it) → project risk ≠ firm risk; changing capital structure; book (not market) weights; unreliable β/data; foreign/different-risk projects; use APV when financing effects are significant.

A8 · Investment appraisal

  • NPV best (absolute value, time value); IRR watch multiple/!=NPV ranking; payback liquidity/risk screen; ARR ignores cash/time.
  • Relevant cash flows → incremental, after-tax, include working-capital changes & opportunity costs; exclude sunk & allocated costs, depreciation (add back), financing flows.
  • Inflation → be consistent: real cash flows ↔ real rate, nominal ↔ nominal (money) rate.
  • Tax → tax on profits, capital allowances (wear&tear) tax shield, timing/lag.
  • Capital rationing → single-period & divisible → rank by profitability index (NPV/investment); indivisible → trial combinations; multi-period → LP.
  • Lease vs buy → compare PV of leasing vs PV of buying/borrowing (after-tax, at after-tax cost of debt).
  • Replacement → equivalent annual cost / optimal replacement cycle.
  • Foreign project → appraise in foreign currency then convert, or convert flows; add country risk; blocked remittances.
  • Qualitative → strategic fit, real options (expand/delay/abandon), ESG/6 capitals, staff/reputation, affordability/financing.
ApplicationNPV positive but small → highly sensitive to the sales-volume input (per sensitivity analysis) → accept cautiously pending firmer demand evidence.

A9 · Valuation (business / shares)

  • DCF (FCFF/FCFE) best in theory; sensitive to g & discount rate → use a range.
  • Relative (P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B) quick & market-based; needs true comparables; adjust for risk/growth/accounting differences.
  • Net asset value = floor; ignores goodwill/earnings; book vs fair value.
  • Dividend growth model for minority/dividend stakes; very sensitive to g.
  • Adjustments → control premium, minority & marketability discounts, surplus/non-operating assets, synergies, contingent liabilities.

A10 · Capital structure & dividend policy

Capital structure / gearing

  • Gearing & interest cover → high → more financial risk, covenant & distress pressure.
  • Trade-off → debt tax shield vs distress/bankruptcy costs; effect on WACC & value.
  • M&M, pecking order, signalling as theory hooks.

Dividend policy

  • Cash/liquidity & investment opportunities (retain for +NPV) → afford it?
  • Signalling & clientele → stable/growing preferred; cuts signal trouble.
  • Legal s4/s46 solvency & liquidity, covenants, tax, control (retain vs new equity).

A11 · Working-capital management

  • Cash conversion cycle = inventory days + debtor days − creditor days; longer → more cash tied up.
  • Overtrading (growth + thin WC → crisis) vs overcapitalisation (idle WC → low return).
  • Financing policy → aggressive (cheap, risky) / conservative (safe, costly) / matching.
  • Debtors → credit policy, settlement discounts (cost vs faster cash), factoring, age analysis.
  • Inventory → EOQ (√(2CoD/Ch)), bulk-discount evaluation, JIT (less holding cost vs stockout/supplier risk).
  • Cash → Baumol & Miller-Orr models; surplus investment; overdraft buffer.
  • Creditors → use free credit; weigh loss of discount/goodwill if stretched.

A12 · M&A, restructuring & distress

  • Reasons for M&A → synergies (cost/revenue), scale, market share, diversification, tech/skills/market access, growth, tax.
  • Types → horizontal/vertical/conglomerate; consideration cash (gearing) vs shares (dilution, EPS effect).
  • Risks/against → overpay, culture clash, integration failure, overstated synergies, debt burden, competition approval.
  • Price → max = standalone + synergies; don't pay all synergy away; defences (poison pill, white knight).
  • Demerger/unbundling/divestiture → unlock value, focus, remove conglomerate discount (links to s46 corporate rules).
  • Turnaround/business rescue → assess viability, restructure debt (debt-equity swap), dispose non-core, cost cuts, new funding; going-concern indicators & mitigations.

A13 · Markets, governance & integrated thinking

  • Market efficiency (EMH) → if semi-strong efficient, share price reflects public info → timing issues/window-dressing won't fool the market.
  • Agency & governance of finance → manager vs shareholder conflicts → incentives, KING IV, treasury controls/authorisation.
  • Shareholder vs stakeholder value & 6 capitals → financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social&relationship, natural → assess impact on each; ESG/reputational risk; King IV "apply & explain".
PART B · MANAGEMENT ACCOUNTING

B1 · Costing systems — which & why

  • Absorption vs marginal → absorption defers fixed cost in inventory (profit differs with stock movement); marginal better for decisions.
  • Activity-based costing (ABC) → use when overheads are high & diverse products → more accurate product cost & pricing; but costly/complex to run.
  • Throughput accounting / theory of constraints → maximise throughput through the bottleneck; rank by throughput per bottleneck-hour.
  • Target costing → market price − required margin = target cost → design to cost (gap analysis).
  • Life-cycle costing → total cost over the product's life (incl. R&D, end-of-life).
  • Kaizen (continuous cost reduction); quality costs (prevention + appraisal + internal & external failure); environmental MA.

B2 · Decision-making (relevant costing)

  • Relevant = future, incremental, cash → ignore sunk, allocated & committed fixed costs, non-cash; include opportunity cost.
  • Make vs buy / outsource → incremental make cost vs buy price; + quality, reliability, capacity, control, IP, staff, strategic importance.
  • Special order → accept if price > incremental cost, spare capacity, won't undercut normal prices/customers.
  • Shutdown/discontinue → lost contribution vs fixed costs saved; knock-on effects; reversibility.
  • Limiting factor → maximise contribution per unit of scarce resource (or throughput per bottleneck hour); qualitative (key customers).
  • Joint products / further processing → process further only if incremental revenue > incremental cost (ignore joint costs already incurred).

B3 · Decisions under risk & uncertainty

  • Sensitivity → % change in an input before the decision flips → finds the critical variable.
  • Scenario (best/base/worst); expected values (probability-weighted; good for repeated decisions, ignores risk attitude & one-off impact).
  • Decision trees (sequential decisions); simulation (many variables).
  • Value of (perfect/imperfect) information → max worth paying = improvement in expected outcome from better info.
  • Risk attitude → maximin (cautious), maximax (optimist), minimax regret.

B4 · CVP & pricing

  • Breakeven = fixed ÷ contribution/unit; margin of safety; target-profit volume; multi-product weighted contribution.
  • Pricing → cost-plus (simple, ignores demand) vs market/value-based; penetration vs skimming; price discrimination; elasticity; competitor & strategic positioning.

B5 · Budgeting & variances

  • Investigate variances → size/%, controllability, trend, interdependence (cheap material → adverse usage), reliability of standard.
  • Advanced variances → materials/labour mix & yield; sales mix & quantity; planning vs operational (revise the standard for uncontrollable changes).
  • Budget behaviour → participation/motivation, budgetary slack, fixed vs flexed comparison, rolling budgets, beyond budgeting, ZBB vs incremental.

B6 · Performance measurement

  • Ratios → profitability (GP%, OP%, ROE, ROCE), liquidity (current/quick), gearing, efficiency (turnovers), investor (EPS, P/E) — compare to prior year, budget, industry; state limitations (accounting policies, one-offs, inflation, non-financial).
  • ROI vs RI/EVA → ROI can reject good projects below divisional ROI; RI/EVA charge for capital used → better goal congruence.
  • Balanced scorecard → financial + customer + internal process + learning/growth → avoids short-termism.
  • Divisional performance & transfer pricing → goal congruence; market vs cost-plus vs negotiated; min = variable + opportunity cost, max = external price; autonomy, fairness, cross-border (s31) tax.
  • Benchmarking & KPIs; controllability principle; non-financial & ESG measures.
