Double Tax Agreements (DTAs)

Summary · how to discuss & apply · worked examples + solutions · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden · from the IAC Tax Notes & Tutorials (Mauritius & UK DTAs examinable)
Why DTAs exist: SA taxes residents on worldwide income and non-residents on SA-source income — so the same income can be taxed twice (once on residence, once on source). A DTA is a treaty between two countries that allocates the taxing right and provides relief. Relief can also come from domestic law (s6quat rebate / s64N). Examinable DTAs: Mauritius & UK — only limited articles (per the Examinable Pronouncements); the full text is in your SAICA Legislation Handbook.

1 · The golden principles of a DTA (learn these — easy marks)

  • A DTA never creates tax — it only decides which country may tax income that both countries' domestic law already taxes. If domestic law doesn't tax it, the DTA can't.
  • It works by giving the taxing right to a country, limiting it, or being silent. An article may or may not remove double tax — sometimes both countries still get to tax, and you then claim relief under the elimination-of-double-tax article (Article 22 Mauritius / Article 21 UK) or under domestic law (s6quat / s64N).
  • Most DTAs follow the OECD Model Tax Convention (or the UN model). They apply to the Income Tax Act via section 108.
  • A DTA applies equally to residents of both contracting states — not only to SA residents or SA taxes.
  • It does not apply automatically — the taxpayer must invoke it.
  • Residence is the gateway: a person must be a resident of a contracting state to use the treaty; a tie-breaker article resolves dual residence (individuals: permanent home → centre of vital interests → habitual abode → nationality; companies: place of effective management).

2 · The two ways to relieve double tax

RouteHow it worksKey rules
Domestic — s6quat rebateA rebate (reduces SA normal tax) for foreign tax paid on foreign-source income included in a resident's taxable income.Residents only · foreign-source amounts only · rebate limited to the SA tax on the foreign income · excess carries forward 7 years · foreign tax translated at the average rate.
Domestic — s64NA rebate against SA dividends tax for foreign tax (e.g. foreign WHT) already paid on a foreign dividend.Caps the combined dividend tax (foreign + SA) at the SA rate.
Treaty — DTA articleThe relevant article either gives the right to one country only, or limits the source country's rate; the elimination article (Art 22 Mau / Art 21 UK) gives a credit in the residence country.Must be invoked; applies per the specific article; can be combined with s6quat on the reduced foreign tax (see below).
The s6quat ↔ DTA interaction (high-yield)You may use s6quat instead of a DTA, but not in addition to it. If the DTA extinguishes the foreign tax, there's no foreign tax left to rebate. If the DTA merely reduces the foreign tax, you claim the s6quat rebate on the lower (post-DTA) amount. Invoking the DTA is worth it whenever your s6quat rebate was already capped (foreign tax > SA tax on that income) — the DTA cuts the foreign tax you can't recover anyway.

3 · How to discuss & apply a DTA — the exam method

  1. Establish residence & source. State who is resident where, and where the income is sourced — that's why double tax arises. Apply the tie-breaker if the person is resident in both.
  2. Confirm the DTA applies. Both parties resident in contracting states; the income type is covered by an article; remember the DTA must be invoked.
  3. Find the correct article for the income type (dividends, interest, royalties, business profits, employment, capital gains) and state which country may tax and any rate limit.
  4. Apply domestic law first (what would SA tax without the treaty?), then overlay the DTA limit.
  5. Eliminate the remaining double tax — quantify relief via the elimination article and/or s6quat/s64N.
  6. Conclude: the net tax in each country, and advise whether to invoke the DTA (compare total tax with vs without).
Mark-earning phrases: "the DTA does not create a tax, it allocates the taxing right" · "as a resident of a Contracting State…" · "the source/residence state's right is limited to X%" · "relief under Article 22/21 (elimination of double taxation)" · "the DTA must be invoked by the taxpayer" · "s6quat rebate is limited to the SA tax on the foreign income".

5 · Worked examples & model solutions (discussion in bullets)

Example 1 — SA dividend to a Mauritian resident (limiting the source rate)

Mr A, a resident of Mauritius, receives a R100 dividend from an SA company.

  • SA position (domestic): SA-source dividend → 20% dividends tax (no s64F exemption applies); included in SA gross income but exempt for normal tax (s10(1)(k)).
  • Mauritius position: also taxable in Mauritius on the basis of Mr A's residence.
  • Apply the DTA (Art 10): the article confirms the residence state (Mauritius) may tax, and limits SA's (source) dividends tax to 10%. By invoking it, SA tax drops from 20% → 10%.
  • Eliminate remaining double tax (Art 22): Mauritius allows the SA 10% as a credit against the Mauritian tax on the dividend.
  • Conclusion: Mr A should invoke the DTA — SA tax limited to 10%, with a credit in Mauritius for that 10%.

Example 2 — Foreign dividend received by an SA company: should LTCE invoke the DTA?

LTCE (SA resident) holds <10% of a Mauritian company and receives a foreign dividend of R1 500 000; full Mauritian tax of R560 000 was paid. The dividend is partly exempt under s10B(3) (ratio 7/27 for a company).

  • Without the DTA the s6quat rebate was already capped at R300 000, so LTCE couldn't recover R260 000 of the R560 000 foreign tax. The DTA reduces the foreign tax to R150 000, cutting the unrecoverable amount.
  • Conclusion / advice: invoke the DTA — total tax falls from R560 000 to R300 000 (a R260 000 saving). (You still claim s6quat, but only on the reduced R150 000.)

Example 3 — Foreign factory (permanent establishment / business profits)

An SA-resident company runs a factory in Swaziland (eSwatini) (a source-based system); the SA–Swaziland DTA provides that the factory income may be taxed in Swaziland.

  • SA position: as a resident, the company is taxed on worldwide income — so the Swaziland factory profit is in SA taxable income too.
  • DTA (business-profits / PE article): the factory is a permanent establishment in Swaziland, so Swaziland (the source state) may tax the profits attributable to that PE.
  • Relief: SA gives a s6quat rebate for the Swaziland tax (limited to the SA tax on that foreign income). Note: if no tax was actually paid in Swaziland (e.g. it had an assessed loss), there is no foreign tax to rebate — s6quat rebate = R0.
  • Conclusion: include the PE profit in SA; claim a s6quat rebate only to the extent Swaziland tax was actually paid.

6 · Common traps & quick reminders

  • Don't say the DTA "exempts" income — it allocates/limits the taxing right; relief comes via the elimination article or s6quat.
  • Residence first: no treaty access without being a resident of a contracting state; resolve dual residence with the tie-breaker before anything else.
  • s6quat is residents-only and foreign-source-only, and is capped at the SA tax on the foreign income (excess carried forward 7 years).
  • No double-dip: s6quat relief is on the foreign tax after DTA reduction — never both in full.
  • Foreign dividends: the s6quat rebate considers all the foreign tax (without regard to the s10B(3) exemption), but the limit uses only the taxable portion of the dividend.
  • Invoke it: always state that the taxpayer must elect to apply the DTA, then conclude with the with-vs-without comparison.
