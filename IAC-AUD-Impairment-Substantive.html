Substantive Procedures — Impairment (IAS 36)

Recoverable amount = higher of value-in-use (VIU) & fair value less costs of disposal (FVLCD/FVLCS) · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden
The core idea: impairment is a valuation question. Recoverable amount = the higher of VIU and FVLCD; impairment = carrying amount − recoverable amount. Audit both the calculation (arithmetic) and the subjective inputs (assumptions, discount rate, growth, cash flows) — that's where the risk sits. (Note: in your Jan 2026 & June 2025 papers this was not asked directly as substantive procedures — the example below is the explosives-CGU case from your Substantive-Procedures pack.)
Example to go over (from your pack)An impairment loss of ZAR74.46m was recognised on an explosives CGU. Management computed VIU using an 8% discount rate and a five-year projected cash-flow schedule (no terminal value); FVLCD was determined by a management expert. Required: describe the substantive procedures over the impairment loss.

1 · General / opening procedures

AreaProcedure
Obtain & re-performObtain management's recoverable-amount calculation; re-perform the casts for arithmetical accuracy; agree the carrying amount to the GL/FAR.
IndicatorsInspect minutes & discuss with management for impairment indicators (external: market/rates/decline; internal: damage, obsolescence, worse performance); confirm goodwill/indefinite-life intangibles are tested annually.
MRL & disclosureObtain a signed MRL on the impairment; inspect the notes for IAS 36 disclosure; inspect the correcting journal & that the TB/AFS were adjusted.

2 · Value-in-use (VIU) procedures

AreaProcedure
Re-perform VIURe-perform the VIU using the stated 8% discount rate; agree the cash flows to the approved five-year strategic plan.
Cash inflowsDiscuss assumptions & assess viability; agree future inflows to signed contracts for new customers and price lists/proof of future sales for existing customers; confirm non-operational/unsigned cash flows are excluded.
Cash outflowsEvaluate reasonableness vs your knowledge of the business (fixed & variable cost of production); agree input costs to price lists/invoices at active market prices (not internal transfer prices); confirm servicing & maintenance costs are included.
Discount rateAudit the reasonableness of the discount rate by comparing it to similar listed companies / a WACC computation; confirm it is pre-tax.
Growth / terminal valueDiscuss why a terminal value was/wasn't included; evaluate the reasonableness of the growth rate vs business knowledge & external market research.
Disallowed items / biasConfirm future restructuring not yet committed & financing/tax cash flows are excluded; consider potential management bias/manipulation (e.g. bonus- or marketing-linked motivation).

3 · Fair value less costs of disposal (FVLCD/FVLCS) procedures

AreaProcedure
Fair value sourceAgree the fair value to an active-market price / recent arm's-length disposals of similar assets; assess the IFRS 13 valuation technique & inputs.
Costs of disposalInspect documentation supporting the costs to sell (legal fees, commission, removal); recompute FVLCD = fair value − costs of disposal; ensure only incremental disposal costs are included (e.g. termination benefits are NOT a cost of disposal per IAS 36 — request adjustment if included).
Reliance on the expertWhere a management expert determined FVLCD (ISA 500): assess competence (qualifications/membership), objectivity/independence (interests/relationships), and the scope & work (engagement letter, methods, assumptions, source data, conclusions vs auditor's expectation). If a significant disagreement remains → appoint an auditor's expert (ISA 620).

4 · Recoverable amount & accounting

AreaProcedure
Recoverable amountInspect that the correct recoverable amount was used — the higher of VIU and FVLCD; recompute the impairment = carrying amount − recoverable amount.
CGU allocationIf a CGU: confirm the loss is allocated to goodwill first, then pro-rata to other assets (none below their own recoverable amount).
Journal & reversalInspect the impairment journal (Dr impairment loss / Cr accumulated impairment) is posted correctly; confirm goodwill impairment is not reversed; any reversal is limited to depreciated historical cost.
Closest exam example to revise (Jan 2026 P1(c))The nearest substantive-procedures-on-impairment question in your two papers is the inventory obsolescence allowance (IAS 2 — lower of cost & NRV, not IAS 36): use GAS to extract items past the clearance date marked down, re-perform the allowance calc, GAS-compare cost vs NRV (50% of price or the discounted price) to find required write-downs, confirm the mark-down % & inspect subsequent sales. Same "valuation" logic, different standard.
Exam tipState the procedure as: verb → source → compare-to → assertion (valuation). Split your answer into VIU and FVLCD; always test the discount rate, growth rate, cash-flow assumptions and the "higher of" rule; reliance on a valuer = competence · objectivity · work. The risk assertion is almost always valuation.
