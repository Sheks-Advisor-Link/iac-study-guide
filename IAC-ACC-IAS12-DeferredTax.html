IAS 12 — Deferred Tax & Tax-Rate Reconciliation

Rules · the calculation formats · a full worked solution · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden  |  company rate 27% · CGT inclusion 80% (effective 21.6%)
Two engines in IAS 12: (1) Current tax = taxable income × rate (start from accounting profit and adjust). (2) Deferred tax = the tax effect of temporary differences (Carrying Amount vs Tax Base) × the rate expected on reversal. The tax-rate reconciliation then proves the total tax expense by starting at "accounting profit × 27%" and adjusting only for permanent differences, rate differences & prior-year items — temporary differences never appear (deferred tax already handles them).

1 · The core rules

Tax base (the amount the asset/liability is "worth" to SARS)

  • Asset: tax base = the amount that will be deductible for tax in future = cost − tax allowances already claimed. (If the economic benefits aren't taxable, tax base = carrying amount.)
  • Liability: tax base = carrying amount − the amount deductible for tax in future. (For revenue received in advance that's already taxed: tax base = CA − amount not taxable in future = often 0.)

Temporary differences → DTL or DTA

SituationTypeGivesWhy
Asset: CA > TB  (or Liability: CA < TB)Taxable temp diffDeferred tax LIABILITYFuture taxable profit will be higher (e.g. tax allowances claimed faster than depreciation).
Asset: CA < TB  (or Liability: CA > TB)Deductible temp diffDeferred tax ASSETFuture tax deduction available (e.g. provision expensed now, deductible later); raise a DTA only if probable future taxable profit.

Rate & "manner of recovery"

  • Use the rate expected when the difference reverses. Recovery through use → normal rate 27%. Recovery through sale (e.g. land, investment property at fair value) → CGT rate (27% × 80% = 21.6%). Investment property at fair value is presumed recovered through sale.

Exemptions & where deferred tax goes

  • Initial recognition exemption: no deferred tax on the initial recognition of an asset/liability in a transaction that is not a business combination and affects neither accounting nor taxable profit; no deferred tax on goodwill.
  • Deferred tax on items recognised in OCI/equity (e.g. a revaluation surplus, FVOCI) goes to OCI/equity, not P/L.
  • Raise a DTA on an assessed loss / unused credits only to the extent future taxable profit is probable.

Exempt income: local dividends, certain government grants. Different-rate items: the non-taxable portion of a capital gain (20% for companies). The golden splitPermanent differences never reverse → they change the effective tax rate → they go in the tax-rate reconciliation. Temporary differences reverse over time → they are dealt with by deferred tax → they do NOT go in the reconciliation.

6 · Watch-outs & exam tips

  • Sort first: for every adjustment ask "permanent or temporary?" — permanent → recon; temporary → deferred tax. Getting this split right earns most of the marks.
  • Direction of deferred tax: asset CA > TB → DTL; asset CA < TB → DTA; reverse for liabilities. Sense-check: tax allowances ahead of depreciation = DTL.
  • Manner of recovery: land/investment property at FV → use the CGT effective rate (21.6%); split a revalued depreciable asset between "use" (27%) and "sale" (21.6%) portions if needed.
  • OCI items: deferred tax on a revaluation surplus or FVOCI goes to OCI, and that part is excluded from the P/L tax expense (and shown separately if reconciling).
  • DTA recognition: only raise a DTA (incl. on an assessed loss) if future taxable profit is probable — otherwise it's an unrecognised item that appears in the recon.
  • Capital gains: tax the gain at 80% inclusion; the 20% non-taxable portion is a reconciling item (a "different rate" effect).
  • Prior-year under/over provision of current tax is a reconciling item (not a temporary difference).
