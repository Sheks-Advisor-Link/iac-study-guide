Corporate Rules of the Income Tax Act

s41 · s42 · s45 · s24BA · s40CA — rules in simple terms + worked examples · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden
The big idea: the corporate rules (s41–47; only s41, s42 & s45 are examinable) let assets move between companies/shareholders in a tax-neutral way — no immediate CGT or recoupment — by making the receiver "step into the shoes" of the transferor. The tax isn't forgiven, it's deferred until the asset later leaves. s24BA and s40CA are the partner provisions that police value mismatches and set the acquirer's base cost.

s41 — Definitions & the gateway

  • "Group of companies" here is narrower than the s1 definition: a controlling company + ≥70%-held subsidiaries. Excluded: companies effectively managed outside SA and non-profit companies. Shares held as trading stock / under a repo are ignored when testing the 70%.
  • The corporate rules override most other sections (e.g. s8(4)(a) recoupments are switched off on the roll-over) — but s41(2) keeps certain sections alive, importantly s24BA.
  • The rules apply automatically to a qualifying transaction unless the parties elect out in writing.
In plain Englishs41 is the "rulebook cover": it tells you who counts as a group (70%), turns off the normal exit taxes when you roll over — but warns that s24BA can still bite a bad-value deal.

s42 — Asset-for-share transactions

When it applies (all must hold)

  • A person (company, trust or natural person) disposes of an asset whose market value ≥ its base cost (no built-in loss);
  • to a resident company, in exchange for equity shares in that company; and
  • after the deal the person holds a "qualifying interest" — listed shares (or to be listed within 12 months), a CIS portfolio, ≥10% equity & voting rights, a fellow group company share, or the person is a natural person who will be engaged full-time in the company's business.
  • The asset must keep its nature (capital asset stays capital; trading stock stays trading stock).

Tax effect (roll-over)

  • The person disposes at base cost / trading-stock value → no capital gain, no recoupment.
  • The shares received take a base cost = the base cost of the asset given up, acquired on the same date.
  • The company "steps into the shoes" of the person (same cost, date, allowances, future recoupment/gain).
  • Applies automatically unless elected out in writing (or the disposal has no tax effect, or the asset is a share/debt in that same company).

Example 16.1 — does it qualify? (the qualifying-interest test)

A person transfers trading stock (cost R100 000, MV R120 000) to ABC (Pty) Ltd for shares:

① Receives 5% of equity shares (unlisted company)No — <10% in an unlisted company
② 5% + company will be listed within 12 monthsYes — listed-share interest
③ 5% + person will work full-time for the companyYes — full-time-engagement interest
PrincipleBelow 10% normally fails — unless the shares are (becoming) listed, or the transferor will be a full-time worker in the business.

Example 16.2 — the roll-over numbers

Mr X transfers a commercial building (base cost R600 000, MV R1 500 000) to ABC (Pty) Ltd, a resident company, for 15% of its equity shares.

Qualifies: resident co · for equity shares · MV (1.5m) > base cost (600k) · 15% ≥ 10% qualifying interests42 applies

PrincipleThe R900 000 built-in gain isn't taxed now — it's parked in both the shares and the building at R600 000, and surfaces only when Mr X sells the shares or ABC sells the building.

s45 — Intra-group transactions

When it applies

  • An asset is transferred between two companies that are part of the same group of companies (the s41 ≥70% definition);
  • the consideration may be anything except equity shares (shares would trigger s42 instead);
  • assets generally keep their nature. Unlike s42, s45 does NOT require MV ≥ carrying value (built-in losses can roll too).
  • Applies by default unless the parties agree in writing not to apply it.

Tax effect

  • No capital gain or recoupment for the transferring company; the transferee steps into the shoes (date & cost of acquisition, allowances claimed to date, etc.).
  • Tax is deferred until the asset is disposed of outside the group (a "de-grouping" charge protects against assets leaving the group shortly after — anti-avoidance, not examinable in detail).

Example — s45 roll-over

Holdco owns 100% of both Sub A and Sub B (all SA resident → a group). Sub A sells a machine (tax value R800 000, MV R2 000 000, allowances of R400 000 claimed) to Sub B for R2 000 000 cash.

Same ≥70% group · consideration is cash (not shares) → s45 applies by defaultroll-over
Sub A: capital gain & s8(4) recoupmentR0 (deferred)
Sub B acquires the machine at Sub A's tax value & historyR800 000
When Sub B later sells the machine outside the grouptax crystallises then
PrincipleMoving assets within a 70% group is tax-neutral — the group is treated as one economic unit; the receiver inherits the cost, date and allowances, so the deferred gain/recoupment comes home only when the asset leaves the group.

s24BA — Value mismatch in an asset-for-share deal

  • Applies when a company acquires an asset and pays with its own shares, but the value given (shares) ≠ the value received (asset) — i.e. not what arm's-length parties would do. (Kept alive even inside s42 by s41(2).)
  • If asset MV > shares MV (company gained value): the company has a deemed capital gain = asset MV − shares MV.
  • If shares MV > asset MV (company over-paid in shares): the excess reduces the holder's base cost in the shares / is treated as a deemed dividend in specie.

Example 17 — s24BA + s40CA together

A (Pty) Ltd issues shares (2% holding, MV R250 000) to James for a capital asset (MV R400 000, base cost R200 000). Not a connected person, <10% → s42 does not apply, so it's an ordinary disposal + s24BA.

A (Pty) Ltd — asset MV R400k > shares MV R250k → deemed capital gain s24BA = 400 000 − 250 000R150 000
Taxable capital gain = 150 000 × 80%R120 000
A's base cost in the asset (s40CA): shares MV 250 000 + s24BA gain 150 000R400 000
James — proceeds on disposing the asset = MV of shares receivedR250 000

Capital gain R50 000 − annual exclusion R40 000 = 10 000 × 40%R4 000
James's base cost in the shares: asset base cost 200 000 − excess (400k−250k) 150 000R50 000
PrincipleIf a company gets an asset worth more than the shares it hands over, SARS taxes the company on the bargain (s24BA). The asset's cost is then bumped up by that taxed gain (s40CA) so it isn't taxed twice later.

s40CA — Base cost of an asset acquired by issuing shares

  • Where a company acquires an asset and the consideration is the issue of its own shares, the company is deemed to have actually incurred a cost = the market value of the shares issued (s40CA(a)) — so it has a proper base cost / cost for allowances.
  • Plus s40CA(b): that cost is increased by any capital gain the company had to recognise under s24BA (avoids double tax — see Example 17, asset base cost = 250 000 + 150 000 = R400 000).
  • Where s42 applies instead, the company takes the asset at the transferor's base cost (step-in) — increased by any s24BA gain.

Example — straightforward s40CA

B Ltd issues new shares with a market value of R1 000 000 to acquire equipment (the deal is at arm's length, so no s24BA).

B Ltd's deemed cost / base cost in the equipment (s40CA(a))R1 000 000
This is the figure B uses for s11(e)/s12C allowances & future CGT base cost—
PrinciplePaying for an asset with your own shares isn't "free" for tax — the company gets a real cost equal to the value of the shares it issued, so it can claim allowances and has a base cost.

Transfer duty, STT & VAT (s41–47)

  • Transfer duty exemption for immovable property transferred under a corporate rule; STT exemption for equity shares transferred.
  • VAT: the parties are treated as one and the same person for goods/services supplied under s42/44/45/47 where both are vendors — but for s42 & s45 only on the supply of a going concern (s8(25) VAT Act).

Exam technique — how to answer a corporate-rules question

  1. Identify the transaction: shares issued for an asset → s42 / s24BA / s40CA; asset moved within a 70% group for cash → s45.
  2. Check the requirements one by one (resident company? equity shares? MV ≥ base cost? qualifying interest ≥10% / listed / full-time? same group ≥70%?). State each as met / not met.
  3. State the consequence: roll-over (no gain/recoupment; step into the shoes) — and the base costs of both the asset and the shares.
  4. Always test s24BA separately on any value mismatch (s41(2) keeps it alive even inside s42); then fix the base cost with s40CA.
  5. Mention the election out in writing, and transfer duty / STT / VAT relief if relevant.
