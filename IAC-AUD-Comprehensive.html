Comprehensive Auditing Summary

The full audit lifecycle · ISAs · ethics · reporting · governance · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden  |  pair with the separate "Substantive Procedures" pack for cycle-by-cycle detail
The audit in one line: the auditor gathers sufficient appropriate evidence to give reasonable assurance that the AFS are free from material misstatement, and expresses an opinion. Everything below is just how: assess the risk → respond to the risk → conclude & report — done ethically, with professional skepticism, inside a quality system.

1 · Fundamentals (ISA 200)

  • Objective: obtain reasonable (high, not absolute) assurance → express an opinion on whether the AFS are fairly presented per the framework (IFRS).
  • Reasonable assurance ≠ guarantee — inherent limitations: sampling, judgement, estimates, collusion/fraud, persuasive not conclusive evidence.
  • Professional skepticism: a questioning mind, alert to contradictory evidence & fraud — never assume management is honest or dishonest.
  • Professional judgement: applied throughout (materiality, risk, evidence sufficiency, opinion).

The audit risk model

AR = IR × CR × DRAudit risk = issuing a clean opinion on materially wrong AFS. IR & CR (= RoMM) are the client's risk — the auditor can't change them. Only DR (detection risk) is the auditor's lever: high RoMM → lower DR → do more/better substantive work (more extent, year-end timing, experienced staff).

2 · Ethics & independence (SAICA Code of Professional Conduct)

Five fundamental principles — "PI-CCP"

  • Professional behaviour · Integrity · Confidentiality · Competence & due care · Professional behaviour / Objectivity. (Integrity, Objectivity, Professional competence & due care, Confidentiality, Professional behaviour.)

Independence — the 5 threats & safeguards

ThreatTrigger in a scenario
Self-interestFee dependence, financial interest/shares in client, loan to/from client, contingent fees, overdue fees.
Self-reviewAuditing your own work — preparing the AFS/valuation/journals you then audit; recent ex-employee.
AdvocacyPromoting the client's position (acting for them in litigation, promoting shares).
FamiliarityLong association, close/family relationship, ex-partner now director, gifts & hospitality.
IntimidationThreats of dismissal, dominant client, litigation, pressure to lower fees / accept wrong treatment.
How to answer an independence question① name the threat → ② link it to the fact → ③ assess significance → ④ give a safeguard (remove the staff member, independent review/EQR, rotate partner, decline/withdraw, use a separate team) → ⑤ conclude whether independence is impaired. Conceptual framework: identify → evaluate → address.

Companies Act independence (auditor)

  • s90(2): the auditor may not be a director/prescribed officer/employee, or someone who kept the books / prepared the AFS, in the current or prior year; nor a person who, alone or with a partner, habitually does the company's secretarial/bookkeeping work. Cooling-off: not within 5 years of being such.

3 · Quality management (ISQM 1 & ISA 220)

  • ISQM 1 (firm level) — 8 components: Governance & leadership · Risk assessment · Ethics · Acceptance & continuance · Engagement performance · Resources · Information & communication · Monitoring & remediation. A risk-based "system around the audits".
  • ISA 220 (engagement level): the engagement partner is responsible for overall quality — direction, supervision, review; EQR (engagement quality review) for listed/high-risk engagements before the report is signed.

4 · Acceptance & continuance (ISA 210/220)

  • Before accepting: assess client integrity, the firm's competence & resources, independence, and any NOCLAR/RI history; agree the terms (engagement letter) & confirm a suitable financial reporting framework + management's responsibilities (the "premise").
  • Predecessor auditor: with client permission, enquire about reasons for change, disagreements, integrity (professional courtesy).
Decline / be cautious triggersmanagement lacks integrity (prior fraud, aggressive accounting), scope limitations imposed, fee/independence problems, firm lacks industry expertise, unrealistic deadline.

5 · Planning — materiality & risk (ISA 320 & 315)

Materiality (ISA 320)

  • Overall materiality = a benchmark × % (e.g. 5% profit before tax, 1% revenue/assets, ½–1% equity) — judgement, adjust for users' needs.
  • Performance materiality (lower) to allow for aggregation risk; specific materiality for sensitive items (directors' remuneration, related parties); clearly trivial threshold.

Understanding the entity & risk assessment (ISA 315)

  • Understand the entity & environment, the framework, and the system of internal control → identify Risks of Material Misstatement (RoMM) at the overall and assertion levels.
  • Significant risks need special audit attention (complex estimates, revenue recognition — presumed fraud risk, related parties, unusual/large non-routine transactions).
RoMM at the overall financial-statement level → response (ISA 330)Weak control environment / management integrity / going-concern / fraud incentives → respond with: more senior staff & supervision, heightened skepticism, unpredictability, lower materiality, more year-end & substantive testing, less reliance on controls & on management representations.

6 · Fraud (ISA 240), laws & regs (ISA 250), NOCLAR

Fraud — ISA 240

  • Fraud triangle: Incentive/pressure · Opportunity · Rationalisation.
  • Two types: fraudulent financial reporting (management override, manipulation) & misappropriation of assets (theft).
  • Auditor: maintain skepticism, presume revenue recognition a fraud risk, test journal entries, review estimates for bias, address management override.

NOCLAR (CPC s360) — steps

  • Obtain understanding → discuss with management/TCWG → request they correct/disclose/deter → assess response → escalate (TCWG, modify opinion, withdraw) → consider disclosure to authority → document.
Reportable Irregularity — APA s45 (auditors only — mandatory)An unlawful act/omission by management that ① caused or is likely to cause material financial loss, OR ② is fraudulent/theft, OR ③ is a material breach of fiduciary duty — during the audit. Report to IRBA within 3 days (written), discuss with management, then a final report within 30 days. (CAs without an audit use NOCLAR, not RI.)

7 · Internal control & tests of controls

  • 5 components (COSO): Control environment · Risk assessment · Control activities · Information & communication · Monitoring.
  • Test controls only if you intend to rely on them (and where substantive alone is insufficient). Tests: inspect, observe, enquire, re-perform; test operating effectiveness over the period.
  • Deficiency = a control missing or not operating; significant deficiency → communicate to TCWG in writing (ISA 265).
Control-deficiency triggersno segregation of duties, owner-managed/override, manual/Excel systems, no authorisation, no reconciliations, weak IT access controls, no independent review — each → identify the risk it creates & recommend the control + a substantive response.

8 · Audit evidence & assertions (ISA 500, 505, 530)

  • Sufficient (quantity) & appropriate (quality = relevance + reliability). Reliability hierarchy: auditor-generated > third-party direct > third-party via client > client external > client internal > oral.
  • Procedures — "AEIOU + CR": Analytical · Enquiry · Inspection · Observation · recalcUlation/re-performance · Confirmation (external, ISA 505).
  • Sampling (ISA 530): representative selection; project misstatements; statistical vs judgemental.

9 · Responding to risk — substantive procedures (ISA 330)

  • Substantive procedures are compulsory for each material class/balance (tests of detail + substantive analytical procedures), regardless of control reliance; and a substantive response to every significant risk.
  • Formula for a procedure (4 ingredients): [action verb] the [source document] and [agree/trace/recalculate] to the [target] to verify the [assertion] of [account].
  • Answer skeleton ("CARDS") for any balance: Cast/re-perform schedule · Agree to GL/TB/AFS · Rep letter · Disclosure note · opening balance to PY; then assertion-driven tests of detail + analytics.

11 · Completion & the audit report (ISA 700/701/705/706)

The opinion decision tree

SituationMaterial but NOT pervasiveMaterial AND pervasive
Misstatement (disagreement)Qualified — "except for"Adverse
Scope limitation (insufficient evidence)Qualified — "except for"Disclaimer
  • Pervasive = not confined to specific elements, OR confined but represents a substantial portion, OR (disclosures) fundamental to users' understanding.
  • EOM (ISA 706): draws attention to a matter properly disclosed (e.g. going-concern uncertainty, a subsequent event) — opinion not modified. Other Matter: a matter not in the AFS but relevant to users.
  • KAM (ISA 701): for listed entities — matters of most significance; describe the matter & how the audit addressed it (doesn't modify the opinion).
  • Report includes: opinion, basis for opinion, going concern, KAM, responsibilities of management & auditor.
Report decision stepsIs there a problem (misstatement or scope limitation)? → Material? → Pervasive? → pick the opinion. If properly disclosed (no misstatement) but important → EOM. Always state the reason and the effect/amount.

12 · Companies Act, governance & assurance landscape

  • Who must be audited? Public & state-owned companies; companies whose PIS ≥ 350; companies holding assets in a fiduciary capacity > R5m; or per MOI. Others may need an independent review (PIS 100–349 if not internally compiled) or be exempt (owner-managed).
  • Directors' duties: s75 (disclose personal financial interest, recuse), s76 (good faith, care/skill/diligence, business judgment rule), s77 (personal liability).
  • King IV: apply and explain; 17 principles; outcomes = ethical culture, good performance, effective control, legitimacy.
  • Audit committee (s94 / King IV): independent non-execs; oversee external audit (appointment, fees, independence), internal audit, financial reporting, risk.
  • Internal audit (King IV): provides assurance on governance, risk & control; external auditor may use its work (ISA 610) after assessing objectivity & competence.

13 · Exam technique & trigger map

Discussion-answer method: ① identify the issue & the ISA/Act → ② state the requirement/principle → ③ apply to the scenario facts → ④ conclude → ⑤ state the action/effect. ~1 mark per applied point; use headings; never just dump theory. For "procedures" use the 4-ingredient formula; for risks use RoMM → response; for ethics use threat → safeguard.
