IFRS 15 — Transfer of Control to the Customer

Step 5: recognise revenue when (or as) control transfers · practical examples · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden
What "control" means (IFRS 15.33): the ability to direct the use of, and obtain substantially all the remaining benefits from, the asset — including the ability to prevent others from doing so. Revenue follows control, not risks & rewards alone (that's only one indicator). First ask: does control transfer over time? If none of the three over-time criteria are met → it transfers at a point in time.

A · Control transfers OVER TIME — if ANY one criterion is met (IFRS 15.35)

#CriterionPlain meaning
1Customer simultaneously receives & consumes the benefits as the entity performsRoutine/repetitive services — if you stopped, another supplier wouldn't redo the work done so far.
2Performance creates/enhances an asset the customer controls as it is createdYou build on the customer's asset/land — they own each brick as it's laid.
3Asset has no alternative use to the entity AND there is an enforceable right to payment for work done to date (incl. a profit margin)Custom item you can't sell to anyone else, and you can demand payment for progress if the customer cancels.

Example A1 — criterion 1 (services consumed as performed)

A security company guards a site for 12 months for R240 000. The customer receives & consumes the protection daily; if the contract ended, the work to date wouldn't be re-performed.
→ Control transfers over time. Recognise R20 000/month (output method — time elapsed).

Example A2 — criterion 2 (building on the customer's land)

A contractor builds a warehouse on land the customer owns. As construction proceeds, the customer controls the work-in-progress (it's attached to their land).
→ Over time. Measure progress (e.g. cost-to-cost: costs incurred ÷ total expected costs) and recognise revenue on that %.

Example A3 — criterion 3 (no alternative use + right to payment)

An engineering firm builds a custom turbine to a client's unique specifications (can't be sold to anyone else); the contract entitles the firm to payment for work completed plus margin if the client cancels.
→ Over time (no alternative use AND enforceable right to payment).
Variant: if the turbine is a standard model the firm could sell to others, OR there's no right to payment for progress → fails criterion 3 → point in time on delivery/acceptance.

Example A4 — SaaS / subscription

A cloud-software subscription gives access for a year. The customer benefits continuously as access is provided.
→ Over time, straight-line over the access period (it's a "right to access", not a one-off "right to use").

B · Control transfers at a POINT IN TIME — assess the 5 indicators (IFRS 15.38)

When no over-time criterion is met, control passes at the moment the customer obtains control. Indicators (no single one is decisive — weigh them):

  1. Present right to payment for the asset.
  2. Legal title has passed to the customer.
  3. Physical possession has transferred.
  4. Significant risks & rewards of ownership have passed.
  5. Customer has accepted the asset.

Example B1 — retail / over-the-counter sale

A customer buys a TV in a store and pays at the till.
→ All indicators met at the till (payment, possession, title, risks, acceptance) → recognise revenue at the point of sale.

Example B2 — delivery terms (FOB shipping point vs destination)

A manufacturer sells goods to a retailer.
  • FOB shipping point: title & risk pass when goods leave the factory → control transfers on shipment → revenue at dispatch.
  • FOB destination / "delivered": risk stays with the seller until the goods arrive → control transfers on delivery → revenue when received by the customer.
→ The delivery term decides the date control (and revenue) transfers.

Example B3 — machine requiring installation & acceptance

A supplier sells a specialised machine that only works after the supplier installs & tests it, with a customer-acceptance clause that is substantive (not a formality).
→ Control transfers only on acceptance after installation — not on delivery. If acceptance were a mere formality, control could pass on delivery.

C · Tricky control situations (frequently examined)

C1 — Consignment stock (no transfer to the dealer)

A manufacturer ships goods to a dealer to hold on consignment; the dealer can return unsold goods and the manufacturer can recall them; the dealer pays only when it sells to an end customer.
→ The dealer does NOT control the goods (manufacturer still directs them & bears the risk) → no revenue on delivery to the dealer; recognise only when the dealer sells to the end customer.

C2 — Bill-and-hold (sold but not yet shipped)

A customer buys goods but asks the seller to hold them in its warehouse until the customer's site is ready.
→ Control can transfer before shipment IF: there's a substantive reason (customer requested it), the goods are separately identified as the customer's, they are ready for transfer, and the seller cannot use them or sell them to anyone else. If met → recognise revenue now (the warehousing may be a separate service).

C3 — Repurchase agreement (control may not transfer)

An entity sells an asset but has a call option to buy it back (or an obligation — a forward).
→ The customer is limited in directing/using the asset → control has not transferred. Account for it as a lease (if repurchase price < original) or a financing arrangement (if ≥ original) — not a sale. (A customer-held put option is assessed separately.)

C4 — Right of return

A retailer sells goods that customers may return within 30 days.
→ Control transfers on sale, but recognise revenue only for the amount the entity expects to be entitled to: recognise revenue + a refund liability for expected returns, and an asset for the right to recover the returned goods.

C5 — Principal vs agent (who controls the good first?)

An online platform sells third-party products.
→ If the platform controls the good before transfer (inventory/pricing/fulfilment risk) it's a principal → recognise gross revenue. If it merely arranges the sale, it's an agent → recognise only the net commission.
Exam approach for any "when is revenue recognised" question① Identify the performance obligation → ② test the 3 over-time criteria (any one met → over time; pick a progress measure) → ③ if none met → point in time: weigh the 5 indicators to pin the date → ④ watch the traps (consignment, bill-and-hold, repurchase, right of return, agent) → ⑤ conclude with the date/period and the amount. Always say "control transfers when…" and justify with the criterion/indicator.
