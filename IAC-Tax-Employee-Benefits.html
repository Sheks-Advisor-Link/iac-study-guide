Employee Benefits — Tax Consequences Made Simple

How to spot, calculate & discuss employment income · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden · from the Milpark/PGDA Worked Examples & Lesson Slides
The whole section in one idea: everything an employer gives an employee is taxed — the only questions are (1) which "bucket" is it, and (2) what amount goes into the employee's income. Master the 4 buckets and the 3-step fringe-benefit method below and you can handle any scenario.

1 · The big picture — 4 buckets of employment income ("SAFS")

BucketWhat it isHow it's taxed
S — Salary/wagesCash for services (salary, bonus, commission, leave pay)Full amount in gross income (par (c) / (i)). Bonuses & commission on a payment basis (s7B).
A — AllowancesCash given up-front to spend on the employer's behalf (travel, subsistence, other) — s8(1)Include it, then reduce it by the qualifying (business) portion. Net private portion is taxed.
F — Fringe benefitsNon-cash benefits (car, house, cheap loan, free asset) — 7th ScheduleCompute the cash equivalent value → include in gross income via paragraph (i).
S — Share incentivesShares/options from employment — s8CGain taxed as remuneration on vesting (not on grant).
Easy way to remember"SAFS" = Salary, Allowances, Fringe benefits, Share schemes. Salary & the taxable bits of A/F/S all end up in gross income, then flow through the normal tax calc (less deductions, less rebates/credits).

2 · The universal 3-step method for ANY fringe benefit ("T–V–C")

  1. T — is it a Taxable benefit? Identify which paragraph 2 of the 7th Schedule it falls under (2(a) free asset, 2(b) use of asset/car, 2(d) accommodation, 2(f) cheap loan, 2(i) medical, etc.).
  2. V — does it have Value? Check the "no-value" rule for that paragraph (e.g. bravery/long-service awards, business-use assets, expat accommodation ≤2 years). If a no-value rule applies → nothing taxed.
  3. C — Calculate the Cash equivalent using that paragraph's valuation rule, then less any consideration paid by the employee. Include the result in gross income (par (i)).
Always write these 3 stepsEven if a step is "yes, no no-value rule applies", say it — each step is a mark, and it stops you jumping straight to a number and missing the no-value escape.

3 · Allowances (s8(1)) — "include it, then reduce it"

3.1 Travel allowance — s8(1)(b)

  • The allowance is included; reduce it by the business portion (business km ÷ total km). Need a logbook.
  • Two reduction methods — pick the one giving the bigger reduction: deemed cost (fixed + fuel + maintenance per the SARS tables) or actual cost (only if records kept). Vehicle cost capped at R800 000 for wear-and-tear & finance.
  • 80% of the allowance goes through PAYE (20% if >80% business use).

Example (WE6) — deemed vs actual, pick the bigger reduction

Car R880k (capped R800k), allowance R6 000/m from 1 Nov (4 months → R24 000), 20 000 km total, 6 000 business.

Actual-cost reduction (W&T R800k/7 + finance + running) × 6 000/20 000R21 905
Deemed-cost reduction R6.94/km × 6 000 kmR41 640
Elect deemed (bigger); reduction limited to the allowance R24 000(R24 000)
Taxable travel allowanceR0
How the calc worksWork out both reductions, choose the larger, but you can never reduce by more than the allowance received. Here the business use wipes it out entirely.
Trap (WE7): if an employee leases a car and subleases it to the employer for a right of use, the rental from the employer is deemed a travel allowance (s8(1)(b)(iv)) — no separate company-car fringe benefit, and the rental expense is not deductible.

3.2 Subsistence allowance — s8(1)(c)

  • For being away from home ≥1 night on business. Reduce by: actual accommodation cost + the higher of deemed or actual meals & incidentals (deemed amounts read off the rate card).

Example (WE8)

3.3 Other allowances — s8(1)(a)

  • Clothing, cellphone, entertainment, daily meal allowances etc. Reduce only by amounts the employee actually spent AND accounted for (or refunded) — capped at the deemed amount where one exists.

Example (WE10) — daily meal allowance, capped at deemed

Allowance (R175 × 10 trips)R1 750
Less reduction: actual R250/day but capped at deemed R161/day × 10(R1 610)
TaxableR140
Key pointYou can only reduce by what you spent and proved — and never more than the deemed daily cap. If the employee doesn't account to the employer, no reduction (WE9).

The motor-vehicle "determined value" (key for 2(b) cars)

Determined value = retail MV when first granted, LESS 15% reducing-balance depreciation for each completed 12 months between the employer's purchase and the grant of use.

Then reduce the value of private use by: business-km portion (business ÷ total km), and private running costs the employee bore (private km × deemed fuel/km; licence/insurance/maintenance × private ÷ total).

Example (WE15) — company car

Vehicle R460 000 (incl. maintenance plan → 3.25%), 25 000 km total, 18 000 business; employee pays fuel (deemed fuel 183.8c/km).

Value of private use: 3.25% × R460 000 × 12R179 400
Less business-use reduction: 18 000/25 000 × R179 400(R129 168)
Less private fuel (employee-borne): 7 000 km × 183.8c(R12 866)
Cash equivalentR37 366
How the calc worksStart with 3.25% × value × 12 = the full private-use value. Knock off the business portion (you're not taxed for working), then knock off running costs the employee paid out of their own pocket.

Laptop cost R8 000 (MV R7 500), used 1 Aug–31 Jan (184 days), employee paid R200; not mainly for business.

Example (WE13) — residential accommodation, 2(d)

Employer rents a flat (R15 000/m for 6 months); employee earns R750 000. Value = lower of cost or the formula.

Cost to employer: R15 000 × 6R90 000
Formula (A−B)×C/100×D/12 = (750 000 − 95 750) × 19/100 × 6/12R62 154
Cash equivalent = lower of the twoR62 154
The formula in wordsA = remuneration proxy (≈ last year's remuneration) · B = the abatement (the tax threshold, R95 750) · C = 17/18/19 (higher if employer also supplies furniture & power) · D = months. Read B & C off the rate card.
Expat no-value (WE14): accommodation to an expat for ≤2 years has no value — except to the extent it exceeds R25 000/month. So R35 000/m → tax only (35 000 − 25 000) × months.

Example (WE17) — low-interest loan vs subsidy, 2(f)

Employee borrows R2 500 000 at 7%; employer pays 3% of the interest; employee pays the rest.

(a) Official rate 6% (employer 3% + employee 4% = 7% > 6%) → it's a subsidy: employer's 3% × R2.5mR75 000
(b) Official rate 8%: interest @ official (8% × 2.5m) R200 000 − employee's 4% (R100 000)R100 000
How to read itIf the total rate the employee effectively pays is below the official rate → low-interest loan benefit = (official − employee's rate) × loan. If the employer pays part of the interest, that subsidy is itself a benefit. Official rate = repo + 1%.

Example (WE19) — medical-aid contributions, 2(i)

  • Employer pays R3 500/m → a fringe benefit in the employee's income (par 12A).
  • BUT it's deemed paid by the employee, so he also gets the s6A medical credit (e.g. (R728 + R246) × 12) against his tax. Net effect is small — but show both.

5 · Share incentives — s8C (tax on vesting)

  • A restricted equity instrument (can't be freely sold for a period) is not taxed on grant or exercise — only when it vests (the restriction lifts).
  • On vesting: gain = market value on vesting − consideration paid → taxed as remuneration (gross income par (n)).
  • Any earlier gross-income inclusion is deferred by s10(1)(nD) until vesting. After vesting, the shares are normal CGT assets with base cost = MV on vesting.

Example (WE22) — option over 1 000 shares

Y1 grant: (MV 15 − paid 10) × 1 000 = 5 000, but exempt s10(1)(nD) (not vested)R0
Y3 exercise: restriction still on → not a vesting eventR0
Y6 vesting (restriction lifts): (MV 48 − paid 10) × 1 000 = remunerationR38 000
Y8 sale: CGT proceeds (60 × 1 000) − base cost (48 × 1 000) = capital gainR12 000
Timeline trickNothing happens until vesting — that's when s8C fires (income). After that it's a normal share: future growth is CGT, using the vesting MV as base cost.

6 · VAT on fringe benefits (employer side)

  • The employer accounts output VAT on the cash equivalent of most fringe benefits (× tax fraction 15/115), because granting the benefit is a deemed supply.
  • Motor-vehicle right of use: input was denied on purchase (s17(2)(c)), so output uses the special formula (0.3% × determined value × …) × 15/115; without a maintenance plan use 0.6%.
  • No VAT on cash salary/allowances (employment isn't an enterprise). Long-service asset: claim input on cost, output on the taxable cash-equivalent.

7 · Exam technique & the triggers to spot

Technique: ① classify the item into a bucket (SAFS) → ② for a fringe benefit run T-V-C (paragraph → no-value rule → cash equivalent) → ③ show the formula & workings (own-figure marks) → ④ less consideration paid → ⑤ state the gross-income inclusion (par (i)/(n)) and any linked credit (s6A) or VAT. One applied point = one mark; always name the paragraph.
