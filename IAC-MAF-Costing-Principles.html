Costing Principles — In-Depth Exam Guide

Calculation frameworks + the reasoning (what's in/out & why) + discussion-question bank + strategy-question triggers · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden
The golden rule of costing: the "right" cost depends on the purpose. For inventory/external reporting → full (absorption) cost. For decisions → only relevant costs (future, incremental, cash) incl. opportunity cost. For control → standard vs actual (variances). Always ask "what is this number for?" before you calculate.

Avoidable vs unavoidableOnly avoidable costs are relevant to a shutdown/discontinue decision.
ControllableFor performance evaluation — assess managers only on what they control.
PART B · COSTING SYSTEMS

B1 · Absorption vs marginal (variable) costing

  • Absorption: product cost = direct materials + direct labour + variable OH + fixed production OH absorbed (via the OAR). Fixed OH sits in inventory → profit moves with inventory levels. Required for IFRS/IAS 2.
  • Marginal: product cost = variable cost only; fixed OH = a period cost. Contribution = sales − variable cost. Better for decisions (no fixed-cost distortion).
OAR = budgeted overhead ÷ budgeted activity (units / labour hrs / machine hrs) Over/(under)-absorbed OH = absorbed (actual activity × OAR) − actual overhead Profit difference (absorption − marginal) = change in inventory units × fixed OH per unit
Why & whenProduction > sales (inventory rises) → absorption profit > marginal (fixed OH deferred in stock). The two reconcile by the fixed-OH in the inventory change. For a decision, use marginal (absorption can wrongly make a loss-making product look profitable, or defer fixed costs).

B2 · Activity-based costing (ABC)

  • Trace overheads to activities → assign to products via cost drivers (set-ups, orders, inspections), not a single volume base.
  • Steps: identify activities → pool costs → choose a driver → driver rate (pool ÷ driver volume) → assign to products.
  • Use when overheads are large & diverse and products differ in volume/complexity → more accurate cost & pricing; cons: costly/complex, driver choice subjective.

B3 · Job / batch / process & throughput

  • Job/batch: accumulate cost per job/batch; OAR for overheads.
  • Process costing: mass production → equivalent units. Cost per EU = costs ÷ EUs; value finished & WIP. Normal loss = expected (cost absorbed by good units; scrap value reduces cost); abnormal loss/gain = unexpected → valued at full cost to P/L. Choose FIFO or weighted average. Joint products: split joint cost by sales value/units at split-off; by-products net off.
  • Throughput accounting (TOC): only material is variable → maximise throughput (sales − material) per bottleneck hour; rank products by that; elevate the constraint.

PART D · RELEVANT COSTING FOR DECISIONS — the reasoning

D1 · What's IN and what's OUT (and why)

Include (relevant)Exclude (irrelevant)
Future incremental cash flows; opportunity costs; avoidable fixed costs; incremental working capital.Sunk costs (already spent); committed costs; allocated/apportioned fixed OH (unless avoidable); depreciation & notional/​non-cash charges; past R&D.

Relevant cost of materials

  • In regular use (held in stock): use replacement cost — using it now means you must replace it.
  • Not in regular use (in stock): use the higher of its scrap/realisable value or its value in an alternative use (the opportunity cost given up).
  • Not in stock: the purchase price.

Relevant cost of labour

  • Spare capacity: only the incremental cost (nil if idle time is already paid; otherwise the extra hours).
  • Full capacity: labour cost + contribution forgone on the work displaced (opportunity cost).

D2 · The decision frameworks

DecisionFramework + qualitative factors
Make vs buy / outsourceCompare incremental cost to make (variable + avoidable fixed) vs buy-in price. Qual: quality, reliability, capacity freed, supplier dependence, control of IP, staff, strategic importance, confidentiality.
Special / one-off orderAccept if price > incremental cost AND spare capacity AND it won't cannibalise normal sales or undercut existing customers; if no spare capacity, add the opportunity cost.
Shutdown / discontinueCompare contribution lost vs avoidable fixed costs saved; consider knock-on effects, redundancy costs, reversibility, reputation.
Limiting factor / product mixMaximise contribution (or throughput) per unit of the scarce resource; overlay qualitative (key customers, minimum supply).
Joint products — process further?Process further only if incremental revenue > incremental further-processing cost; ignore the joint costs (already incurred at split-off).

A customer offers R250 000 for a one-off contract. Should it be accepted? Below is the relevant-cost statement with a reason for every line (the reason is where the marks are).

Memory hook for the reasons: materials = regular use→replacement / not regular→higher of scrap or alt-use / not held→buy price; labour = spare→nil / full→wage+contribution forgone; always exclude sunk, committed, allocated & depreciation, always include incremental cash & opportunity costs.
PART E · STANDARD COSTING & VARIANCES
VarianceFormula
Material price(std price − actual price) × actual qty purchased
Material usage(std qty for actual output − actual qty used) × std price
Material mix / yieldMix: (actual qty in std mix − actual qty in actual mix) × std price · Yield: (std input for output − actual total input) × std cost
Labour rate(std rate − actual rate) × actual hours paid
Labour efficiency(std hrs for output − actual hrs worked) × std rate; idle time = idle hrs × std rate
Variable OH exp / effExp: (std rate − actual rate) × actual hrs · Eff: (std hrs − actual hrs) × std rate
Fixed OH exp / volumeExp: budget − actual FOH · Volume (absorption only): (actual − budget output) × FOAR (= capacity + efficiency)
Sales price / volumePrice: (actual − std price) × actual units · Volume: (actual − budget units) × std contribution (marginal) or std profit (absorption); split into mix & quantity
Interpreting variances (discussion)Investigate by size/%, controllability, trend, interdependence (cheaper material → adverse usage/quality; a rushed job → adverse efficiency) and whether the standard was realistic. Planning variance = caused by a flawed original standard (uncontrollable); operational variance = what management could control — split them for a fair evaluation.
PART F · BUDGETING
  • Types: functional (sales, production, materials, labour, OH) → master budget; cash budget (receipts/payments timing — liquidity); flexed budget (flex to actual activity for fair comparison).
  • Approaches: incremental (last year ± %, embeds slack) vs zero-based (justify from zero); rolling (always current); activity-based budgeting; beyond budgeting.
  • Behavioural: participative (motivation but slack) vs imposed (fast but demotivating); set realistic, attainable targets; controllability.
PART G · PRICING & TRANSFER PRICING

Pricing strategies

  • Cost-plus (simple, covers cost; ignores demand/competition).
  • Market/value-based (price to value; maximises profit, needs market data).
  • Penetration (low price to win share); skimming (high price to early adopters); price discrimination (different segments, needs separable markets/no resale).
  • Target costing (price − margin = target cost → design to it); life-cycle pricing.

Transfer pricing

  • Aim: goal congruence, fair divisional evaluation, autonomy.
  • Methods: market-based (best if a market exists), cost-based (full/variable ± markup), negotiated.
  • Rule: min = variable/marginal cost + opportunity cost to the seller; max = lowest external buy price.
  • Watch divisional motivation & cross-border tax (s31).

G2 · Transfer pricing — in depth (worked example + discussion)

GENERAL RULE: minimum (seller) = marginal/variable cost + opportunity cost maximum (buyer) = lower of the external buy-in price & the buyer's net marginal revenue A transfer is worthwhile for the GROUP only if: seller's marginal cost + opportunity cost < external buy price.

Scenario. Division A makes component X (variable cost R60, fixed R20/unit, external selling price R100). Division B needs X to make product Y and can buy X externally for R95.

CaseMinimum (Division A)Maximum (Division B)Outcome & reason
A has SPARE capacity= variable cost only = R60 (no sale given up → no opportunity cost)= external price R95Range R60–R95. Transfer internally — A's R60 marginal cost < R95 external. Group saves R95 − R60 = R35/unit. Negotiate within the range (e.g. R75) so both divisions show a profit.
A at FULL capacity (sells all externally @ R100)= variable cost R60 + opportunity cost (contribution forgone R100 − R60 = R40) = R100= external price R95No deal: minimum R100 > maximum R95 → do not transfer. A sells outside @ R100; B buys outside @ R95 — best for the group. Forcing a R100 transfer would make Y unprofitable (dysfunctional).
Why the opportunity cost flips the answerWith spare capacity A loses nothing by supplying B, so the floor is just its marginal cost (R60). At full capacity, supplying B means giving up a R100 external sale, so the floor rises to the market price (R100) — and internal supply is only sensible if B's value exceeds that. This is the single most-tested transfer-pricing idea.

Detailed discussion points (for "advise on the transfer price")

  • Three objectives (always state these): ① goal congruence (divisions act in the group's best interest); ② fair performance evaluation & motivation; ③ divisional autonomy (managers free to buy/sell as they choose). These often conflict — a price good for one division can harm the group.
  • Market-based price — best when a competitive external market exists: objective, encourages efficiency, preserves autonomy. Cons: a reliable market price may not exist / be volatile; the external price includes selling & distribution costs not incurred on internal sales → use an adjusted (arm's-length) market price.
  • Cost-based price — Variable/marginal cost: leads to correct short-run group decisions, but the supplier earns no contribution → demotivating & distorts its ROI/RI. Full cost: risks passing on inefficiency and the fixed-cost element distorts the buyer's decisions (treats a group-fixed cost as variable). Cost-plus: gives the supplier a margin but the mark-up is arbitrary & can cause sub-optimal buying. Use standard (not actual) cost so inefficiencies aren't transferred.
  • Negotiated price — respects autonomy & motivation, but depends on relative bargaining power, is time-consuming, may not be group-optimal, and can breed conflict; needs a dispute-resolution mechanism (head-office arbitration — which itself erodes autonomy).
  • Dual / two-part pricing — credit the supplier at one price (e.g. market) and charge the buyer another (e.g. marginal cost), or a fixed periodic fee + marginal cost → can achieve goal congruence & fair evaluation, but is complex & can hide inefficiency.
  • Capacity matters — the right price changes with spare vs full capacity (opportunity cost), as shown above; re-assess if capacity changes.
  • Performance-evaluation distortion — the transfer price shifts profit between divisions, so it directly affects each division's ROI/RI/EVA; apply the controllability principle and don't penalise a manager for a price imposed on them.
  • International transfer pricing (high-yield): groups may set prices to shift profit to low-tax jurisdictions — but SARS s31 requires an arm's-length price (OECD guidelines), with a primary adjustment + a 20% secondary (deemed dividend); also consider customs duties, exchange controls, double taxation & documentation. Tax-driven prices can conflict with good internal performance measurement.
  • Conclusion: recommend a price (often market-based if a market exists, else a negotiated price within the min–max range) that best balances goal congruence, fair evaluation & autonomy — and note the capacity & tax considerations.
PART H · DECISION-MAKING UNDER RISK & UNCERTAINTY
  • Sensitivity: how far an input can move before the decision flips → the critical variable.
  • Expected value = Σ(probability × outcome) — good for repeated decisions; ignores risk attitude & one-off impact.
  • Decision trees (sequential decisions, roll back); simulation (many variables).
  • Value of perfect/imperfect information = the increase in expected value from better decisions.
  • Risk attitude: maximin (cautious), maximax (optimist), minimax regret.

PART J · DISCUSSION-QUESTION BANK (what can be asked + how)
  • "Critically evaluate the costing system" → absorption vs ABC: is the current base distorting product costs? recommend ABC if overheads large/diverse (give pros/cons).
  • "Why do absorption & marginal profit differ?" → fixed OH in inventory; reconcile; implications for decisions & performance.
  • "Should we make/buy / accept the order / close X?" → relevant-costing answer + qualitative factors + recommendation.
  • "Discuss the variances / their causes" → size, controllability, interdependence, planning vs operational, realistic standard.
  • "Evaluate the budgeting process / behaviour" → participative vs imposed, slack, flexed vs fixed, ZBB/rolling/beyond budgeting.
  • "Advise on the transfer price / pricing strategy" → goal congruence / min-max rule; cost-plus vs value-based; penetration vs skimming.
  • "Is the performance measure appropriate?" → ROI vs RI/EVA (dysfunctional behaviour), balanced scorecard, controllability, non-financial & ESG.
  • "Limitations of CVP / standard costing / EOQ" → state the assumptions & why they break in the scenario.
  • "Throughput / bottleneck" → rank by throughput per bottleneck hour; how to elevate the constraint.
  • Modern environment → why target/life-cycle/kaizen/quality/JIT costing suit the scenario; criticisms of traditional standard costing.
How to answer a costing discussion① state the principle/technique → ② apply it to the scenario facts/numbers → ③ give both advantages & limitations → ④ recommend & conclude. Bring a number in to support the words.
PART K · STRATEGY QUESTIONS — triggers & the points to hit

K1 · How to recognise a strategy question (triggers)

Scenario triggers → "should the company invest / expand / enter a new market / acquire / close?"; "critically evaluate the decision / strategy"; "does it align with the strategic objectives?"; a change in the macro environment (interest rates, regulation, technology, competitors); sustainability/ESG goals; a new product, store, division or contract.

K2 · The frameworks to apply (pick what fits)

Porter's 5 ForcesIndustry attractiveness: rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer & supplier power → can the firm earn returns here?

6 Capitals / integrated thinkingImpact on financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social & relationship, natural capital → long-term value & ESG.

K3 · The main points to TOUCH ON in every strategy answer

  1. Strategic fit — does it align with the company's mission, objectives & core competencies? (state the objective, then test the decision against it.)
  2. Competitive advantage — does it create or protect a sustainable advantage (cost or differentiation)? can rivals copy it?
  3. Financial viability — returns vs cost: NPV/IRR, payback, contribution, affordability & funding (link to the numbers).
  4. Risk — business, financial, execution/integration, market, regulatory, reputational; sensitivity of the assumptions.
  5. Resources & capabilities — does the firm have the people, capacity, technology & funding to execute?
  6. Stakeholders — employees, customers, suppliers, community, shareholders, regulators — who is affected & how?
  7. Opportunity cost & alternatives — what else could the resources do? is there a better option?
  8. Sustainability & the long term — 6 capitals, ESG, reputation, integrated/triple-context value over time.
  9. Conclude with a clear recommendation — weigh financial + qualitative + risk → decide.
Strategy-answer formula"This decision is [aligned/not aligned] with the objective of [X] because [scenario fact]; it [creates/erodes] competitive advantage; financially it [adds/destroys] value (NPV/return); the key risks are […]; on balance I recommend […]." Mark-earners: link every point to a specific fact/number, give both sides, cover non-financial & sustainability, and conclude.
PART L · EXAM TECHNIQUE (costing)
  • Identify the purpose first → inventory (absorption) / decision (relevant) / control (variances) → that picks the costing approach.
  • Calcs: vertical layout, label each line, show workings (own-figure marks), state assumptions; for relevant costing add a one-line reason per item (why included/excluded) — these earn marks.
  • Discussions: principle → apply → both sides → recommend; bring numbers in.
  • Don't include sunk/allocated/depreciation in a decision; don't forget opportunity cost & qualitative factors.
