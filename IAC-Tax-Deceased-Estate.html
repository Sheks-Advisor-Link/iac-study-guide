Death of a Natural Person — CGT · Estate Duty · Donations Tax · Trusts

Structure, calculation formats & worked examples · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden  |  CGT inclusion 40% · estate duty 20%/25% · donations tax 20%/25%
The big picture — death triggers up to 3 separate taxes & 3 taxpayers. ① The deceased (final income-tax return to date of death, incl. CGT on a deemed disposal s9HA). ② The deceased estate (a separate taxpayer for income/gains after death — s25). ③ Estate duty on the dutiable estate. Donations tax & trusts are the lifetime estate-planning tools that shrink what's taxed at death. Do the calcs in order: CGT → final income tax → estate duty (the income-tax/CGT debt is then deductible for estate duty).

1 · CGT on death — s9HA (deemed disposal)

  • The deceased is deemed to dispose of all assets at market value on the date of death (a deemed disposal to the estate).
  • Exceptions (no CGT): ① assets bequeathed to the surviving spouse → roll-over (s9HB): deemed disposed at base cost, the spouse inherits the base cost (tax deferred to second death); ② cash/SA currency; ③ retirement-fund interests & qualifying long-term insurance proceeds.
  • Annual exclusion in the year of death = R300 000 (instead of the usual R40 000).
  • Primary residence: the R2 000 000 gain exclusion still applies to the deemed disposal of the home.
  • The deceased estate acquires the assets at market value (= its base cost); when the estate later sells/distributes, the estate computes its own CGT (R40 000 annual exclusion, individual rates).

Format — deceased's CGT on death

Per asset: Proceeds (= market value at death) − base cost = capital gain/(loss)x
(exclude assets to spouse — roll-over; exclude cash)—
Primary residence: gain − R2 000 000 exclusionx
Sum of capital gains & lossesx
Less: annual exclusion (year of death)(300 000)
= Net capital gain × 40% inclusionx
= Taxable capital gain → into the final return (taxed at individual rates)x

2 · Income tax — the deceased & the deceased estate

  • Deceased (final return): all income received/accrued to the date of death, plus the taxable capital gain above; taxed at individual rates with rebates apportioned for the part-year.
  • Deceased estate (s25): a separate "natural person" taxpayer for income & gains arising after death until assets are distributed/an heir is ascertained; taxed at individual rates. (The estate generally does not qualify for the s6 rebates or the interest exemption — confirm against the Act.)
  • Once income accrues to an ascertained heir/legatee, it is taxed in the heir's hands.

3 · Estate duty (Estate Duty Act)

Format — the estate-duty calculation

Property (s3(2)) — all the deceased's property at death (immovable & movable; worldwide if ordinarily resident), at market valuex
+ Deemed property (s3(3)) — domestic life-insurance policy proceeds on the deceased's life; donationes mortis causa; an accrual claim in the estate's favour; property the deceased was competent to dispose ofx
= Gross value of the estatex
− Deductions (s4): funeral & deathbed expenses; debts due at death (incl. the deceased's final income-tax/CGT liability); administration costs (executor's & Master's fees); bequests to PBOs (s4(h)); property to the surviving spouse — s4(q), unlimited(x)
= Net value of the estatex
− s4A abatement (portable to spouse → up to R7m)(3 500 000)
= Dutiable amountx
× 20% (first R30m) / 25% (above R30m)
= Estate dutyx
Who paysThe executor pays the duty out of the estate, but s11/s13 apportions some of it to recipients of deemed property (e.g. a policy beneficiary bears the duty on their policy). The s4A abatement not used by the first-dying spouse rolls over to the survivor.

4 · Donations tax (the lifetime estate-planning tool)

  • 20% (cumulative donations ≤ R30m) / 25% (> R30m) on the value of property donated by a resident; donor is liable (payable by the end of the month after the donation).
  • Exemptions: donations to a spouse (unlimited, s56(1)(a)); the annual R100 000 (natural persons); bona-fide maintenance; PBOs; donations cancelled within 6 months.
  • Donationes mortis causa (a donation in contemplation of death) are exempt from donations tax — but fall into estate duty as deemed property.
  • s58 deems a donation where property is sold for inadequate consideration; s7 / par 68–73 attribute income/gains back to the donor (while alive); s7C deems a donation on a low/interest-free loan to a trust.

Format — donations tax (per year)

Market value of property donatedx
− exempt donations (spouse, PBO, maintenance)(x)
− annual exemption (natural person)(100 000)
= Net donation × 20% (/25%)x

5 · Trusts & death (why they're an estate-planning tool)

  • Assets placed in a trust during life are NOT part of your estate at death → no estate duty on them, and no s9HA CGT on death (the trust, not you, owns them). Future growth accrues in the trust, outside your estate ("pegging").
  • But if you sold assets to the trust on a loan account, that loan is an asset in your estate (at its face value) and s7C deemed a donation each year on the foregone interest (official rate − rate charged), using the R100k exemption.
  • Trust income/CGT while you're alive: conduit principle (s25B) flows vested income to beneficiaries keeping its nature; otherwise taxed in the trust (45%; CGT inclusion 80% → effective 36%); s7 attributes income/gains from donated assets back to you (the donor) — this attribution stops at your death.

7 · Estate-planning techniques (how to reduce the taxes)

TechniqueEffect
Bequeath to spouses4(q) estate-duty deduction + s9HB CGT roll-over → defers both taxes to the second death (and the abatement is portable → up to R7m).
Use a trust ("peg" the estate)Move growth assets out of the estate now → future growth escapes estate duty & the s9HA CGT on death; watch s7C & the loan account left in the estate.
Donate during lifeR100 000 p.a. free; reduces the estate, but donations tax at 20%/25% on the excess — compare to estate duty at 20%/25%.
Life insuranceProvides liquidity to pay estate duty & CGT without forcing asset sales (but the policy is deemed property unless structured/exempt).
Buy-and-sell / keymanFunds the purchase of a deceased's business interest; can be excluded from the estate if requirements met.

8 · How to approach & discuss it in the exam

  1. State who the taxpayers are — the deceased (to date of death), the deceased estate, and (for planning) any trust; identify the taxes that arise.
  2. Do the calculations in order: ① CGT on death (s9HA) → ② the deceased's final income tax → ③ estate duty (remember the income-tax/CGT liability is a debt deductible there).
  3. For each asset, ask: to the spouse? (→ roll-over & s4(q)) · primary residence? (→ R2m) · cash? (→ no CGT) · a policy/accrual/trust asset? (→ deemed property).
  4. Apply each exclusion/deduction and quote the section (s9HA, s9HB, s4(q), s4A).
  5. Discussion marks: explain the interaction (spouse bequest defers both taxes; a trust keeps growth out of the estate; donations tax vs estate duty trade-off), then conclude/advise on the most tax-efficient structure.
