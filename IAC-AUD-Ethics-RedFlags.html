PART 1 · SAICA CODE OF PROFESSIONAL CONDUCT (CPC) — independence & the 5 fundamental principles
Behaviour / action to look out forWhat it threatensImpact & required action
Audit team / firm / immediate family holds shares or a direct or material financial interest in the clientSelf-interest Objectivity & independenceIndependence impaired — firm cannot act unless the interest is disposed of or the person removed from the team (also s90 ineligibility).
Fee dependence — one client is a large % of firm income (>15% of fees for a PIE, 2 yrs running)Self-interestIntimidation ObjectivityPressure not to upset the client → objectivity compromised. Reduce dependence; pre/post-issuance independent (EQ) review; disclose to TCWG.
Overdue / unpaid fees from a prior auditSelf-interest (acts like a loan)Independence threatened — don't issue the next report until paid; independent review.
Contingent fee for an audit / assurance engagementSelf-interest ObjectivityProhibited — outcome-linked fee destroys objectivity; do not accept.
Accepting gifts or hospitality that are more than trivial & inconsequentialSelf-interestFamiliarity Integrity & objectivityCreates obligation/bias → decline anything more than trivial.
Loan or guarantee to/from the client (not a bank on normal terms)Self-interestNot permitted unless immaterial & arm's-length; otherwise independence lost.
Auditor prepared the AFS / kept the books / did the valuation / designed the IT system now being auditedSelf-review ObjectivityAuditing your own work → prohibited for an audit client (esp. PIE); use a separate team & management must take responsibility, or don't provide the service.
Promoting or representing the client — litigation, negotiating, promoting its sharesAdvocacy ObjectivityAuditor seen to take the client's side → decline the advocacy role / separate teams / withdraw.
Long association — same partner/senior staff on the client for many yearsFamiliaritySelf-interestProfessional scepticism dulled → rotate (Companies Act s92: max 5 yrs, then 2-yr cooling-off); reassign staff.
Close or family relationship at the client (e.g. partner's spouse is the financial director); ex-partner now a directorFamiliaritySelf-interestBias risk → remove the staff member from the engagement; cooling-off for ex-partners.
Team member about to join the client, or recently served there in a finance roleSelf-reviewFamiliarityCooling-off; the person must not have influenced the audited period; remove from team.
Client threatens dismissal/replacement or litigation, or pressures the auditor to accept an incorrect treatment or cut feesIntimidation Objectivity & integrityAuditor pressured to give in → escalate to TCWG, obtain legal advice, stand firm on standards, withdraw if unresolved.
CA(SA) in business pressured to prepare or be associated with misleading informationIntegrityObjectivityMust refuse, escalate internally, and resign if pressure continues; do not sign off misleading info.
Disclosing client information to a third party without authorityConfidentialityBreach of a fundamental principle — only disclose where a legal right or duty exists (RI, NOCLAR, court order).
Accepting / continuing work the firm is not competent or resourced to do; "lowballing" an unrealistic feeProfessional competence & due careQuality of work compromised → decline, or obtain expertise/resources before proceeding.
Acting for two clients with competing interests; conflict of interest not managedObjectivityBiased service → disclose & obtain consent, use separate teams, or decline.
Becoming aware of fraud, tax evasion, bribery, pollution or a breach of law by the client/employer (NOCLAR, s360/s260)IntegrityProfessional behaviour vs ConfidentialityMust respond: understand it → discuss with management/TCWG → request rectification → consider disclosure to an authority in the public interest → document. Confidentiality does not block this.
The CPC process behind every row: Identify the threat → Evaluate its significance (the "reasonable & informed third party" test) → Address it (eliminate the circumstance, apply a safeguard, or decline/withdraw). Safeguards come from the profession/legislation, the firm, or the client (TCWG).
PART 2 · COMPANIES ACT 71 OF 2008 — director conduct, auditor eligibility & statutory duties
Behaviour / action to look out forWhat it threatens (section)Impact & required action
Proposed auditor is/was a director, prescribed officer, employee, bookkeeper/AFS-preparer or company secretary (current or prior year)s90 — auditor eligibility & independenceAuditor is disqualified / ineligible; appointment invalid. 5-year cooling-off before acting.
Same designated audit partner on the client for more than 5 consecutive yearss92 — mandatory rotationMust rotate; 2-year cooling-off; non-rotation = non-compliance & familiarity threat.
Director acts not in good faith, not for a proper purpose, or not in the company's best interests; fails to apply reasonable care, skill & diligences76 — directors' standards of conductBreach of fiduciary duty; opens the door to s77 liability.
Director has a personal financial interest in a contract and does not disclose it / does not recuses75 — personal financial interestContract may be voidable; director must account for any profit; breach of duty.
Director causes the company loss through a breach of s75/s76, fraud or a misrepresentations77 — director liabilityPersonal liability for the loss/damage caused.
Trading recklessly, with gross negligence, or while factually insolvent / with intent to defraud creditorss22 — reckless tradingCommission may order it to cease; directors personally liable; possible reportable irregularity.
Financial assistance to a director/related party or for share purchases without a special resolution & the solvency-&-liquidity tests44 / s45 — financial assistanceAssistance is void; directors who authorised it are personally liable.
Auditor identifies an unlawful act/omission by management that has caused or is likely to cause material financial loss, or is fraud, or breaches a fiduciary duty (a Reportable Irregularity)APA s45 — reportable irregularityStatutory duty: report to IRBA within 3 days, notify management, then a 30-day follow-up report. Overrides confidentiality.
Public / state-owned company has no audit committee, or members are not independent non-executivess94 — audit committeeNon-compliance & governance/oversight failure; auditor independence not properly overseen.
PART 3 · KING IV (2016) — corporate governance ("apply and explain")
Behaviour / action to look out forWhat it threatens (principle)Impact
Leadership lacking integrity, transparency, accountability, fairness or responsibility; poor ethical "tone at the top"P1 — ethical & effective leadershipErodes the ethical culture → governance failure & reputational damage.
Ethics not actively governed; no social & ethics committee where requiredP2 + Reg 43 — organisational ethicsEthics & citizenship unmanaged → compliance & stakeholder risk.
Board not independent / unbalanced; CEO is also the chair; a single dominant individualP7 — composition: balance, independence, separation of chair & CEOConcentration of power, weak challenge → poor, biased decisions & weak accountability.
Director conflicts of interest not declared or managedP1/P8 — integrity & committeesSelf-serving decisions → loss of stakeholder trust.
Remuneration not fair, responsible or transparent; pay not linked to performanceP14 — remuneration governanceMisaligned incentives, excessive pay → stakeholder backlash / negative say-on-pay vote.
Reporting incomplete, not transparent, or no integrated reportP5 — reports & disclosure build trustInformation asymmetry → loss of stakeholder & investor confidence.
Risks & opportunities not governed; technology & information not governed; compliance not governedP11 / P12 / P13Unmanaged exposure → losses, cyber/data breaches, legal penalties.
Weak or absent assurance / internal audit; no combined assurance modelP15 — assurance & integrity of informationControl weaknesses & misstatements undetected → unreliable information.
Stakeholder relationships ignored; no stakeholder-inclusive approachP16 — stakeholder relationshipsDamage to reputation & the social licence to operate.
Principles not applied, or applied without explaining howKing IV overarching — apply & explainNon-compliance with the Code → JSE-listed companies must disclose the gap; reputational signal.
Exam pattern for all three frameworks: name the behaviour → state what it threatens (threat/principle/section) → state the impact (independence impaired · personal liability · reportable irregularity · governance failure · reputational damage) → give the action (safeguard, rotate, disclose, report to IRBA, decline/withdraw). 🍀
