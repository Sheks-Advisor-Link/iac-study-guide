Part 0 · Exam technique — the meta-skill

0.1 How tax is tested in the IAC

  • You are given a legislation handbook / rate card in the exam. You do not memorise every threshold — you memorise the structure, triggers and method, and read exact rates/brackets off the card. Marks are for applying the law to the facts, not reciting it.
  • Two answer types: (a) Discussion ("discuss/explain the tax consequences") and (b) Calculation ("calculate the tax liability / taxable income"). Many questions blend both.
  • ~1 mark per valid point. Short, distinct, applied sentences win. A paragraph with one idea = 1 mark; five crisp applied points = 5 marks.
  • Communication-skills marks (X1) are awarded for logical layout, headings, and a memo/letter format when asked. Always use the requested format (memo "To/From/Subject", letter sign-off).
  • Read the "Required" precisely: who is the taxpayer (individual / company / trust)? Which year? Which taxes (normal tax only? incl. VAT? CGT? donations?)? Stay in scope.

0.2 The discussion framework — "Identify → Rule → Apply → Case law → Conclude"

Per issue, write ① Section – name the section/definition that governs the item.   ② Rule – state the requirement it imposes.   ③ Apply – tie it to the specific facts/figures in the scenario.   ④ Case law – cite the leading case for any principle (one named case = often a mark).   ⑤ Conclude – state the consequence (included/deductible/taxable, and the amount).

Model extract (Pine Jel): "s11(a) is not relevant as s23B(3) gives priority to s24J… s24J(2) requires the amount be incurred in the production of income… purpose was to on-lend at a higher rate, so it is in production of income (PE Tramways)… re-lending at a profit constitutes a trade (Burgess / Solaglass)… Conclusion: the interest is deductible." — each step scored a mark.

0.3 The calculation framework

  • Always present a vertical pro-forma with a description column and a R column; show every working (workings earn marks even if the final number is wrong — "own-figure" / method marks).
  • Label each line with the section driving it (e.g. "s11(e) wear-and-tear"). Use brackets for deductions.
  • For VAT always show the tax fraction 15/115 (output) or 15/115 on cost (input) and the section for timing/value.
  • State your assumptions where facts are ambiguous — a reasonable stated assumption protects the marks.

0.4 Trigger-spotting method

  • Tax questions are keyword-driven. Each fact in the scenario is a deliberate trigger pointing to a section. Train yourself to read the scenario and annotate each fact with its section before writing.
  • Work the master pro-forma top-to-bottom (Part 1) — it forces completeness so you never miss an inclusion or deduction.
  • See Part 3 for the keyword → section → action cheat-sheet.

Part 2 · Topic-by-topic summary

1 · Gross income, residence & source

GI definition (s1): the total amount, in cash or otherwise, received by or accrued to (resident = worldwide; non-resident = from an SA source), excluding receipts of a capital nature.

  • Resident = ordinarily resident (Cohen / Kuttel) or physically present (day test). Resident → taxed on worldwide income; non-resident → only SA-source.
  • Amount = money or money's value (Lategan; Brummeria — interest-free loan has a value).
  • Received = for own benefit (Geldenhuys; Pyott — deposits still GI; Witwatersrand Racing Clubs).
  • Accrued = unconditionally entitled (Mooi; Lategan). Earlier of received/accrued (Delfos).
  • Capital vs revenue = intention / scheme of profit-making (Pick 'n Pay; Visser — tree vs fruit; Natal Estates; Stott; Founders Hill). The big one — argue both sides, conclude.
Triggers"foreign", "emigrated", "non-resident", "ordinarily resident" → residence & source + s6quat / DTA. · "deposit / received in advance / refundable" → received for own benefit + maybe s24C. · "sold an asset — is it capital?" → capital vs revenue case law.
TechniqueFor capital-vs-revenue, set out the intention test, apply BOTH the taxpayer's and SARS's argument with case law, then conclude. Onus that an amount exists is on SARS (Butcher Bros); onus that it's capital/exempt is on the taxpayer (s102 TAA).

2 · Special inclusions & exempt income

  • Special inclusions (par (a)–(n) of GI) bring in amounts even if capital: annuities (a), alimony, services/restraint of trade (cA/cB), lump sums (d) & (e)(retirement) — separate tables, recoupments (n)/s8(4), lease premiums (g)/(h), key-man proceeds.
  • Exempt income (s10): local dividends (s10(1)(k)); interest exemption (s10(1)(i)) — <65: R23 800, 65+: R34 500; UIF/foreign pensions; s10(1)(o) foreign employment (R1.25m cap, 183/60 days); bursaries; uniform allowances.
  • Foreign dividends — s10B: exempt if ≥10% holding (participation), else partial exemption ratio (effective ~20%).
Triggers"restraint of trade", "annuity", "alimony", "lump sum", "lease premium" → special inclusion. · "interest received" → s10(1)(i) exemption. · "dividend" → s10(1)(k) exempt for normal tax (but 20% dividends tax). · "works abroad 183 days" → s10(1)(o)(ii).

3 · General & specific deductions

General deduction formula = s11(a) (positive test) read with s23 (negative test).

  • s11(a) requirements: ① carrying on a trade; ② expenditure & losses; ③ actually incurred (unconditional liability — Edgars; Nasionale Pers); ④ in the production of income (Port Elizabeth Tramways — closely connected to income-earning operations; Joffe — necessary concomitant); ⑤ during the year; ⑥ not of a capital nature (New State Areas — fixed vs floating capital; BP SA).
  • s23 prohibitions: private/domestic (23(a),(b) home office rules); not in production (23(g)); tax itself (23(d)); provisions/reserves; bribes (23(o)); fines.
  • s23H — prepaid expenditure: limited to portion enjoyed in the year (unless ≤R100k or service within 6 months).
  • Pre-trade expenditure s11A; legal costs s11(c); bad debts s11(i); doubtful debts s11(j).
Triggers"is X deductible?" → s11(a) + s23 step-through. · "paid in advance / prepaid" → s23H. · "before the business started" → s11A. · "home office" → s23(b). · "legal fees / damages" → s11(c)/(a) + capital nature.
TechniqueMarch through each s11(a) requirement as a sub-heading, apply facts + case law to each, then check it isn't blocked by s23. One mark per requirement satisfied/failed.

4 · Trading stock (s22)

  • Closing stock s22(1) — added to income at lower of cost or NRV (net realisable value); write-downs for obsolescence allowed if justified.
  • Opening stock s22(2) — deducted (= prior closing).
  • Cost = cost price + costs to bring to present location/condition (incl. customs); no NRV write-down merely for slow-moving without evidence.
  • s22(8) — stock applied for private/non-trade use or distributed → deemed recovery at market value (or cost). s22(4) — acquired for no consideration → market value in & out.
  • s23F — limits deduction where stock not yet disposed of / income not yet received.
Triggers"inventory / stock on hand", "obsolete stock" → s22(1) lower of cost/NRV. · "took stock for own use / donated stock / drawings" → s22(8). · "free stock / inherited stock" → s22(4).

6 · Recoupments & disposal allowances

  • s8(4)(a) recoupment: on disposal, recoup allowances previously claimed = lower of (proceeds, cost) − tax value. Included in income (special inclusion). Capped at cost (excess is a capital gain).
  • s11(o) scrapping/disposal allowance: if proceeds < tax value on disposal (and useful life ≤10 yrs originally), the shortfall is deductible.
  • s24M — proceeds not determinable; s20B — proceeds not yet accrued.

Recoupment & CGT on asset disposal (typical layout)

ProceedsR500 000
Cost R600 000, allowances claimed R250 000 → tax valueR350 000
Recoupment s8(4) = lower(proceeds, cost) − tax value = 500 000 − 350 000R150 000 (income)
CGT: proceeds 500 000 − recoupment 150 000 = 350 000; base cost = cost 600 000 − allowances 250 000 = 350 000 → no gainR0

If proceeds > original cost, the excess above cost is a capital gain; recoupment is limited to allowances claimed.

Triggers"sold a depreciable asset" → ALWAYS test s8(4) recoupment AND CGT. · "sold for less than tax value" → s11(o). · "proceeds payable over years / contingent" → s24M / s20B.

7 · Repairs vs improvements (s11(d))

  • s11(d) deduction for repairs (restoration of part, same materials/function) — fully deductible. Requires the asset to be used in trade / income-producing.
  • Improvement (betterment, new advantage, different material creating greater capacity) = capital → not s11(d); add to base cost / claim building allowance instead.
  • Cases: Flemming (genuine repair = restoration of damaged part); African Products (renewal of subsidiary part is a repair).
Triggers"repaired / replaced / renovated / upgraded a building or machine" → s11(d) repair vs improvement test. "Improved capacity / better than before" = improvement (capital).

8 · Capital gains tax (Eighth Schedule, via s26A)

Capital gain = Proceeds − Base cost, per asset, per disposal.

  • Proceeds (par 35) less base cost (par 20 — cost of acquisition, improvements, direct costs; pre-2001 use valuation date value / TAB / 20% rule).
  • Net capital gain = sum of gains/losses − annual exclusion (R40 000 indiv; R300 000 in year of death) → × inclusion rate: indiv/special trust 40%; company/other trust 80%.
  • Effective max rates: individual 18% · company 21.6% · trust 36%.
  • Primary residence (par 45): first R2 000 000 of the gain excluded.
  • Roll-over relief: par 65/66 (involuntary / replacement asset), s9HA (death), corporate rules.
  • Recoupment is removed from proceeds for CGT (no double tax).
Triggers"sold / disposed / donated / emigrated / died" → disposal event. · "house / home" → par 45 R2m. · "shares sold" → capital (CGT) unless trader (revenue). · "replaced damaged asset with insurance" → par 65/66 roll-over.
Watch-outDonation, death, ceasing to be resident, and distribution in specie are all deemed disposals at market value. Capital losses are ring-fenced against capital gains only (cannot reduce ordinary income).

9 · Value-added tax

  • VAT payable = output tax − input tax. Standard rate 15%; tax fraction 15/115.
  • Output on taxable supplies by a vendor in the course of an enterprise. Zero-rated (s11 — exports, basic foods, going concern) vs exempt (s12 — financial services, residential rent, public transport, education). Exempt ≠ enterprise → no input.
  • Time of supply (s9): earlier of invoice or payment; special rules — connected persons, fixed property (s9(3)(d): earlier of registration or payment), instalment credit, lay-by.
  • Value of supply (s10): consideration × 15/115; connected persons → open market value.
  • Input denied (s17(2)): entertainment, motor cars (sedans/double cabs), club subscriptions.
  • Deemed supplies: fringe benefits, ceasing to be a vendor (exit), short-term insurance payouts, indemnity.
  • Adjustments: change in use (s18), irrecoverable debts (s22 — claim input on bad debt; recovery → output), bad debt recovered.
Triggers"vendor / registered for VAT" → VAT in scope. · "exported / sold as a going concern / brown bread, milk" → zero-rated (s11). · "interest, rent of a house, school fees" → exempt (s12). · "company car / staff meals" → fringe-benefit deemed supply + possible input denial. · "fixed property sold" → s9(3)(d) timing. · "bad debt written off" → s22 input adjustment.
TechniqueFor each transaction: ① Is it a supply by a vendor in an enterprise? ② Standard / zero / exempt? ③ Time of supply? ④ Value × 15/115? ⑤ Any input denial or adjustment? Do output and input for both parties if asked.

10 · Individuals — allowances & advances (s8(1))

  • Travel allowance: included in income; reduce by business portion. Deemed cost (fixed cost + fuel + maintenance per SARS tables ÷ total km × business km) or actual costs; need a logbook. 80% subject to PAYE (20% if >80% business use). Reimbursive: tax-free up to prescribed rate/km if no other travel allowance & ≤ business km.
  • Subsistence allowance (s8(1)(c)): away ≥1 night → reduce by deemed daily amounts (meals+incidental / incidental only — read rate card) or actual (proof). Foreign travel: per-country amounts.
  • Right of use of company car (7th Sch 2(b)) is a fringe benefit, not s8(1) — compare the two if the question offers a choice.
Triggers"travel allowance + logbook / km" → s8(1)(b) deemed vs actual. · "away on business / nights away / per diem" → s8(1)(c) subsistence. · "should I take a car or an allowance?" → compare 7th Sch 2(b) FB vs travel allowance.

12 · Retirement contributions & medical credits

  • s11F retirement deduction: greater of contributions limited to 27.5% of higher of (remuneration, taxable income incl. taxable capital gain), capped at R350 000 p.a. Excess carries forward.
  • s6A medical scheme fees credit: fixed monthly credit per member + dependants (read rate card) — a rebate off tax.
  • s6B additional medical credit: for out-of-pocket + excess contributions; formula differs for 65+/disability vs under-65 (25% of (qualifying expenses − 7.5% of taxable income) component).

13 · Employees' tax (PAYE) & provisional tax

  • PAYE withheld by employer on "remuneration" (4th Sch). Excludes amounts to independent contractors (control/supervision test; premises & tools statutory exclusion). Travel allowance: 80% in remuneration. SDL 1%, UIF 1%+1%.
  • Provisional tax: taxpayers earning non-remuneration income (companies, sole traders, rental, etc.). 1st payment (6 months): 50% of estimated tax. 2nd (year-end): full estimate — if taxable income ≤R1m use "basic amount"; >R1m must be within 80% or face s89quat/par20 penalty. 3rd (top-up, voluntary) to avoid interest.
Triggers"is she an employee or independent contractor?" → 4th Sch remuneration/independent-trade test. · "estimate / 6-month / 80% / basic amount" → provisional tax. · "under-estimated taxable income >R1m" → par 20 under-estimation penalty.

14 · Retirement lump sums & severance benefits

  • Lump sums on retirement/death/retrenchment and severance benefits are taxed on separate (concessional) tax tables — cumulative, with a tax-free amount (read rate card, e.g. first ~R550 000 at 0%).
  • Withdrawal (resignation) lump sums use a harsher withdrawal table (smaller tax-free amount).
  • s10C exempts the portion from non-deductible contributions previously made.
  • Severance benefit qualifies for the retirement table if: ≥55, or ill-health, or retrenchment (not a connected-person director >5% unless retrenchment).
Triggers"retrenched / retired / lump sum from pension/provident/RA" → 2nd Schedule + retirement table. · "resigned and withdrew" → withdrawal table. · "retrenchment package" → severance benefit (retirement table).

15 · Dividends & contributed tax capital (CTC)

  • Dividends tax 20% — a withholding tax on the beneficial owner, withheld by the company/regulated intermediary. Exempt recipients: SA resident companies, retirement funds, PBOs (declaration + written undertaking required).
  • CTC = consideration received for share issues; a return of CTC is not a dividend (capital, reduces base cost / triggers CGT). Directors must designate CTC vs dividend.
  • Dividends exempt from normal tax (s10(1)(k)) but subject to dividends tax (unless exempt owner). Dividends in specie → company pays the 20%.
Triggers"declared a dividend" → 20% dividends tax + identify exempt owners. · "return of capital / capital distribution" → CTC vs dividend (CGT). · "dividend to a holding company" → exempt (declaration).

📄 Tested in: Tax 02 (Dizzy — dividends tax), Tax 07 (On The Line), Tax 08 (Beeprop).

16 · Companies & assessed losses (s20)

  • Company rate 27%. s20 assessed loss carries forward; for companies the set-off is capped at the higher of R1m or 80% of taxable income (so a minimum 20% of profit is taxed before old losses).
  • Individuals: assessed loss from a "suspect trade" (hobby-type, s20A) is ring-fenced (cannot offset other income) if the "3-of-5 years loss" / listed-trade tests are met.
  • Trade requirement: a company must still carry on a trade to carry the loss (continuity).
Triggers"company with brought-forward loss + big profit" → s20 80%/R1m cap. · "individual side-business making losses (farming, B&B, sport)" → s20A ring-fencing. · "dormant company / change of shareholding" → loss continuity.

17 · Corporate rules (s41–47) — group reorganisations

SectionTransactionRelief
s42Asset-for-shareRoll-over: transferor disposes at base cost/tax value; no immediate CGT/recoupment; gets shares.
s44Amalgamation/mergerRoll-over on merger of companies.
s45Intra-group transferTax-neutral transfers between ≥70%-held group companies.
s46 / s47Unbundling / liquidation distributionRoll-over relief on distributing shares / winding up.
  • Relief also covers transfer duty, STT and VAT (s45 §). De-grouping charge if assets leave the group within 6 years.
Triggers"transferred assets to a group company / restructure / incorporate a business for shares" → corporate rules. Test: is it asset-for-share (s42), intra-group (s45, ≥70%), or unbundling (s46)?

📄 Tested in: Tax 01 (Arnod — corporate rules), Tax 05 (Ukwaka — corporate rules), Tax 08 (Beeprop — asset-for-share).

18 · Interest & section 24J

  • s24J spreads interest on an "instrument" over its term on a yield-to-maturity (accrual) basis — overrides cash timing. Issuer deducts (s24J(2)); holder includes (s24J(3)).
  • s23B(3): where s24J applies, s11(a) cannot — apply the specific provision.
  • s24O — interest to acquire shares in an operating company deemed in production of income (deductible).
  • s8F/8FA — interest on hybrid debt / hybrid interest reclassified as a dividend in specie (not deductible).
  • Withholding tax on interest to non-residents 15%.
Triggers"loan / bond / debenture / interest accrued" → s24J accrual. · "borrowed to buy shares" → s24O. · "shareholder loan, excessive interest, subordinated" → s8F/8FA hybrid. · "interest paid to a foreign lender" → 15% WHT.

📝 Milpark example — interest deductibility (Pine Jel)

Mr Cele borrows at the bank and on-lends to a company at a higher rate.

Argument chains11(a) excluded by s23B(3) (s24J applies). s24J(2): deductible if from a trade & in production of income → on-lending at a higher rate = a trade (Burgess/Solaglass) & in production of income (PE Tramways). Burden of proof s102 (TAA). Conclusion: deductible.

📄 Tested in: Tax 03 (Pine Jel — s24J), Tax 09 (s24J).

19 · Foreign tax (s25D / s24I / s6quat / s10B / s9D)

  • s25D — translate foreign receipts/accruals & expenditure to ZAR at the spot or average rate.
  • s24I — exchange gains/losses on monetary items (foreign debtors/creditors/loans, FECs) included/deducted on a year-end translation basis (realised + unrealised).
  • s6quat — rebate for foreign taxes paid on foreign income (limited to SA tax on that income).
  • s9D — CFC net income imputed to SA resident shareholders (>50% holding) unless FBE exemption.
  • s10B — foreign dividend exemptions (participation / ratio).
Triggers"foreign currency transaction / forex debtor or loan / FEC" → s24I + s25D. · "paid tax overseas" → s6quat rebate. · "controlled foreign company / offshore subsidiary >50%" → s9D. · "foreign dividend" → s10B + s6quat.

📝 Milpark example — non-resident interest WHT (s50B)

German resident earns SA interest: bank R255 000, JSE-listed bonds R140 000, loan to ABC (Pty) Ltd R125 000.

All SA-source interest, exempt from normal tax (s10(1)(h) for non-residents)exempt
WHT on interest s50B: only the ABC loan 125 000 × 15%18 750
Bank & JSE-bond interest exempt from WHT (s50D)0
In plain EnglishNon-residents pay SA tax only on SA-source income; their interest is generally exempt from normal tax but a 15% WHT applies (with bank/listed-bond exemptions).

📄 Tested in: Tax 05 (Ukwaka — s6quat, DTAs).

20 · Transfer pricing (s31)

  • Applies to cross-border transactions between connected persons not at arm's length. SARS adjusts to the arm's-length price (primary adjustment) and treats the benefit as a deemed dividend/loan (secondary adjustment, 20% dividends tax).
  • Documentation & OECD methods (CUP, resale, cost-plus, TNMM, profit-split).
Triggers"sold to / charged a foreign parent or subsidiary at a non-market price / management fee / royalty to offshore connected party" → s31.

21 · Leasing

  • Operating lease: lessor includes rental (GI); lessee deducts rental (s11(a)). VAT on each rental.
  • Lease premium (par (g) GI): lump sum for the right of use → fully taxable to lessor on receipt; lessee deducts over the lease period (s11(f)), max 25 years.
  • Leasehold improvements (par (h)): obligation to improve → value included in lessor's GI; lessor s11(g) allowance over lease term; lessee s11(g) deduction.
  • Instalment credit / finance lease (s23A): capital allowances limited to rental income (anti-avoidance). Sale-and-leaseback rules.
Triggers"lease premium / lump sum to lease" → par (g) + s11(f). · "tenant must build/improve" → par (h) + s11(g). · "sale and leaseback" → s23A/s23D limitations.

22 · Intellectual property & royalties

  • s11(gB)/(gC) — deduction for acquiring/registering IP (some IP excluded — trademarks not deductible). Invented/devised IP: allowance on cost over time.
  • s23I — denies deductions for royalties/IP payments to connected non-taxable persons (anti-avoidance).
  • Royalty withholding tax 15% to non-residents.
Triggers"bought/registered a patent, design, copyright" → s11(gB)/(gC) (trademark = no deduction). · "royalty to a foreigner" → 15% WHT + s23I.

23 · Debt benefits — s19 & Eighth Schedule par 12A

  • When a debt is reduced/waived for less than the amount owed → a "reduction amount" (debt benefit).
  • Use the loan was applied to determines the consequence: deductible expenditure (operating) → s19 (recoupment / reduce assessed loss); trading stock → reduce cost/closing stock; allowance asset → par 12A reduces base cost then limits future allowances (s19(7)); capital asset → par 12A reduces base cost (then capital loss/gain).
  • VAT only reverses if the creditor who waived also charged the original VAT (Pine Jel principle).
  • Exclusions: donations (s19(8)), group/connected reductions, deceased estate, etc.
Triggers"debt written off / waived / compromise / business rescue / debt restructure" → s19 / par 12A. Trace what the borrowed money was spent on — that drives the treatment.

📝 Milpark example — debt waived (Pine Jel)

NatBank waives a loan: R450 000 funded repairs (s11(d) deduction) and R800 000 funded an allowance asset (building).

R450 000 (funded deductible repairs) → s19 recoupment (VAT-exclusive 391 304)s8(4)/s19(5)
R800 000 (funded allowance asset) → par 12A reduces base cost; s19(7) limits future allowancespar 12A
In plain EnglishTrace what the borrowed money paid for: operating cost → recoupment (s19); allowance/capital asset → reduce base cost & cap future allowances (par 12A). VAT only reverses if the creditor who waived also charged the original VAT.

📄 Tested in: Tax 03 (Pine Jel — s19/par 12A), Tax 06 (Speak Easy — debt benefits).

24 · Share-based payments (s8C)

  • s8C — equity instruments acquired by virtue of employment vest → gain (market value − consideration paid) taxed as remuneration on vesting, not on grant.
  • Restricted shares: no tax until restriction lifts (vesting). Dividends on restricted shares may be revenue (s10(1)(k) exclusion).
  • Company side: s11(lA) deduction for shares issued to employees.
Triggers"employee share scheme / options / restricted shares / vesting" → s8C (tax on vesting, included in remuneration).

25 · Estate duty (Estate Duty Act)

  • Dutiable estate × 20% (first R30m) / 25% (above R30m).
  • Property (s3(2)) + deemed property (s3(3): life insurance proceeds, donationes mortis causa, accrual claim) − deductions (s4: debts, funeral, bequests to spouse s4(q), PBOs s4(h)) − s4A abatement R3 500 000 (portable → surviving spouse can use R7m).
  • Spouse bequest fully deductible (s4(q)).
Triggers"died / deceased estate / estate duty" → Estate Duty Act pro-forma. · "life policy on the deceased" → s3(3) deemed property. · "left everything to spouse" → s4(q) deduction.

📝 Milpark example — estate duty (Mr A)

Property + deemed (house 1.9m, car 250k, art 5m, bank 202k)7 352 000
Less deductions (credit card 2 000, executor 50 000, income tax 120 000)(172 000)
Net value of estate7 180 000

In plain EnglishEverything owned + deemed (life policies, accrual) at death-date MV, less debts/admin and the R3.5m abatement, × 20% (25% over R30m). Spouse bequests fully deductible. Use death-date MV (or actual arm's-length sale), ignoring later damage or sweetheart will-prices.

📄 Tested in: Tax 01 (Arnod — estate duty), Tax 09 (Bala Trust — estate duty).

26 · Donations tax

  • 20% (≤R30m cumulative) / 25% (>R30m) on the value of property disposed of gratuitously by a resident. Donor liable (donee jointly if unpaid).
  • Exemptions: spouse donations (unlimited); annual R100 000 (natural persons); bona fide maintenance; PBOs; donations cancelled within 6 months.
  • s58 deemed donation: disposal for inadequate consideration. s7/par 68–73 attribution: income/gains diverted via donation taxed back to donor.
Triggers"gave / donated / sold cheaply to a relative" → donations tax + s58 + attribution (s7). · "donation to spouse / PBO" → exempt. · ">R100 000 to children" → 20% on excess.

27 · Deceased estates & trusts

  • Death (s9HA): deemed disposal of all assets at market value (CGT) — except to surviving spouse (roll-over). Annual exclusion R300 000 in year of death.
  • Deceased estate is a separate taxpayer for post-death income/gains until distribution.
  • Trusts: rate 45% (special trusts taxed like individuals). Conduit principle (s25B / par 80): income/gains flow through to beneficiaries with a vested right and are taxed in their hands; otherwise taxed in the trust.
  • s7 / par 68–73 attribution: income/gains attributed back to the donor where a donation/low-interest loan funded the trust.
  • s7C: interest-free / low-interest loan to a trust → the foregone interest (official rate) is a deemed annual donation (uses the R100k exemption).
Triggers"trust / beneficiary / vested vs discretionary" → conduit s25B. · "interest-free loan to a trust" → s7C deemed donation. · "donor funded the trust" → s7 attribution. · "died" → s9HA + spouse roll-over.

📝 Milpark example — trust conduit principle (Connect Trust)

Discretionary trust; R100 000 income (div 45k / interest 35k / rent 20k); annuity + discretionary distributions; rest retained. Income keeps its nature flowing out (s25B / par 80).

  • Vested/distributed to beneficiary → taxed in beneficiary (interest keeps s10(1)(i), dividends s10(1)(k)).
  • Minor benefits / donor funded → attributed to the donor (s7(3)/(5)); income from a different donor's asset is NOT attributed to that donor.
  • Retained, no donor → taxed in the trust @ 45%.
In plain EnglishThree buckets for every rand: beneficiary (if vested), donor (if minor benefits / donor funded), else the trust. Split each income type proportionately, drop into the right bucket. Losses can't flow through — s25B(3) caps a beneficiary's deduction at their vested income.

📄 Tested in: Tax 07 (On The Line — trusts), Tax 09 (Bala Family Trust).

28 · Tax administration (TAA)

  • s102 burden of proof on the taxpayer (that an amount is exempt/deductible/non-taxable); SARS bears onus that an amount exists.
  • Assessments & prescription: SARS generally cannot reopen after 3 years (5 for self-assessment) unless fraud, misrepresentation or non-disclosure.
  • Dispute: objection (s104) → appeal → ADR / Tax Board / Tax Court. s164 "pay now, argue later" (request suspension of payment).
  • Penalties: understatement penalty (s222 — 0–200% by behaviour table), admin non-compliance penalties, s89quat interest. Voluntary disclosure (VDP) relief.
  • Tax practitioners must register (s240); SAICA member ethical duties.
Triggers"SARS reassessed / additional assessment / dispute / penalty" → TAA. · "3 years ago / prescribed" → prescription. · "disagree with SARS" → s104 objection + s164 suspension. · "understated income" → s222 USP table.

29 · General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR, s80A–80L)

  • SARS can attack an "impermissible avoidance arrangement" if: ① an arrangement; ② sole/main purpose = tax benefit; ③ + one tainted element: abnormality (not arm's length), lack of commercial substance, misuse/abuse of the Act, or (business context) abnormal rights/obligations.
  • Consequence: SARS disregards/re-characterises the arrangement.
  • Distinguish tax planning (legal) vs avoidance (GAAR) vs evasion (criminal).
Triggers"scheme / step transaction / round-trip / no commercial purpose / solely to save tax / abnormal terms" → GAAR. Always check specific anti-avoidance (s7, s8F, s23I, s31) first, then GAAR as the catch-all.
