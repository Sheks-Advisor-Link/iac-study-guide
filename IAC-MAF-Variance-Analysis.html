Variance Analysis — Summary & Discussion Points

Every variance: formula · causes · who's responsible · what to look at · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden
What variances are: the difference between the flexed-budget (standard) cost/revenue for the actual output and the actual result. Favourable (F) = better than standard; Adverse (A) = worse. The point isn't the number — it's why it happened, who controls it, and whether one variance caused another. Always reconcile budget → actual via an operating statement.

SalesPrice(actual price − standard price) × actual units sold
Volume(actual units − budgeted units) × standard contribution (marginal) / standard profit (absorption) — split into mix & quantity for multi-product

Reading the sign: for cost variances the formula is (standard − actual), so a positive = Favourable. For sales variances it's (actual − standard/budget), so a positive = Favourable too. Quantity multiplier: price variances use the quantity purchased/sold; usage/efficiency variances use the standard rate/price.

Two reminders① Use the flexed budget (standard for the actual output) — not the original fixed budget. ② Fixed-OH volume exists only under absorption costing (marginal has only the expenditure variance).

4 · When & whether to investigate a variance

  • Size & % — material in rand and relative to the standard (set a threshold).
  • Controllability — is it within the manager's control, or external/market (→ a planning issue)?
  • Trend — one-off vs a worsening pattern over months.
  • Interdependence — does it explain/result from another variance (price↔usage, rate↔efficiency, mix↔yield, price↔volume)?
  • Reliability of the standard — is the standard out of date or unrealistic?
  • Cost vs benefit — only investigate if the expected benefit exceeds the cost.

5 · Planning vs operational variances

  • Planning variance = caused by a flawed/out-of-date original standard → uncontrollable → revise the standard (e.g. a market price rose for everyone). Difference between the original and the revised standard.
  • Operational variance = difference between the revised (realistic) standard and the actual → what management could control → use it for fair performance evaluation.
Why it mattersSplitting them avoids blaming a manager for an unrealistic standard (planning) and isolates true performance (operational) — a common discussion mark.

6 · Interdependence — link the variances (high-yield)

  • Cheaper material (F price) → more waste & rework (A usage) and possibly A labour efficiency & quality problems.
  • Lower-grade labour (F rate) → A labour efficiency (slower) and A material usage.
  • Favourable material mix → adverse yield (cheaper blend, lower output).
  • Sales price cut (A price) → higher sales volume (F volume) — assess the net effect.
  • Overtime to meet demand → A labour rate (premium) but F sales volume.

7 · Limitations of standard costing & behavioural points

  • Modern environment: less useful in JIT/TQM/customised/short-life production; standards date quickly; focuses on cost, not quality & continuous improvement (kaizen); backward-looking.
  • Dysfunctional behaviour: buying cheap to beat the price variance (→ quality issues); over-producing to absorb fixed OH / create a favourable volume variance (builds inventory); cutting corners to hit efficiency targets.
  • Behavioural: the tightness of standards affects motivation — ideal (demotivating), attainable (best for motivation), basic/loose (no challenge); avoid a blame culture; apply controllability.
  • Non-financial measures (quality, delivery, customer satisfaction) should sit alongside variances (balanced scorecard).
Exam technique for a variance question① compute the variance (state F/A); ② give the most likely cause(s) tied to the scenario; ③ name who is responsible & whether it's controllable; ④ flag any interdependence with other variances; ⑤ say whether to investigate (size/controllability/trend) and whether the standard needs revising (planning vs operational); ⑥ conclude. One applied point = one mark — always link to the scenario.

Compiled for private IAC exam preparation. Use the flexed budget; remember fixed-OH volume is absorption-only; and the marks in a discussion are in the causes, responsibility, interdependence & investigation — not just the numbers.
