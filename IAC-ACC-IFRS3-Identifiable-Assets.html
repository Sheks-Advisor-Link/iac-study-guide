IFRS 3 — Business Combinations (in depth)

The acquisition method · identifiable assets · the exceptions · what's excluded & why · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden
Your question — brand, advert & customer relationships? Brand = YES (a marketing-related identifiable intangible — recognised at fair value in a combination even though the acquiree could not recognise its own internally-generated brand). Customer relationships = YES (customer-related intangible — always if contractual, and if non-contractual then where separable). An advert / advertising spend = NO (it is a cost, not a separable or contractual asset — it helped build the brand, whose value is captured in the brand intangible or, failing that, in goodwill).

1 · The acquisition method — the 4 steps

  1. Identify the acquirer — the entity that obtains control of the acquiree (IFRS 10). (Watch reverse acquisitions.)
  2. Determine the acquisition date — the date control is obtained (usually when consideration passes & the acquirer can direct the relevant activities).
  3. Recognise & measure the identifiable assets acquired, liabilities assumed and any non-controlling interest (NCI).
  4. Recognise & measure goodwill (or a gain on a bargain purchase).

2 · Recognition principle & the identifiability test

  • At acquisition date the acquirer recognises, separately from goodwill, the identifiable assets acquired & liabilities assumed, if they: ① meet the definition of an asset/liability at acquisition date; and ② are part of what was exchanged for the acquiree (not a separate transaction).
  • The acquirer recognises intangibles the acquiree never recognised (e.g. an internally-generated brand, customer relationships, in-process R&D) — because in a combination they are recognised & fair-valued afresh.
Identifiability criterion (key for intangibles)An intangible is identifiable (so recognised separately from goodwill) if it is EITHER:
  • Separable — capable of being separated & sold, transferred, licensed, rented or exchanged (alone or with a related contract/asset); OR
  • Arises from contractual or other legal rights (whether or not transferable/separable).
Meet either limb → recognise it separately. Meet neither → it stays inside goodwill.

3 · Measurement principle

  • Identifiable assets & liabilities are measured at acquisition-date fair value (IFRS 13).
  • NCI is measured at the acquirer's choice (per transaction): (a) fair value → "full" goodwill, or (b) the NCI's proportionate share of net identifiable assets → "partial" goodwill.

4 · The five categories of identifiable intangibles (recognise separately)

CategoryExamplesIdentifiable?
Marketing-relatedTrademarks, trade names, brands, internet domain names, masthead, non-competition agreementsYes — contractual/legal or separable

So, on your three itemsBrand/trademark → marketing-related → recognise at FV (even if the acquiree built it internally & never recognised it). Customer relationships → customer-related → recognise (contractual = always; non-contractual = if separable, e.g. a customer list that can be sold/licensed). Advertising → not a category — an advert is not separable & not a contractual right; advertising spend is expensed (IAS 38). Its benefit lives in the brand (recognised) or is subsumed in goodwill.

5 · Net identifiable assets & goodwill

Net identifiable assets = identifiable assets acquired − liabilities assumed (all at fair value, plus deferred tax on the FV adjustments per IAS 12).

Consideration transferred (at fair value)x
+ Non-controlling interest (FV or proportionate)x
+ Fair value of any previously-held equity interest (step acquisition)x
− Fair value of net identifiable assets acquired(x)
= Goodwill  (if negative → gain on bargain purchase, recheck then recognise in P/L)x
  • Goodwill is the residual; it is not amortised but tested for impairment annually (IAS 36); it is not separable.
  • Consideration transferred = FV of assets given + liabilities incurred + equity issued + contingent consideration (at FV). Acquisition-related costs are expensed (advisory, legal, due diligence) — not part of consideration (debt/equity issue costs follow IFRS 9 / IAS 32).

6 · The recognition & measurement EXCEPTIONS (examinable)

Most items follow the recognition (asset/liability definition) & measurement (fair value) principles — but IFRS 3 lists specific exceptions:

TypeItemHow it's treated (the exception)
Recognition exceptionContingent liabilitiesRecognise a present obligation from a past event if its FV can be measured reliably — even if an outflow is not probable (differs from IAS 37). (No recognition for a possible obligation.)
Recognition + measurementIncome taxes (IAS 12)Recognise & measure deferred tax per IAS 12, not at fair value (incl. deferred tax on FV adjustments & acquired tax losses).
Recognition + measurementEmployee benefits (IAS 19)Per IAS 19.
Recognition + measurementIndemnification assetsRecognise & measure on the same basis as the indemnified item (mirror it), with a collectability allowance.
Measurement exceptionReacquired rightsMeasure on the remaining contractual term (ignore potential renewals/market assumptions); amortise over that term.
Measurement exceptionShare-based payments (IFRS 2)Replacement awards measured per IFRS 2 (market-based measure), not FV.
Measurement exceptionAssets held for sale (IFRS 5)Measure at fair value less costs to sell.
Recognition + measurementLeases (IFRS 16, acquiree as lessee)Recognise a lease liability at the PV of remaining payments & an ROU asset at the same amount, adjusted for off-market (favourable/unfavourable) terms. (Short-term/low-value need not be recognised.)

7 · What is NOT recognised as an identifiable asset — and why

ItemWhy it's excluded
Assembled workforceNot identifiable — not separable and not a contractual/legal right; the value of a skilled workforce is subsumed into goodwill (IFRS 3 specifically prohibits separate recognition).
Acquiree's internally-generated goodwillCannot be separately identified/measured → part of the goodwill residual.
Advertising / promotional spendIt is an expense (IAS 38), not a separable or contractual asset; the benefit it created shows up in the brand (if identifiable) or in goodwill.
Future / potential customers & contracts not yet existingNo asset exists at acquisition date (only relationships/contracts that exist at that date qualify); future business potential = goodwill.
Acquirer's planned restructuring / future lossesNot a liability of the acquiree at acquisition date — no restructuring provision unless the acquiree already had the obligation; the acquirer's own plans are post-combination costs.
Items that are separate transactionsSettling a pre-existing relationship, paying for future services (earn-outs tied to employment), reimbursing acquisition costs → excluded from consideration & net assets (accounted for separately — see IFRS 3.51–52).

8 · Other key rules

  • Measurement period — up to 12 months after acquisition date to finalise provisional amounts; adjust retrospectively only for facts/conditions that existed at acquisition date.
  • Contingent consideration — classify as a liability or equity. A liability is remeasured to FV through P/L; equity is not remeasured.
  • Step acquisition (control achieved in stages) — remeasure the previously-held interest to FV at acquisition date; the gain/loss goes to P/L (recycle related OCI per the relevant standard).
  • Bargain purchase — reassess the identification & measurement first; then recognise the gain in P/L.
  • Goodwill — not amortised; annual impairment test (IAS 36); allocate to CGUs.

Exam approach① identify the acquirer & acquisition date → ② for each item ask "does it meet the asset/liability definition & the identifiability test (separable OR contractual)?" → ③ fair-value it (watch the exceptions: deferred tax, employee benefits, contingent liabilities, reacquired rights, SBP, HFS) → ④ exclude assembled workforce, internally-generated goodwill, advertising, future customers & separate transactions (→ goodwill) → ⑤ compute goodwill = consideration + NCI + previously-held interest − net identifiable assets.
