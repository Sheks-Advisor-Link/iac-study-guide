Audit Exam Master Pack — SAICA IAC

Ethics · Planning · Client acceptance · Tests of controls · Substantive-procedure mind-maps · CAATs · T. Van Heerden 2026  |  Note: the current governance code is King IV (2016) — there is no King V.
How to answer ANY ethics question: ① identify the ethical issue & which framework applies (CPC / Companies Act / King IV / general) → ② state the principle/requirement → ③ apply it to the scenario facts → ④ assess significance → ⑤ state the action/safeguard/procedure the auditor or management must take → ⑥ conclude. For independence: threat → safeguard. For wrongdoing: NOCLAR / Reportable Irregularity steps. ~1 mark per applied point.
PART 1 · GENERAL ETHICS & DECISION-MAKING (non-legislation)

1.1 · What ethics is & the two approaches

  • Ethics = the moral principles that govern behaviour — doing what is right, not merely what is legal or profitable.
  • Rules-based (a list of do's/don'ts — certain but rigid, "tick-box", can be gamed) vs principles-based (the SAICA Code & King IV approach — apply judgement to the spirit of the principle; flexible & harder to circumvent).

1.2 · Ethical theories — "good for self" vs "good for others"

TheoryThe test of "right"Lens / example
EgoismGood for SELF — act in your own self-interest."What's in it for me?" — the driver behind most fraud & self-interest threats.
Utilitarianism / consequentialismGood for OTHERS — the greatest good for the greatest number; judged by outcomes.Choose the option giving the best net benefit to all stakeholders (society, employees, shareholders).
Deontology (Kant)Duty/rules — act on universal principles regardless of outcome; some acts are simply wrong."Don't lie / don't steal" even if it would produce a good result. Underlies integrity.
Virtue ethicsAct as a person of good character would."What would an honest, courageous, fair professional do?"
Rights-basedRespect individuals' rights.Right to privacy (confidentiality/POPIA), fair treatment.
Justice / fairnessFair distribution & due process.Treat like cases alike; fair procedures & outcomes.

1.3 · Ethical decision-making models (use to structure an answer)

ModelSteps / questions
AAA model (7 steps)① Determine the facts → ② identify the ethical issue → ③ identify the norms, principles & values → ④ identify the alternatives → ⑤ choose the alternative consistent with the norms → ⑥ assess the consequences → ⑦ decide.
Tucker's 5-question modelIs the decision: Profitable? · Legal? · Fair? · Right? · Sustainable (environmentally/socially sound)? Only proceed if "yes" to all.
Fundamental-principles test (CPC)Test the decision against the 5 fundamental principles (below). If it breaches any → don't do it / apply a safeguard.
Conflict-resolution (CPC R210/R360)Consider: relevant facts, ethical issues, fundamental principles, internal procedures; weigh alternatives; consult (TCWG, SAICA, legal); document; if unresolved → consider withdrawing / declining.
Exam techniqueIf asked "discuss the ethical considerations" → name the principle/threat, apply to facts, give the safeguard/action. If asked "how should X resolve this" → use the conflict-resolution steps (facts → principles → alternatives → consult → document → withdraw if needed).
PART 2 · SAICA CODE OF PROFESSIONAL CONDUCT (CPC)

2.1 · The 5 fundamental principles

PrincipleWhat it meansExample breachAction
IntegrityStraightforward & honest in all dealings.Knowingly signing off misleading AFS; turning a blind eye to fraud.Refuse association; correct or withdraw.
ObjectivityNo bias, conflict of interest or undue influence.Auditing a company in which you hold shares.Remove the interest / decline; apply safeguards.
Professional competence & due careMaintain skill & act diligently per standards.Accepting work outside your expertise; sloppy audit.Decline, get training, use an expert, supervise.
ConfidentialityDon't disclose client info without authority, or use it for personal gain.Sharing client info; insider trading on it.Keep secret unless legal duty/right to disclose.
Professional behaviourComply with laws/regulations; avoid discrediting the profession.Misleading advertising; conviction for dishonesty.Comply; uphold the profession's reputation.
Confidentiality — when you MAY/MUST disclose① Permitted by law & client authorises; ② Required by law (e.g. court subpoena, documents for legal proceedings, reporting to a regulator — RI, PRECCA); ③ Professional duty/right (defend yourself in litigation/disciplinary, comply with a SAICA/IRBA review, protect the public interest). Disclose only the necessary amount, to the right party, in good faith.

2.2 · The conceptual framework & the 5 threats

Process: Identify threats to the fundamental principles → Evaluate their significance (against the "reasonable & informed third party" test) → Address them (eliminate the circumstance, apply safeguards, or decline/withdraw).

ThreatScenario triggerSafeguard / action
Self-interestShares/financial interest in client; fee dependence; overdue fees; contingent fees; loan to/from client; gifts; reliance on the fee.Dispose of the interest; remove the individual; independent (EQ) review; limit fee %; decline contingent fees.
Self-reviewAuditing your own work — you prepared the AFS / did the valuation / kept the books / designed the IT system.Use a separate team; don't provide the service to an audit client; management must take responsibility & make decisions.
AdvocacyPromoting the client's position — acting for it in litigation, promoting its shares.Decline the advocacy role; separate teams; withdraw.
FamiliarityLong association; close/family relationship at client; ex-partner now a director; accepting significant gifts/hospitality.Rotate the partner (s92: max 5 yrs); cooling-off; reassign staff; decline gifts.
IntimidationThreat of dismissal/replacement; dominant client; threatened litigation; pressure to accept wrong treatment or reduce fees.Escalate to TCWG; obtain legal advice; stand firm on standards; withdraw if needed.
Safeguard categories① created by the profession/legislation (education, CPD, standards, IRBA inspection, rotation); ② within the firm (independent review/EQR, separate teams, policies, ethics partner); ③ within the client (competent TCWG, audit committee oversight). If no safeguard reduces the threat to an acceptable level → decline/withdraw.

2.4 · NOCLAR — Non-Compliance with Laws & Regulations (CPC s360 / s260)

Applies when a CA(SA) becomes aware of actual or suspected non-compliance with laws/regulations by the client/employer (e.g. fraud, tax evasion, pollution, bribery, breach of the Companies Act).

StepAction by the AUDITOR (in practice)Action by the CA in BUSINESS
1 Obtain understandingUnderstand the matter — nature, circumstances, potential consequences; apply knowledge & expertise.Same — gain a full understanding of the act & the law.
2 DiscussDiscuss with the appropriate level of management and, where needed, TCWG.Raise with your immediate superior / next level / TCWG.
3 Request actionAdvise/request that they rectify, remediate, disclose & deter recurrence.Recommend they take appropriate action.
4 Assess responseEvaluate whether the response is appropriate & timely.Same.
5 Determine further actionIf unsatisfactory: escalate (higher authority/TCWG); consider the impact on the audit opinion; consider withdrawing; consider disclosure to an authority (if permitted/required); for auditors → assess Reportable Irregularity (APA s45).Consider informing the auditor; consider obtaining legal advice; consider resigning if serious & unresolved.
6 DocumentDocument the matter, discussions, response & conclusions.Document.
Override of confidentialityNOCLAR allows (and sometimes requires) disclosure in the public interest — confidentiality is not a reason to stay silent on serious non-compliance. Always act in good faith.

PART 3 · AUDITING PROFESSION ACT (APA) & IRBA

3.1 · Reportable Irregularity — APA s45 (auditors only — MANDATORY)

A Registered Auditor who, during an audit, is satisfied/has reason to believe a Reportable Irregularity exists must report it to the IRBA. (A CA without an audit appointment uses NOCLAR, not s45.)

3-part definition (ALL: an unlawful act/omission by a person in management that…)
① caused or is likely to cause material financial loss to the entity/its members/creditors/investors; OR ② is fraudulent or amounts to theft; OR ③ represents a material breach of a fiduciary duty owed by management — committed during the audit period.
StepAction & timeline
1Within 3 days of being satisfied → send a written report to IRBA with details.
2Notify the members of management (TCWG) in writing & give a copy of the IRBA report (unless it would prejudice an investigation).
3Take all reasonable measures to discuss with management & allow them to make representations.
4Within 30 days of the first report → send IRBA a second (final) report stating whether the RI is continuing, has been adequately dealt with, or no RI exists, with details.
ExampleDuring the audit you find the CEO authorised a R3m loan to himself without a special resolution (breach of s45) and concealed it → material breach of fiduciary duty / likely loss → Reportable Irregularity → report to IRBA within 3 days, inform TCWG, final report in 30 days. Also a NOCLAR (s360) & possibly a s22 reckless-trading / s76 breach.

3.2 · IRBA Code & registration

  • Registered Auditors must comply with the IRBA Code of Professional Conduct (substantially the IESBA Code, mirrors the SAICA CPC) and IRBA rules; subject to IRBA inspection & disciplinary process.
  • Only a Registered Auditor may perform an audit; must be registered with IRBA and a member of SAICA (for CAs).
PART 4 · COMPANIES ACT 71 OF 2008 — ethics & governance

4.1 · Directors' duties (s75–77)

SectionRequirementTrigger / action
s75 — Personal financial interestA director with a personal financial interest (or a related person's) in a matter must disclose it & its general nature, recuse himself, not vote, and not be counted in the quorum on that matter.Director's family company tenders / director benefits → disclose, leave the meeting, don't vote. Failure → the decision may be invalid & s77 liability.
s76 — Standards of conductAct ① in good faith & for a proper purpose; ② in the best interests of the company; ③ with the degree of care, skill & diligence reasonably expected. Don't use position/information for personal gain or to harm the company.Apply to any director decision; combine with the business-judgment rule below.
s76(4) — Business judgment ruleA director satisfies the duty of care if: ① took reasonable steps to be informed; ② had no material personal financial interest (or disclosed per s75); ③ made/​supported the decision in good faith for a proper purpose with a rational basis.Use to defend/assess whether a director breached the duty of care.
s77 — LiabilityA director is personally liable for loss/damage from a breach of fiduciary duty (s76), the Act, reckless trading, or false/misleading statements.Conclude on personal liability.

4.2 · Other Companies Act ethics provisions

SectionRule & action
s22 — Reckless/insolvent tradingA company must not trade recklessly, with gross negligence, with intent to defraud, or while insolvent. Action: directors must stop; the Commission may issue a notice; s77 liability.
s45 — Financial assistance to directors/related partiesLoans/guarantees to directors/related companies need: a special resolution (within prior 2 years), the board must apply the solvency & liquidity test, and terms must be fair & reasonable. s93 — Auditor rights & dutiesRight of access to records, to attend meetings & be heard. Duty to report RIs (APA s45).
s94 — Audit committeePublic & state-owned companies must have an audit committee of ≥3 independent non-executive directors; functions: nominate the auditor, approve fees, ensure independence, oversee financial reporting, internal controls & the finance function.

PART 6 · KING IV (2016) — corporate governance

6.1 · Philosophy & outcomes

  • Apply and explain (King IV strengthened this from King III's "apply or explain") — you must apply the principles & explain how.
  • Principles-based, outcomes-focused. The 4 governance outcomes: ① ethical culture · ② good performance · ③ effective control · ④ legitimacy.
  • Built on ethical & effective leadership, the organisation as a responsible corporate citizen, sustainable development, and an integrated view (the 6 capitals & triple context).

6.3 · The ethics principles in depth (P1–P3) + actions

PrincipleWhat the board must do
P1 — Ethical leadershipEach member leads ethically & effectively, exemplifying integrity, competence, responsibility, accountability, fairness & transparency; set the "tone at the top".
P2 — Organisational ethicsGovern ethics so it becomes the organisation's culture: approve a code of conduct & ethics policies; manage ethics across the value chain (set standards → institutionalise via codes/training/incentives → monitor (ethics hotlines, dilemmas) → report); delegate to the social & ethics committee.
P3 — Responsible corporate citizenEnsure the company is & is seen to be a responsible citizen — workplace (employment equity, safety), economy (anti-corruption, fair competition), society (CSI), environment (sustainability).

Independence & appropriate skills required.

PART 8 · APPLYING IT IN THE EXAM

8.1 · The ethics-answer framework

  1. Spot the framework — independence/threat (CPC); wrongdoing (NOCLAR / RI / PRECCA); director conduct (Companies Act s75–77); governance (King IV); general (theories/models).
  2. State the rule/principle precisely (name the section/principle/threat).
  3. Apply to the facts — quote the scenario ("the partner's spouse is the financial director → familiarity & self-interest threat").
  4. Assess significance (reasonable & informed third party).
  5. State the action — safeguard, procedure, who reports to whom, by when.
  6. Conclude — is independence impaired / is it an RI / is the director liable?

8.2 · Worked example — independence + NOCLAR combined

ScenarioYour audit firm prepared the client's AFS and tax calculations, earns 40% of its total fees from this client, and during the audit you discover the company has been dumping waste illegally and the CEO authorised an undisclosed loan to himself.
  • Self-review threat (prepared the AFS/tax that you now audit) → prohibited for the AFS of an audit client; use a separate team for tax & ensure management takes responsibility, or don't provide it.
  • Self-interest/intimidation (40% fee dependence) → significant threat → reduce dependence, pre/post-issuance independent review, disclose to TCWG; if it can't be reduced → resign.
  • Illegal dumping = NOCLAR → understand it → discuss with management/TCWG → request they rectify & disclose → assess response → consider opinion impact, withdrawal & disclosure to the authority; consider environmental law.
  • Loan to CEO → breach of s45 (no special resolution) & s75/s76 fiduciary duty → likely a Reportable Irregularity (material breach of fiduciary duty) → report to IRBA in 3 days, inform TCWG, final report in 30 days; possibly PRECCA if ≥R100k fraud.
Final remindersAlways: name the threat/section → apply to the fact → give the safeguard/procedure → state who acts & by when → conclude. Confidentiality never blocks a legal duty to report (RI, PRECCA) or public-interest disclosure (NOCLAR). 🍀
PART 9 · PLANNING THE AUDIT (ISA 300 / 315 / 320)

9.1 · Audit strategy & plan

  • Audit strategy (overall scope, timing, direction) → then the detailed audit plan (nature, timing & extent of risk-assessment, control & substantive procedures).
  • Planning is continuous & iterative — revise as new information arises.

9.2 · Materiality (ISA 320)

TypeWhat it is / benchmark
Overall materialityA benchmark × %: e.g. 5% of profit before tax, ½–2% of revenue or total assets, 1–5% of equity — adjust for the users' needs & the entity's nature.
Performance materialityLower than overall (e.g. 50–75%) to allow for the risk that small misstatements aggregate to a material amount.
Specific materialityA lower figure for sensitive items — directors' remuneration, related-party transactions, fraud-prone areas.
Clearly trivialBelow this, misstatements need not be accumulated.

9.3 · Understanding the entity & risk (ISA 315)

  • Understand the entity, its environment, the reporting framework & the system of internal control → identify Risks of Material Misstatement (RoMM) at the overall financial-statement and assertion levels.
  • Significant risks (need special attention): revenue recognition (presumed fraud risk), complex/judgemental estimates, related-party & non-routine/unusual transactions, going concern.
  • Perform analytical procedures at planning (ratios & trends vs prior year/budget/industry) to spot unusual fluctuations.
RoMM triggerResponse (ISA 330)
Weak control environment / owner-managed override / poor integrityMore senior staff & supervision, heightened professional skepticism, unpredictability, lower materiality, more year-end substantive testing, less reliance on controls & on management representations.
Complex estimates / going concern / new systemUse experts, test assumptions, build independent estimates, increase extent of testing.

PART 11 · TESTS OF CONTROLS (ISA 330 / 265)

11.1 · When & how to test controls

  • Test controls only when you intend to rely on them (and where substantive procedures alone can't give sufficient evidence, e.g. high-volume automated processes).
  • 5 COSO components: control environment · risk assessment · control activities · information & communication · monitoring.
  • Test methods — "I-O-I-R": Inquire (of staff) · Observe the control operating · Inspect evidence (signatures, reconciliations, logs) · Re-perform the control. Test operating effectiveness over the period, not just at a point.

11.2 · Example controls & how to test them (per cycle)

CycleControlTest of control
RevenueCredit sales approved against a credit limit; sales matched to signed delivery notes.Inspect a sample of orders for credit approval; agree invoices to signed PODs & the price list; re-perform the 3-way match.
Purchases3-way match (PO–GRN–invoice); payments authorised.Inspect matched documents & authorisation signatures; re-perform the match; test the approval limits.
PayrollNew employees authorised by HR; changes to the masterfile approved; segregation between HR & payroll.Inspect HR authorisations & masterfile-change logs; observe segregation; re-perform a wage calculation.
InventoryGoods received checked & recorded; periodic counts & reconciliations.Inspect GRNs & count sheets; observe the count process; inspect reconciliations & follow up differences.
BankMonthly bank reconciliations reviewed & signed; dual authorisation of EFTs.Inspect signed reconciliations & re-perform one; inspect EFT release logs for dual authorisation.
Deficiencies (ISA 265)A control missing/not operating = a deficiency; a significant deficiency must be communicated in writing to TCWG. For each deficiency: state the risk it creates, recommend the control, and add a substantive response (you can't rely → do more substantive work).

PART 13 · CAATs — Computer-Assisted Audit Techniques (in depth)

13.1 · What & why

  • CAATs use the computer to perform audit procedures on electronic data — letting you test 100% of a population quickly, accurately & repeatably (vs a manual sample).
  • Two types: (1) Audit software / GAS (e.g. ACL, IDEA) — interrogate & analyse the client's actual data (re-perform, extract exceptions). (2) Test data — input dummy transactions (valid & invalid) into the client's system to test whether the programmed controls work (e.g. a sale over the credit limit should be rejected).

13.2 · Common CAAT procedures + examples

CAAT procedureWhat it does / example
RecalculationRe-perform calculations on the whole file — recalc depreciation per the FAR, interest on loans, invoice totals (qty × price), VAT, ECL allowance.
Exception / exclusion reportsExtract items meeting a condition — debtors over their credit limit, negative balances, balances > materiality, dormant accounts.
Gap & sequence checksFind missing or duplicate document numbers (invoices, cheques) → completeness / fictitious transactions.
Duplicate testingIdentify duplicate payments, duplicate employees/bank accounts (ghost employees), duplicate invoices.
Ageing & re-ageingRe-age the debtors/inventory listing by invoice/receipt date → test the age analysis & valuation.
Stratification & summarisationSplit a population into bands (by value/type) to focus testing; total & sub-total to agree to the GL.
Matching / joining filesMatch the HR masterfile to the payroll (ghost employees); match GRNs to invoices; match the asset register to the GL.
Sampling & selectionSelect a representative or value-weighted sample for further testing.
Benford's Law / digital analysisTest for manipulated/fabricated numbers (unusual digit frequencies).
Cut-off & date analysisList transactions in a window around year-end to test cut-off.

13.3 · CAATs by account (quick application)

AccountCAATs you'd run
ReceivablesRe-age the analysis; recalc invoices & interest; extract over-limit & credit balances; recompute the ECL; gap test on invoice numbers.
InventoryExtract negative/zero/old lines; recalc cost (qty × unit cost); compare cost to NRV field; identify slow-moving by last-movement date.
PPERecalc depreciation & carrying amounts per the register; extract fully-depreciated assets still in use; recompute additions/disposals.
PayrollMatch HR file to payroll (ghosts); duplicate bank accounts/ID numbers; recalc PAYE/net pay; extract pay outside the approved scale.
Payables/BankDuplicate-payment test; gap test on cheque/EFT numbers; recompute supplier balances; extract payments just after year-end (unrecorded liabilities).
Advantages of CAATsLimitations
Test 100% of the population; fast & accurate; consistent & repeatable; can test data & programmed controls; strong for large/automated systems.Set-up cost & expertise needed; depends on data integrity & correct file layout; risk of corrupting live data (use copies); test data may stay in the live system; only as good as the audit logic written.
Worked example — CAATs on receivablesUsing audit software on the debtors file: ① recalc the age analysis by invoice date & agree the total to the control account; ② extract accounts over the credit limit & with credit balances; ③ recompute the ECL using the client's matrix & agree default rates to history; ④ run a gap test on invoice numbers for completeness/fictitious sales. → addresses accuracy, valuation, presentation & completeness in one pass on 100% of the data.
Exam tip for CAATs questionsState ① the CAAT procedure → ② the data/field it runs on → ③ the assertion/risk it addresses. e.g. "Use audit software to recalculate depreciation on the asset-register file and compare to the recorded amount, to verify the accuracy/valuation of PPE."
