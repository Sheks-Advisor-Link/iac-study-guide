IFRS 3 — "What is part of the business combination?"

IFRS 3 paras 51–52 & Application Guidance B50–B62 · summarised & linked to the acquisition method · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden
The whole point (one line): when one company buys another, not every rand that changes hands is payment for the business. IFRS 3 says strip out any "separate transactions" and account for them on their own — only the amount truly exchanged for the acquiree goes into "consideration transferred" (and therefore into goodwill).

1 · The core rule (paras 51–52)

  • The acquirer & acquiree may have had a pre-existing relationship or other arrangement before the deal, or may agree something during negotiations that is really separate from buying the business.
  • The acquirer must identify amounts that are NOT part of the exchange for the acquiree and account for them separately per the relevant IFRS — they are excluded from consideration transferred (so they don't distort goodwill).
Why it mattersIf you wrongly lump a separate transaction into the purchase price, you overstate goodwill and understate expenses/gains in profit or loss. The standard forces the two to be split.

2 · The 3 factors that decide "part of the deal" vs "separate" (B50)

Easy way to remember — "RIT"Reasons · Initiated by whom · Timing. If the answer is "for the acquirer/combined entity, started by the acquirer, agreed as part of the deal" → it's a separate transaction (keep it out of the purchase price).

3 · The separate transactions you must strip out (B51–B62)

(a) Settling a pre-existing relationship (B51–B53)

  • The acquirer and acquiree were already linked (e.g. supplier–customer, a licence, or a lawsuit between them). Buying the company effectively settles that relationship.
  • That settlement is a separate transaction → recognise a gain or loss in profit or loss (not in goodwill).
  • Measure: non-contractual relationship → at fair value; contractual → the lower of (i) the settlement amount and (ii) any "off-market" amount in the contract (favourable/unfavourable). Reacquired rights are measured separately.
ExampleA had a R5m lawsuit pending against the target T. A then buys T. The R5m claim is effectively settled by the purchase → A recognises a R5m settlement gain/loss in P/L and reduces consideration by that amount (so goodwill isn't inflated).

(b) Payments for FUTURE services — employees / selling shareholders (B54–B55)

  • Contingent payments to former owners who stay on as employees may really be remuneration for post-combination services, not part of the price.
  • The killer indicator: if the payment is forfeited when employment ends → it is remuneration → expense over the service period (post-combination), NOT consideration.
  • Other indicators it's remuneration: payment linked to continued employment; duration matches a required employment period; level of pay is otherwise low; only employee-shareholders get it (per share they'd get the same as other owners if it were consideration).
ExampleA buys T for R20m and agrees to pay the founder an extra R3m "earn-out" — but only if she stays employed for 3 years. Forfeited on leaving → it's remuneration: expense R1m/year post-combination, not R3m added to the purchase price/goodwill.

(c) Replacement share-based payment awards (B56–B62)

  • The acquirer replaces the acquiree employees' share options with its own. Split the value: the portion for pre-combination service = part of consideration; the portion for post-combination service = remuneration expense (IFRS 2) over the remaining vesting period.

(d) Acquisition-related costs (para 53)

  • Advisory, legal, valuation, due-diligence fees → expense as incurred (NOT part of consideration). Debt/equity issue costs follow IFRS 9 / IAS 32. Reimbursing the acquirer for these = separate transaction.

4 · How it fits the IFRS 3 acquisition method (the big picture)

IFRS 3 is applied in 4 steps: ① identify the acquirer → ② determine the acquisition date → ③ recognise & measure the identifiable net assets, NCI & goodwill → ④ this section sits inside step ③: make sure "consideration transferred" is clean.

Goodwill = Consideration transferred + NCI + Previously-held interest − Net identifiable assets acquired (at fair value)
The link — what these B50–B62 paragraphs protectThey keep "consideration transferred" pure. Anything that is really a separate transaction is pulled OUT of consideration so it doesn't flow into goodwill:
  • Settle a pre-existing relationship → P/L gain/loss
  • Pay for future services (earn-outs tied to employment) → post-combination expense
  • Replacement awards → split consideration vs IFRS 2 expense
  • Acquisition costs → expense
The result: goodwill reflects only what was paid for the business, and P/L reflects the rest in the right period.

5 · Don't confuse it with contingent CONSIDERATION (para 39–40, 58)

  • Contingent consideration = an extra payment for the business that depends on a future event (e.g. "pay R2m more if profit > R10m") and is NOT tied to employment → it IS part of consideration, measured at fair value at acquisition date.
  • If a "contingent payment" is instead tied to continued employment → it's remuneration (section 3(b)), not consideration. The employment link is the dividing line.

Exam triggersIn a combination scenario, watch for: "the companies were already trading partners / had a dispute" (→ settle pre-existing, P/L); "the seller must stay employed to get the payment" (→ remuneration, not goodwill); "the acquirer paid legal/advisory fees" (→ expense); "extra payment if targets met" (→ test the employment link). Always conclude whether the amount goes into consideration/goodwill or to P/L.
