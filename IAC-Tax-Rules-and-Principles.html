Part 1 · Every tax rule, in simple terms

Gross income (s1)

  • Resident taxed on worldwide income; non-resident only on SA-source.
  • "Amount" = cash or anything with a money value; use it at the date you become entitled.
  • Taxed at the earlier of received or accrued; "received" = for your own benefit; "accrued" = unconditionally entitled.
  • Capital receipts excluded — unless a special inclusion (par a–n) drags them in (annuities, restraint, lump sums, lease premiums).

Exemptions (s10)

  • Local dividends exempt (but 20% dividends tax). Interest exemption R23 800 (<65) / R34 500 (65+).
  • Foreign employment (183/60 days) exempt up to R1.25m. Foreign dividends partly exempt (s10B).

Deductions (s11(a) + s23)

  • Deduct if: trade · expenditure actually incurred · in production of income · not capital (and not blocked by s23).
  • s23H prepaid: only the part used this year (unless ≤R100k or delivered ≤6 months).
  • s11A pre-trade costs ring-fenced to that trade. s11(c) legal (non-capital, trade). s11(d) repairs (not improvements).

Trading stock (s22)

  • Closing stock added (lower of cost/NRV); opening stock deducted.
  • Free/inherited stock in at market value (s22(4)); stock for own use/donated = deemed recovery (s22(8)).

Capital allowances

  • s11(e) wear & tear: straight-line, apportion part-year. s12C plant: 40/20/20/20 new (full year).
  • s13quin/s13sex buildings 5% (10% low-cost homes); bought part of a building → 55% of price = cost.
  • s12B/12BA renewables 100%/125%. Lease premium s11(f) over term; improvements s11(g) over months left.

Recoupments (s8(4))

  • Sell a depreciated asset → recoup allowances = lower(proceeds, cost) − tax value; never more than allowances claimed.
  • Proceeds > cost → excess is a capital gain. Proceeds < tax value → s11(o) scrapping loss.

CGT (8th Schedule)

  • Gain = proceeds − base cost; net gains − annual exclusion (R40k) × inclusion (40% indiv / 80% co).
  • Deemed disposals at market value: donation, death, emigration, dividend in specie, asset to private use, connected persons.
  • Primary residence: first R2m of gain free. Roll-overs: spouse (s9HB), insurance/replacement (par 65/66), death (s9HA).

VAT (15%)

  • Output − input = VAT. Register if 12-month supplies > R1m. Zero-rated (exports, basics, going concern) vs exempt (interest, residential rent, transport, education — no input).
  • Imported goods: customs +10% +duties ×15%. Imported services: VAT on non-taxable-use %. Cars/entertainment = input denied.

Individuals & fringe benefits

  • Travel allowance: deemed cost or actual (logbook); 80% PAYE. Subsistence: deemed daily if ≥1 night away.
  • FB: car 3.5%/3.25% of value; cheap loan = official − charged; asset = MV − paid; accommodation by formula.
  • s11F retirement: 27.5% of higher of pay/TI, cap R350k. Medical credits s6A/s6B.

Employees' & provisional tax

  • PAYE on "remuneration"; independent contractors excluded. Labour brokers/PSPs special rules.
  • Provisional: 1st = 50% est.; 2nd = full est. (within 80% if TI>R1m); basic amount if low.

Lump sums & severance

  • Retirement/retrenchment/severance = retirement table (cumulative); resignation = harsher withdrawal table.
  • Subtract non-deducted contributions first; leftover exempts annuity (s10C).

Dividends, CTC & companies

  • Dividends tax 20% on the owner (company pays for in-specie & deemed dividends); SA-company owners exempt (s64F).
  • CTC = real capital in; returning it isn't a dividend. Company rate 27%; assessed loss s20 capped at higher of R1m/80%.
  • Corporate rules s42 (asset-for-share) / s45 (intra-group ≥70%) give roll-over relief.

Interest & foreign

  • s24J spreads interest on a yield-to-maturity basis (beats cash timing; s23B priority).
  • s24I forex gains/losses on monetary items (realised + unrealised). s6quat foreign tax rebate. s9D CFC.

Estate duty & donations

  • Estate duty: (property + deemed − debts − R3.5m abatement) × 20% (25% >R30m); spouse bequests deductible.
  • Donations tax 20%/25% on MV after R100k free; spouse/PBO/maintenance exempt; s7 attributes diverted income back to donor.

Trusts

  • Conduit (s25B): income keeps its nature and is taxed in the vested beneficiary; else in the trust (45%).
  • Minor/donor-funded income → back to donor (s7). Interest-free loan to trust → s7C deemed donation.

TAA & GAAR

  • Onus on taxpayer (s102); 3-year prescription; object s104; pay-now-argue-later s164; USP s222.
  • GAAR (s80A): arrangement + main purpose tax benefit + a tainted element → SARS re-characterises. Specific anti-avoidance first.

Part 2 · One principle from every worked example

Gross income — illustrative examples

  • Free use of money: an interest-free loan/benefit is GI valued at the interest saved (Brummeria).
  • Payment in kind: value at market value on the date you become entitled, not the contract date.
  • Notional income: what you could have earned is never taxed — you must actually receive/accrue (Lategan/Mooi).
  • Agent money: only your own-benefit slice (commission) is GI; never gross-up then deduct (Geldenhuys).
  • Overpayment: a duplicate receipt isn't GI until you're unconditionally entitled (may be recouped later).
  • Illegal income: still fully taxable.
  • No discounting: include the full face amount once entitled, even if only payable later (Peoples Stores).

Deductions

  • s11A: pre-trade costs only shelter income from that same trade; excess carries forward.
  • s23H: deduct only the used-up portion of a prepayment, unless total prepaid ≤R100k or delivered within 6 months.
  • s11(c): legal fees deductible if non-capital and protecting trade income (e.g. enforcing a loss-of-profits claim) — and the payout is taxable.
  • s11(l)/(m): employer retirement-fund contributions deductible; annuities to retired ex-employees fully deductible.

Trading stock & foreign exchange (Corp Tax)

  • s23F: no deduction for stock bought but not yet sold / income not yet received.
  • s22 cost: stock cost = price + all costs to bring it to its present location & condition.
  • Debt benefit on stock: a waived loan that funded stock reduces the cost of that stock (s19).
  • s22(4): stock acquired for nothing comes in at market value.
  • s22(8): stock taken for private use or distributed as a dividend in specie = deemed recovery at cost/MV.
  • s25D: translate foreign amounts to rand at spot (or average) rate.
  • s24I: monetary items (forex debtors/loans/FECs) are retranslated at year-end — include realised and unrealised differences.
  • s24I(7): forex differences on a capital asset bought pre-production are deferred until the asset is brought into use.

Capital allowances (Corp Tax)

  • s11(e): straight-line and apportioned for the months the asset is used.
  • s12C: 40/20/20/20 on new manufacturing plant — full year, no apportioning; cost includes installation; later moving costs spread over remaining life.
  • s12B/12BA: renewables get 100% (or 125% in the incentive window).
  • s13sex: 5% (10% for low-cost units); if you buy part of a building, use 55% of the price as cost.
  • s12N: a lessee who improves (government) land is deemed the owner and claims the building allowance.
  • IP s11(gC): patents/designs get an allowance over their life — trademarks get nothing.

Capital gains tax (CGT lecture examples)

  • CGT-1 residence: residents taxed on worldwide gains; non-residents only on SA immovable property & PE assets.
  • CGT-2 intention: a personal asset sold with no profit-scheme is capital (and often excluded).
  • CGT-3/4 mechanics: the annual exclusion reduces both gains and losses before the inclusion rate.
  • CGT-5 emigration (s9H): ceasing to be resident triggers a deemed disposal at MV — except SA immovable property & cash.
  • CGT-6 immigration: becoming a resident resets base cost to market value.
  • CGT-7 base cost: include acquisition cost, transfer duty, improvements, advertising & agent's commission — exclude repairs.
  • CGT-8 allowance assets: remove the recoupment from proceeds and reduce base cost by allowances — recoupment can never exceed allowances claimed.
  • CGT-9: donations tax paid is added (proportionally) to base cost.
  • CGT-10 proceeds: reduce proceeds by any amount already taxed in gross income (no double tax).
  • CGT-12/13 primary residence: first R2m of gain free; apportion for >2ha land or business/letting use.
  • CGT-14 personal use: moving an asset to private use is a deemed disposal at MV; personal-use losses are disregarded (par 53).
  • CGT-15 par 65: involuntary disposal (insurance) qualifies for roll-over; insurance proceeds limited to original cost.
  • CGT-16 connected persons: transactions are deemed to happen at market value.
  • CGT-17 death (s9HA): deemed disposal at MV at death — except assets to a spouse (s9HB roll-over at base cost).
  • CGT-18 s9C: equity shares held ≥3 years are deemed capital — even for a share dealer.
  • CGT-19: a dividend in specie is a deemed disposal at market value.
  • CGT-20 par 76B: a return of capital reduces base cost; once base cost hits nil the excess is an immediate gain.
  • CGT-21–23 par 12A: a waived debt that funded an asset reduces its base cost (and caps future allowances).

VAT (worked examples)

  • Registration: compulsory once 12-month taxable supplies exceed R1m — add all branches together (s50).
  • Import goods: VAT base = customs value + 10% + duties, then ×15%.
  • Import services: self-account VAT only on the non-taxable (exempt) portion of use.
  • Exports: zero-rated only if you hold export proof within 90 days, otherwise standard-rated.
  • Going concern: zero-rated when the criteria are met and both parties are vendors.
  • Fixed property (Pine Jel): output/input accounted on a payment basis (s9(3)(d)/s16(4)).
  • Credit waiver (Pine Jel): a loan is an exempt financial service — VAT only reverses (s22) if the creditor who waived also charged the original VAT.

Individuals & fringe benefits (Worked Examples 2024)

  • Assessed loss s20A: losses from "suspect" hobby-type trades are ring-fenced (3-of-5-year / listed-trade tests).
  • Travel allowance: reduce by business km using deemed cost tables or actual costs — logbook essential; 80% goes through PAYE.
  • Subsistence: deemed daily amounts apply only if away ≥1 night.
  • Car FB: 3.5% (3.25% with maintenance plan) of determined value per month, reduced for business use & employee-borne costs.
  • Loan FB: benefit = (official rate − rate charged) × loan × period.
  • VAT on FB: the employer accounts output VAT (tax fraction) on the fringe benefit granted.
  • s8C shares: the gain on a restricted equity instrument is taxed as remuneration on vesting, not on grant.

Employees' & provisional tax (Worked Examples 2024)

  • Independent trade: not an "employee" if free of control/supervision (statutory exclusion if own premises & ≥3 staff).
  • Labour broker: PAYE withheld unless a valid exemption certificate is held.
  • Personal service provider: taxed at company rate with very limited deductions; PAYE applies.
  • Basic amount: if the last assessment is >18 months old, escalate it 8% per year.
  • Provisional 2nd payment: if TI > R1m the estimate must be within 80% of actual or a par-20 penalty applies.

Retirement (worked examples)

  • s11F: deduct up to 27.5% of the higher of pay/taxable income, capped at R350k; excess carries forward.
  • Severance: qualifies for the retirement table only if ≥55, ill-health, or retrenchment.
  • Withdrawal: amounts transferred between funds are tax-free; only the cash taken is taxed (harsher table).
  • Cumulative: tax all lump sums to date, subtract tax on earlier ones = tax on the current one.
  • s10C: contributions never deducted come back to exempt your annuity income.

Dividends, CTC & corporate rules

  • CTC: a capitalisation issue out of profits is tainted (not CTC); returning genuine CTC is not a dividend.
  • Dividend in specie / cheap loan: the company pays the 20% (deemed-dividend on a low-interest shareholder loan = official rate × loan).
  • s64N: a rebate for foreign dividend tax already withheld caps total tax at 20%.
  • Liquidation: split the distribution into CTC (capital) and dividend (20% DWT); company shareholders are exempt (s64F).
  • s42: asset-for-share gives roll-over relief when the requirements are met.
  • s24BA: an asset-for-share at a value mismatch triggers a company capital gain / reduced share base cost.
  • s103: changing shareholding solely to use an assessed loss is disallowed (anti-avoidance).

Donations, estate duty & attribution

  • Donations tax: 20% on market value after the R100k annual exemption; spouse/PBO/maintenance/6-month-return are exempt.
  • s7 attribution: income diverted via donation is taxed back in the donor — minor child s7(3), spouse s7(2), non-resident s7(8).
  • Estate duty: (property + deemed property − debts/admin − R3.5m abatement) × 20%; spouse bequests fully deductible.
  • Estate valuation: use death-date market value (or the actual arm's-length sale price); ignore later damage or below-value will conditions.

Trusts

  • Conduit (s25B): income keeps its nature flowing out and is taxed in the vested beneficiary; retained, un-donated income is taxed in the trust at 45%.
  • Attribution: income to a minor or from a donor-funded asset is taxed in the donor — but income from a different donor's asset is not.
  • s25B(3): a beneficiary's deduction is capped at their vested income — losses stay trapped in the trust.
  • s7C: an interest-free/low-interest loan to a trust creates a deemed annual donation (the foregone interest).

International & withholding (s35A / s50)

  • s35A: a buyer of SA property from a non-resident withholds 7.5% (individual) / 10% (company) / 15% (trust) as an advance of the seller's tax.
  • s50B interest WHT: 15% on SA-source interest paid to a non-resident — but bank & listed-bond interest is exempt (s50D).
  • s6quat: a rebate for foreign taxes paid, limited to the SA tax on that foreign income.
  • s9D / s10B: CFC income is imputed to >50% resident holders; foreign dividends get participation/ratio exemption.
