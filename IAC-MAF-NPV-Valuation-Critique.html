Critically Analysing NPV & Valuations

The correct calculation base · the rules · what to look for & compare to · SAICA IAC 2026 · T. Van Heerden
The skill being tested (e.g. June 2025 "criticise the FCF valuation"): you're not re-doing the maths — you're judging whether the inputs, method & assumptions are reasonable. Attack three things: ① the cash flows (right items in/out?), ② the discount rate (right rate for the risk?), ③ the assumptions (growth, period, terminal value, bias) — and always say what you'd compare each to.

The relevant-cash-flow rules (what's IN / OUT)Include: incremental, future, after-tax operating cash flows · the tax shield on capital allowances · working-capital investment & its release · opportunity costs · residual value. Exclude: sunk costs · allocated/apportioned head-office fixed costs (non-incremental) · depreciation (non-cash — but DO use its tax effect) · financing cash flows (interest, dividends, loan repayments — already in the discount rate) · past R&D.
Two consistency rulesInflation: discount real cash flows at the real rate, or nominal/money cash flows at the money rate (Fisher: 1+money = (1+real)(1+inflation)) — never mix. Discount rate: use the WACC for an average-risk project financed in the existing structure; risk-adjust (pure-play beta) for different risk; use APV if financing side-effects are significant.

2 · How to CRITICISE an NPV — the checklist

AttackWhat to look for (the error)Compare to / fix
Discount rateCompany WACC used for a different-risk project; book (not market) weights; rate not risk-adjusted; capital structure changing; real vs nominal mismatch with the cash flows.Compare to a risk-adjusted/pure-play rate & market data; check consistency with inflation basis.
Sunk & allocated costsSunk costs (e.g. past research, a feasibility study) wrongly included; allocated head-office overhead included though it won't change.Strip them out — only incremental costs are relevant.
Depreciation & financingDepreciation deducted as a cash flow (it's non-cash); interest/dividends/loan repayments in the cash flows (double counts — they're in the rate).Remove; include only the tax shield on allowances.
Working capitalWC investment omitted; or not released at the end; growth in WC for rising sales ignored.Add the WC outflow & the terminal release.
TaxTax ignored or at the wrong rate; timing/lag ignored; capital allowances/recoupments omitted.Apply the correct rate & timing; include the allowance tax shield & recoupment.
Forecast periodPeriod too short (cuts off value) or too long (unreliable far-out forecasts).Use the asset/project life; add a terminal value if it continues.
Growth & terminal valueOptimistic/unsupported growth; terminal value dominates the NPV; growth ≥ discount rate; no terminal value where the project continues.Compare growth to GDP/industry/history; sensitivity on g.
Assumptions & biasOptimism (management/bonus-linked); inconsistent assumptions; unreliable source data; no sensitivity/scenario analysis.Back-test prior forecasts vs actual; run sensitivity; corroborate data.
QualitativeIgnores real options (expand/delay/abandon), strategic fit, ESG, competitor response.Note flexibility & non-financial value beyond the NPV number.
What to compare the NPV to (the verdict)① NPV vs zero (positive = adds value); ② IRR vs the cost of capital; ③ sensitivity — how far an input can move before NPV = 0 (the critical variable); ④ scenario (best/base/worst); ⑤ payback (liquidity/risk screen); ⑥ assumptions vs industry data, history & prior-forecast accuracy. Conclude: "the NPV is positive but highly sensitive to [X] / relies on optimistic [Y] → accept cautiously / recommend further work."

3 · The valuation methods — correct base & how to critique each

3.1 DCF (the main one)

FCFF = EBIT × (1 − tax) + depreciation − capex − increase in working capital
Discount FCFF at the WACC → Enterprise value; − net debt = equity value
FCFE = FCFF − after-tax interest + net new borrowing; discount at Ke → equity value directly
Terminal value = final FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g); discount back to present
  • Critique & compare: match the cash flow to the rate (FCFF↔WACC, FCFE↔Ke — a classic error); is the growth rate ≥ long-run GDP (unrealistic)? does the terminal value make up most of the value (over-reliant)? is the forecast period & the rate reasonable? are the FCFs normalised (strip one-offs)? Compare to a multiples valuation & to recent transactions; do a range/sensitivity.

3.2 Relative valuation (multiples)

Equity value = earnings × P/E · or EV = EBITDA × EV/EBITDA (then − net debt) · or NAV × P/B
  • Critique & compare: are the comparable companies truly comparable (size, risk, growth, sector, gearing)? adjust for accounting-policy differences; is the metric (earnings/EBITDA) distorted by one-offs or cyclicality? a listed-company multiple includes a marketability premium an unlisted target lacks. Compare to DCF & to actual sector deals.

3.3 Net asset value & dividend growth

NAV = fair value of assets − liabilities (a floor/minimum value)
Dividend growth (Gordon): P0 = D1 ÷ (Ke − g)
  • NAV critique: ignores internally-generated goodwill & future earnings; uses book not fair values; suits asset-heavy/liquidation, not a going concern.
  • DDM critique: extremely sensitive to g; needs Ke > g; assumes constant growth (unrealistic); only for dividend-paying minority stakes.
Valuation adjustments to checkAdd a control premium for a controlling stake; apply a minority discount and a marketability/liquidity discount (unlisted); add surplus/non-operating assets; add buyer-specific synergies; deduct contingent liabilities. A range (not a single number) is more credible.

4 · The rules & what to compare to — quick recap

  • NPV rule: accept if NPV > 0 (positive = value added). IRR rule: accept if IRR > cost of capital (watch multiple IRRs & ranking conflicts vs NPV — NPV wins).
  • Match the rate to the flow: all-firm flows (FCFF) → WACC; equity flows (FCFE/dividends) → Ke; real → real, nominal → nominal.
  • Only relevant cash flows: incremental, future, after-tax; include opportunity cost & working capital; exclude sunk, allocated, depreciation (non-cash) & financing.
  • Always compare to: zero / cost of capital · sensitivity & scenario · another method (DCF vs multiples vs NAV) · industry data, history & prior-forecast accuracy · recent comparable transactions.
  • Conclude with a recommendation that weighs the financial result and the reliability of the assumptions and the qualitative/strategic factors.
Exam phrasing that scores"The discount rate does not reflect the project's higher risk — a risk-adjusted rate should be used." · "Sunk research costs of Rx have been incorrectly included." · "Depreciation is non-cash and should be excluded; only its tax shield is relevant." · "The terminal value represents x% of the valuation, so it is highly sensitive to the growth & discount-rate assumptions." · "FCFF was discounted at Ke instead of WACC." · "The result should be tested with sensitivity analysis and compared to a multiples valuation & recent transactions."
