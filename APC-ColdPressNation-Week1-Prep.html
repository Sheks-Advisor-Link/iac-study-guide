3 · Trigger-based research

Trigger 1 — Acquisition of Onverwacht farm

  • They bought the net assets and operations of the farm (not shares). First question: is it a business or just a group of assets? It has inputs (land, trees, labour), a process (farming) and outputs (apples) → I think it's a business combination (IFRS 3). I'd still do the IFRS 3 "concentration test" to double-check it isn't just a single asset.
  • If it's a business → apply the acquisition method: identify the acquirer (CPN), the acquisition date (1 Aug 2022), recognise identifiable assets & liabilities at fair value, and any goodwill = consideration − net identifiable assets. If it's not a business → asset acquisition (no goodwill, spread the cost).
  • The farm has never done IFRS (all at historic cost) → I'll need fair values at acquisition date for the land, the trees and the growing apples (links to Trigger 5).
  • Audit angle (first time the farm is in the group): ISA 510 opening balances, ISA 315 understand the new business & risks, and probably a valuation expert for the land/trees/fruit (ISA 500 / ISA 620 — competence, objectivity, work).

Trigger 2 — R12m loan from the parent (MegaDrinks)

  • Accounting (existing loan): IFRS 9 — recognise initially at fair value. Because it's from the parent (related party) the rate might not be market-related. If it's below market there's a day-1 difference that is likely a capital contribution from the parent (equity) rather than income. After that, measure at amortised cost using the effective interest rate. Need the actual loan terms to do this.
  • Borrowing costs: IAS 23 — only capitalise if the loan funded a qualifying asset. The farm/juicing assets probably aren't qualifying assets (don't take a long time to get ready), so I think interest is expensed — confirm.
  • Tax (this is a big one because MegaDrinks is offshore):
    • Interest deductible for CPN under s11(a) / s24J if in production of income.
    • s31 transfer pricing — connected non-resident, so the interest rate must be arm's length. Also s23M (limitation on interest paid to a creditor not subject to SA tax) and thin-capitalisation.
    • Withholding tax on interest (s50A–H, 15%) on interest paid to the non-resident parent, possibly reduced by a double tax agreement. First interest payment is 31 July 2023.
  • Restructuring of the loan: need the details, but it will be one of — a modification (IFRS 9 — do the "10% test", plus qualitative factors) or a derecognition/extinguishment, or possibly a conversion to equity (debt-to-equity swap). Strategic: lower gearing, better ratios, ease cash flow for the group.
  • "Unintended" tax on restructuring (what Amien asked me to watch for): if any debt is reduced/waived → s19 & para 12A (recoupment / reduce base cost). If converted to shares → possible dividends tax / deemed dividend issues. I need to flag these so we structure it to avoid them.

Trigger 3 — Production process & farm integration

  • Website still shows the process before Onverwacht was integrated (and no organic references) → the supply chain is now vertically integrated.
  • Apples grown on our own farm now move internally from farm to factory — that's not a sale, so no revenue; it transfers at cost/FVLCS into the juicing process (links to inventory).
  • Risks & controls to think about: quality control of own fruit, the cold chain, recording internal transfers correctly, organic-compliance claims (are they substantiated?), and controls over the new farm's records feeding into group reporting.

Trigger 4 — Marketing strategy (Nate's 5 ChatGPT ideas)

  • I should evaluate each of the 5 ideas (online ads, influencers, customer experience, product diversification, strategic partnerships) against a limited budget and our premium/organic brand — pros, cons, cost, how measurable, and fit.
  • Tax: marketing spend is generally deductible under s11(a) (in the production of income, revenue in nature). I'd watch for anything that creates an enduring benefit (could be capital).
  • Ethics: Nate got these straight from ChatGPT. As a CA I need professional scepticism — AI output isn't authoritative, can be wrong/generic, and I shouldn't put company/confidential info into it. I should apply my own judgement, not just accept it.

Trigger 5 — Agricultural assets (trees, land, growing & harvested apples)

This is the one Amien specifically asked me to write a memo on ("what standard is that again?"). He also said not to go into IFRS 13 detail. So I think this is a likely task.
  • The apple trees = bearer plants → accounted for under IAS 16 (PPE), not IAS 41. They're held to produce apples over many periods, so cost (or revaluation) model and depreciated over their fruiting life (>20 years).
  • The growing apples (still on the tree) = biological assets → IAS 41, measured at fair value less costs to sell at each reporting date, with FV changes going to profit or loss.
  • At harvest, the picked apples = agricultural produce → measured at FVLCS at the point of harvest; that value becomes the "cost" going into inventory (IAS 2) from then on.
  • The orchard land = IAS 16 PPE (owner-occupied), cost or revaluation model.
  • Operating costs (electricity, labour, fertiliser, pesticides, maintenance) → expensed as incurred; under the FV model you don't capitalise these into the biological asset.
  • Value-creation "picture" Amien wants: as the trees mature the yield rises; the growing apples increase in fair value → FV gains recognised in P/L before any sale; at harvest we get produce at FVLCS; then it's juiced/sold. So value shows up progressively through FV changes, not only when we sell.

Trigger 6 — Inventory

  • IAS 2: cost = purchase price + conversion costs (labour + production overheads) + costs to bring to location/condition. Fixed overheads are allocated based on normal capacity — so under-used capacity means some fixed overhead is expensed, not put into inventory (this links straight to Trigger 7).
  • Fruit comes from three sources: bought from neighbours, our own farm (comes in at FVLCS from IAS 41), and imported out-of-season fruit → need consistent costing.
  • Lower of cost and NRV; batch process and flash-freezing.
  • Amien said historical costing "wasn't that bad" but needs improvement → if the old numbers were actually wrong, that's a prior-period error under IAS 8 (retrospective restatement), not just a change going forward.
  • Audit of inventory: existence (attend the count), valuation (recompute the costing/overhead allocation), cut-off, and IAS 2 disclosure.

Trigger 7 — Unused (spare) capacity: two new product options

  • Nate wants me to work out the effect on the contribution margin % and operating profit margin % for the company as a whole for two new-product options (current CM 34%, OP margin 3.5%).
  • This is a CVP / relevant-costing question. Because there's spare capacity, the fixed costs are already covered, so I'd use incremental/relevant costing — only the extra variable/incremental costs and the extra contribution from the new products matter.
  • I'll need to split costs into fixed vs variable, look at it per product line and for the whole company, and remember that adding volume that uses spare capacity should also improve fixed-overhead absorption (links back to inventory costing).
  • Strategic: does the new product fit the premium/organic brand, will it cannibalise existing sales, does it genuinely use the off-peak spare capacity, and is it worth the effort for the margin gained.
