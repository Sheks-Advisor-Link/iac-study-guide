PART A — PERFORMANCE EVALUATION

A1 · Financial ratio analysis (the core toolkit)

CategoryRatio & formulaWhat it tells you / how to critique
ProfitabilityGross / operating / net margin = profit ÷ revenue; ROE = profit ÷ equity; ROA / ROCE = operating profit ÷ (capital employed); EBITDATrend & vs peers. Strip out one-offs (e.g. profit on disposals, impairments, tax credits) → quality of earnings. Margin fall = price pressure or cost creep. Watch operating gearing (sticky fixed costs).
LiquidityCurrent = CA ÷ CL; Quick = (CA − inventory) ÷ CL; Cash = cash ÷ CLShort-term survival. A collapse is often driven by a reclassification of debt to current (IAS 1) → really a refinancing/going-concern signal, not trading.
Solvency / gearingD:E = debt ÷ equity; Total liab ÷ equity; Equity ratio = equity ÷ assets; Net debt = debt − cash; Interest cover = operating profit ÷ finance costFinancial risk. Rising gearing + falling earnings = distress. No interest cover when there is an operating loss. Links to covenants & refinancing.
EfficiencyReceivables/inventory/payables days; asset turnover = revenue ÷ assets; cash conversion cycleWorking-capital management. Improving "days" can be an artefact of lower revenue or forced actions (paying suppliers faster drains cash).
Investor / marketEPS (IAS 33); P/E; dividend yield & cover; net asset value per shareMarket view & returns to shareholders. Adjust for dilution & rights issues (see Part D).
How to write a performance-evaluation discussion (exam technique): for each ratio — (1) state the number and the direction (vs prior year / budget / peer), (2) give the reason from the scenario, (3) say what it means for the business, and (4) add qualitative factors and a "compare to what" (prior year, budget, competitor e.g. De Beers/Petra, industry). Always separate underlying performance from one-offs.

A2 · Non-financial measures & the Balanced Scorecard

PerspectiveExample measures
FinancialMargins, ROCE, free cash flow, cost per unit, EVA
Customer / marketMarket share, customer satisfaction/retention, realised price vs index
Internal processYield/recovery, throughput, quality/defect rates, safety incidents
Learning & growth (incl. ESG)Staff turnover, training, emissions, rehabilitation, innovation
  • A balanced set mixes financial + non-financial and leading (predictive) + lagging (outcome) indicators, and links to strategy.
  • Relying on financial measures alone drives short-termism and can be gamed — pair with operational/ESG measures.

A3 · Divisional performance measures

MeasureFormulaWatch-outs
Return on investment (ROI)Divisional profit ÷ divisional assetsCan cause dysfunctional decisions — a manager rejects a project above the group's cost of capital because it dilutes their high ROI.
Residual income (RI)Profit − (imputed interest × assets)Fixes ROI's flaw (accepts any project earning above the charge) but is absolute, so hard to compare divisions of different size.
EVANOPAT − (WACC × capital)Focuses on value creation; needs accounting adjustments; sensitive to WACC.
  • Controllability principle: only hold a manager to account for what they can control (e.g. cost per unit, not the diamond price or exchange rate). Uncontrollable items (market price, impairments) should be excluded or benchmarked out.
  • Benchmarking: internal (vs budget/prior year), competitor (De Beers, Petra), and industry — strip out cycle effects with relative/market-adjusted targets.

A4 · Variance analysis — discussion points

VarianceWhat a variance signals · what to look at
Sales price / volume / mixPrice = market/discounting; volume = demand/capacity; mix = selling more of high- vs low-margin lines. Link mix to strategy (value-led = better mix).
Material price / usagePrice = supplier/FX/commodity; usage = efficiency, waste, quality of input. Interdependence: cheap poor-quality input → adverse usage.
Labour rate / efficiencyRate = wage rates/overtime; efficiency = productivity, skills, downtime. Cutting rates can hurt efficiency & safety.
Overhead exp / volume / capacityFixed-overhead volume variance = under/over-absorption vs normal capacity (links to IAS 2 inventory costing).
  • Always discuss interdependencies (one favourable variance causing an adverse one) and controllability (who is responsible), and whether the standard/budget itself was realistic.
PART B — INCENTIVE SCHEMES

B1 · Purpose & good-design principles

  • Purpose: align management with shareholders, motivate, retain key people, and drive the strategy. Long-term (share-based, multi-year vesting) schemes discourage short-termism.
Design principleMeaning / what to check
ControllableReward only what management can influence — not the diamond price, FX, geology or an external award.
Achievable / realisticTargets must be reachable (e.g. "grow profit 5%" is meaningless on a loss). Unrealistic targets demotivate or invite manipulation.
Measurable & objectiveClear definitions and independently verifiable data (avoid subjective terms like "high-value" without a threshold).
Balanced & tieredMix of financial + non-financial, leading + lagging; tier targets by level of responsibility (exec = group; mine manager = own mine).
Not manipulableGuard against "hitting the target, missing the point" — e.g. dumping inventory, sterilising ore, asset sales, deferring maintenance.
Aligned to strategy & survivalReward what actually matters now (cash, going concern, value-led production, safety), not vanity metrics.

B2 · Types of incentive & IFRS 2 accounting

TypeIFRS 2 classification & measurement
Cash bonusEmployee benefit (IAS 19) — expense as earned.
Shares / share options (equity-settled)Measure at grant-date fair value of the equity granted; do NOT remeasure. Expense over the vesting period. Adjust the number expected to vest for service & non-market performance conditions; market conditions are built into grant-date FV and not trued-up.
Cash-settled (e.g. SARs)Recognise a liability, remeasured to fair value at each reporting date and at settlement, with changes in P/L. (Diamante-style: number of shares fixed at vesting on share price then → careful whether equity- or cash-settled in substance.)
Vesting conditions: (1) Service — stay employed; (2) Non-market performance — e.g. profit/EPS target → affects the number that vest (true-up); (3) Market — e.g. share-price/TSR target → built into grant-date FV, no true-up even if not met. Journal (equity-settled): Dr employee expense / Cr equity (SBP reserve), spread over the vesting period.

B3 · Tax of incentive schemes (SA)

  • Deductibility (employer): remuneration/incentives deductible under s11(a) (in the production of income, not capital) — timing when the obligation is unconditional.
  • Variable remuneration — s7B: bonuses/commission accrue for tax when paid (not when accrued), so the employer's deduction and the employee's inclusion align on payment.
  • Employee share schemes — s8C: gains on "restricted equity instruments" are taxed as income in the employee's hands when restrictions lift (vesting); the employer deduction interacts with s11(lA).
  • SARs / phantom shares: cash paid = remuneration → deductible s11(a); taxed in the employee's hands as remuneration (PAYE).
  • Employees' tax (PAYE) — the control test: if the company controls the manner/means/premises of the work (e.g. content from our premises, pre-approval), the person is an employee → withhold PAYE; a genuinely independent contractor → no PAYE.

B4 · Governance & ethics of remuneration (King IV)

  • King IV Principle 14: remuneration should be fair, responsible and transparent; a remuneration committee of independent non-execs sets policy; disclose the policy & implementation report; put to a shareholder (say-on-pay) vote.
  • Malus & clawback: withhold/recover awards on restatement, misconduct, safety incident or covenant breach — appropriate when a company is fragile.
  • Ethical red flags: "reward for failure" (paying management while loss-making / bonuses paused for staff), manipulation risk, no safety/ESG measures, fairness while asking shareholders for a rights issue.
PART C — DEBT vs EQUITY (classification, restructuring, tax)

C1 · IAS 32 — liability vs equity

  • Liability if there is a contractual obligation to deliver cash (or another financial asset), or to deliver a variable number of own shares.
  • Equity if there is no such obligation and (for derivatives) it is settled fixed-for-fixed (a fixed number of own shares for a fixed amount of cash).
  • Substance over form: "shares" that carry a mandatory redemption or fixed dividend can be a liability; a "loan" convertible into a fixed number of shares has an equity component.

C2 · Compound instruments & IFRS 9 measurement

ItemTreatment
Convertible loan (compound)Split: liability = FV of a similar loan without the conversion feature; equity = residual (balancing figure). Interest expense uses the liability-component rate, not the coupon.
Initial recognitionAt fair value. A related-party / off-market loan (e.g. below-market from a parent) has a day-1 difference — often a capital contribution to equity, or a day-1 gain/loss.
SubsequentAmortised cost using the effective interest rate (unless FVTPL).

C3 · Debt restructuring — modification vs extinguishment

The 10% test (IFRS 9): compare the PV of the new cash flows discounted at the original effective rate to the carrying amount of the old loan. ≥10% different (or qualitatively substantial) → extinguishment: derecognise the old loan, recognise the new one at FV, gain/loss to P/L. <10% → modification: remeasure to the PV of new flows at the original EIR, difference to P/L, keep using the original EIR.
  • Debt-for-equity swap (IFRIC 19): equity issued to extinguish debt is measured at the FV of the equity (or of the debt if more reliable); difference to P/L. (May not apply between parent & sub under common control.)

C4 · Tax of debt (SA Income Tax Act)

ProvisionEffect
s24JInterest deductible on a yield-to-maturity basis if incurred in the production of income (in a trade).
s23MLimits the interest deduction where interest is paid to a connected creditor not subject to SA tax (e.g. a foreign parent).
s31 (transfer pricing)Cross-border, connected persons must charge an arm's-length rate; a disallowed excess is a deemed dividend in specie → dividends tax.
s50A–H (WHT)15% withholding tax on interest paid to a non-resident (final tax; may be reduced by a DTA). Withheld & paid by the SA payer.
s8F / s8FAHybrid debt (e.g. convertible-to-shares or profit-linked) — interest re-characterised as a dividend in specie, non-deductible.
s19 & para 12ADebt benefit (debt reduced/waived/converted to shares of lesser value) triggers recoupments / reduced base cost / capital-allowance reversals. No relief between a s41 group unless all parties are SA-resident.

PART D — RIGHTS ISSUES

D1 · What & why

  • A rights issue offers new shares to existing shareholders pro-rata, usually at a discount to market, so they can maintain their proportionate holding.
  • Reasons: raise equity to deleverage, fund investment, or support going concern / a refinancing (e.g. a bank extension contingent on a rights issue). Cheaper than a public issue; rewards loyal shareholders; avoids diluting control to outsiders.

D2 · Accounting & EPS (IAS 33)

  • Proceeds increase share capital & share premium (equity); share-issue costs are debited to equity.
  • Because a rights issue is at a discount it contains a bonus element. For EPS (IAS 33), adjust the weighted-average number of shares (and restate prior-year EPS) using a rights-issue adjustment factor = market price before ÷ theoretical ex-rights price (TERP).
TERP & value of a right. TERP = [(shares before × cum-rights price) + (new shares × issue price)] ÷ total shares after. Value of one right ≈ (cum-rights price − issue price) ÷ (number of rights needed for one new share + 1), or simply (cum-rights price − TERP). Rights can be renounceable (sold on) or not.

D3 · Strategic & other considerations

  • Dilution: shareholders who don't take up their rights are diluted (holding %, EPS). Watch control shifts (e.g. one shareholder underwriting).
  • Approval & success risk: needs a shareholder resolution; may be underwritten to guarantee the funds; a discount must be deep enough to be taken up but not so deep it signals distress.
  • Going concern & ethics: raising equity from shareholders while rewarding management in dilutive shares (see Part B) is a fairness/optics issue.
  • Tax: issuing shares is generally not a taxable event for the company; for shareholders, cost of the new shares forms part of CGT base cost.
PART E — ESG & SUSTAINABILITY

E1 · What ESG is & why it matters

  • Environmental (emissions, water, waste/tailings, rehabilitation, biodiversity), Social (safety, communities, labour, human rights), Governance (board, ethics, remuneration, transparency).
  • ESG affects value creation, risk, cost of capital, licence to operate and reputation — increasingly a strategic and reporting issue, not a "nice to have".

E2 · Integrated reporting & King IV

  • Integrated thinking / the six capitals (financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social & relationship, natural): decisions trade off and connect these capitals over time.
  • King IV: P1 ethical & effective leadership; P3 responsible corporate citizen; P4 strategy & performance in the triple context; P5 reports build trust (integrated report); a Social & Ethics Committee (also Companies Act Reg 43). "Apply and explain."
  • Greenwashing risk: a good report rating ≠ real ESG performance — reward substance (actual emissions/rehabilitation/safety), not disclosure alone.

E3 · Climate risk & emissions

Climate risk typeExamples (mining)
PhysicalStorms/floods threatening shaft safety & tailings stability; water scarcity.
TransitionCarbon taxes, energy transition, changing regulation & investor expectations.
LiabilityLitigation/fines for environmental damage or misleading disclosure.
  • GHG Scopes: Scope 1 (direct), Scope 2 (purchased energy), Scope 3 (value chain). Net-zero roadmaps set reduction targets (e.g. 35–40% cut in Scope 1&2 by 2030).
  • Tools: climate scenario analysis, TCFD-aligned disclosure, insurance, and a mitigation/adaptation strategy.

E4 · ESG in the financials & decisions

  • Rehabilitation / decommissioning provisions (IAS 37): present obligation to restore a site → provision at the PV of expected costs, unwound as a finance cost; changes via IFRIC 1.
  • Impairment & stranded assets (IAS 36): transition/regulation can shorten mine lives and trigger impairments.
  • Capital allocation: weigh ESG/long-term value alongside NPV — some value-accretive projects are declined on ESG or social-licence grounds, and vice versa.
  • Reporting standards: IFRS S1/S2 (ISSB) sustainability & climate disclosure, GRI, JSE sustainability & climate disclosure guidance.

E5 · ESG & incentives / exam technique

  • Link ESG to strategy, risk and value creation and to stakeholders, not just compliance.
  • ESG KPIs in incentive schemes should measure real outcomes (TRIFR safety rate, tailings/rehabilitation compliance, emissions vs roadmap), tiered and controllable — not a single external award.
  • In a discussion: identify the ESG issue → the risk/opportunity → the impact on value/stakeholders → the governance response (King IV, committee, disclosure).
